Mardi Himal Trek Overview

Our eight-day Mardi Himal Trek follows forest trails from Kande to the ridge beneath Machhapuchhre. You walk through Pitam Deurali and Rest Camp before reaching High Camp, the highest overnight stop. The following morning allows an upper viewpoint or higher base camp hike, depending on the agreed plan, before descending through Badal Danda and Siding.

After arriving in Kathmandu, you fly to Pokhara on Day 2 and continue by road to Kande. The walking route leads through Australian Camp and Pothana to Pitam Deurali, where you spend the first trekking night. The next day continues through rhododendron and oak forest, passing Forest Camp before reaching Rest Camp. Paths climb through wooded hills, with uneven ground and occasional openings towards the surrounding mountain slopes.

From Rest Camp, the route climbs through Low Camp towards High Camp. Trees become fewer as you gain height, and clear weather opens views of Machhapuchhre, Annapurna South and Hiunchuli. This stage brings a substantial increase in sleeping altitude. Walk steadily, take regular breaks and discuss how you feel with your guide throughout the ascent.

The upper hike can stop at the viewpoint, around 4,200 m, or continue towards the higher base camp, around 4,500 m, when agreed and suitable. The exposed trail requires careful footing and favourable conditions. Afterwards, descend through High Camp to Badal Danda. The final walking day follows forest paths to Siding, before the road transfer to Pokhara for the night.

Five walking days and seven nights complete the programme. You fly back to Kathmandu on Day 7 for a farewell dinner and hotel stay before Day 8 departure.

Trek Difficulty and Fitness

The trek is moderate to challenging, with five walking days involving sustained climbs, uneven paths and long descents. Rest Camp to High Camp gains substantial altitude, while the upper hike follows exposed mountain ground. Prepare for repeated uphill and downhill walking with a daypack before departure.

Previous hiking experience is helpful. First-time trekkers should build endurance before departure and become comfortable walking uphill and downhill regularly with a daypack.

Begin preparing around two to three months beforehand, allowing longer if you are starting from a low fitness level. Gradually combine regular walks, hill hikes and strength exercises. Practise in the boots you will wear on the trek.

Altitude and Acclimatisation

The itinerary has no dedicated acclimatisation day. Rest Camp to High Camp brings a substantial gain in sleeping altitude, followed by the upper hike before descending to Badal Danda.

Tell your guide about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not ascend with symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite rest at the same elevation, descend. Good fitness does not prevent altitude illness, and the upper hike may need to change according to health.

Medical Conditions and Travel Insurance

If you have an existing medical condition, seek advice from a healthcare professional familiar with high-altitude travel before booking. Discuss the planned route and any medicines you take.

Insurance is required and excluded from the price. Choose cover for the full planned route, including trekking to approximately 4,500 metres if the higher base camp is selected. Check medical treatment, emergency evacuation, exclusions and assistance procedures. Carry policy details and emergency contacts.

Accommodation and Room Facilities

Your itinerary includes two Kathmandu hotel nights, one in Pokhara and four mountain nights. Pitam Deurali, Rest Camp, High Camp and Badal Danda each have one overnight stay during the programme.

Mountain rooms generally have twin beds and basic furnishings, with shared bathrooms common higher up. High Camp evenings can be cold. Confirm room arrangements and bring suitable sleeping equipment, warm layers and supplies for the lodge nights.

Food and Dietary Requirements

Days 2–5 include breakfast, lunch and dinner with tea or coffee. Lodge menus commonly offer dal bhat, noodles and other simple dishes.

Tell us about allergies or dietary requirements before booking so we can discuss what can be arranged along the route. Explain strict requirements clearly, including any concerns about shared cooking equipment.

Drinking Water

Carry reusable bottles and drink boiled or appropriately treated water. Stream water and untreated tap water may still contain germs that cause illness.

Boiling is the most reliable way to kill germs. If boiling is unavailable, filtration followed by disinfection offers broader protection than many portable filters alone. Follow the instructions for your water-treatment equipment or tablets.

Guide and Porter Support

Your package includes a licensed guide and one porter for every two trekkers. Confirm baggage limits and the solo porter arrangement before packing. Carry water, warm layers and personal essentials in your daypack.

Showers, Charging and Communication

Hot showers, Wi-Fi and device charging may cost extra, with availability varying between lodges. Services can be less reliable higher on the ridge. Carry a charged power bank and tell family that contact may be limited. Ask your guide about available facilities at each overnight stop.

Permits and Documents

ACAP is included. Mardi Himal requires an agency-issued TIMS card and licensed guide under the tourism board’s listed provisions. Confirm TIMS arrangements and costs before departure. Carry your passport, insurance details and permit copies for checking.

Travel Delays and Extra Time

Snow, wind and poor visibility can affect the hike. Rain and road conditions affect transfers, while domestic flight delays can alter the trekking schedule.

You return to Kathmandu on Day 7 and stay overnight before Day 8 departure. Discuss contingency arrangements if flight delays affect your return journey.

Weather and Best Time for the Mardi Himal Trek

Spring, from March to May, and autumn, from September to November, are generally the preferred seasons for Mardi Himal. Both offer opportunities for mountain views and favourable walking conditions, although cold, wind, rain and snowfall remain possible around High Camp and the upper ridge.

Seasonal Weather Conditions

March–May

Spring

Lower forest walks become warmer, with rhododendron flowers possible. High Camp nights remain cold, and snow or ice can persist on the upper trail, particularly early in spring. The higher hike depends on current access.

September–November

Autumn

Early September can retain monsoon rain on lower paths. October and November generally favour clearer views, with colder nights later in the season. Cloud, wind and snowfall can still affect the upper ridge.

December–February

Winter

Cold nights, snow and ice may prevent the upper hike. Lodge availability and trail access need checking. Discuss the intended destination, equipment and alternatives before planning a winter departure.

June–August, sometimes extending into September

Monsoon

Rain can make forest paths muddy and slippery, with leeches possible on lower sections. Clouds often hide the peaks. Road conditions and weather-related flight disruption can affect travel, so allow flexibility around the departure plan.

These are seasonal patterns; conditions can vary from year to year and during an individual trek.

How Weather Changes Along the Route

Forest walks can feel warmer in sunshine, while High Camp becomes colder after sunset. The early upper hike is more exposed, with wind adding to the cold along the ridge. Even in spring and autumn, the upper trail can hold snow and ice. Cloud and wind may affect the hike. Carry warm layers and weather protection, and let your guide assess conditions before leaving High Camp for higher ground.

Which Season Should You Choose?

Choose spring for warming days and possible rhododendron flowers on forest trails. Choose autumn, particularly October or November, if mountain visibility is your priority. Winter requires snow and access checks, while monsoon departures need flexibility around wet paths and travel.

Mardi Himal Trek FAQs

General & Route

Can I do this trek without previous trekking experience? ∨

Yes, with suitable fitness and preparation. This route includes sustained climbs, uneven paths and an exposed upper ridge. Practise longer hill walks with a daypack before departure, and discuss your experience with us before choosing the higher hike.

What is the highest altitude on this itinerary? ∨

The viewpoint is around 4,200 metres. The higher base camp option reaches approximately 4,500 metres if agreed and completed. High Camp, around 3,550–3,580 metres, is the highest overnight stop. The destination depends on conditions and health.

Can we choose between viewpoint and base camp? ∨

Yes, discuss the upper destination before departure. The viewpoint is lower than the higher base camp, which requires additional effort and time. Weather, trail conditions and your health determine how far the group can proceed safely.

Do we sleep overnight at base camp? ∨

No. You stay at High Camp on Day 4, visit the viewpoint or base camp on Day 5, then descend to Badal Danda for the night. The upper hike is a daytime visit, with no overnight stay higher up.

How does this trek differ from Poon Hill trekking? ∨

Mardi Himal climbs to a higher ridge beneath Machhapuchhre, with an optional viewpoint or base camp hike. Poon Hill focuses on forest and village trails with a lower sunrise viewpoint. Compare the walking demands, altitude and time available before choosing.

Is there a dedicated acclimatisation day? ∨

No. The itinerary has five walking days and no acclimatisation day. Rest Camp to High Camp gains substantial sleeping altitude. Walk steadily, report symptoms and discuss adjustments if the ascent or upper hike becomes unsuitable.

Is insurance covering trekking to 4,000 metres sufficient? ∨

No. The viewpoint reaches around 4,200 metres, and the higher base camp may reach approximately 4,500 metres. Choose insurance covering your full planned route, trekking activity, medical treatment and emergency evacuation in Nepal. Check altitude exclusions before departure.

What should I do if I feel unwell at altitude? ∨

Tell your guide about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not ascend with symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite rest at the same elevation, descent is necessary. Your upper hike may need to change.

Do I need to carry my main trekking bag? ∨

A porter carries your main luggage, with one porter for every two trekkers. Confirm the baggage allowance before packing. You carry a daypack with water, warm layers, medicines, valuables and essentials during the walking day.

Do I need to bring a sleeping bag and down jacket? ∨

Plan to bring suitable sleeping equipment and an insulated jacket for cold ridge nights and early starts. Confirm any equipment arrangements before packing. Bring your own boots, clothing and essentials, keeping warm layers accessible during the hike.

Can I book as a solo traveller? ∨

Yes. The solo starting price is from USD 905 per person. Send us your dates so we can confirm availability and your quotation, including the room arrangement, porter allocation and any supplement for your departure.

Is there a Kathmandu night before international departure? ∨

Yes. You fly from Pokhara to Kathmandu on Day 7, share a farewell dinner and stay overnight before Day 8 departure. Domestic flights can still be delayed, so allow flexibility and discuss contingency arrangements before finalising your travel.
