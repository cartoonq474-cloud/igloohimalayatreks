Annapurna Circuit Trek — 14 Days

Follow the Marsyangdi Valley through villages to Manang before crossing Thorong La on our 14-day Annapurna Circuit Trek. You walk through Dharapani, Chame and Upper Pisang, with an acclimatisation day in Manang before continuing towards the pass at 5,416 m. The descent takes you to Muktinath, Kagbeni and Jomsom, where the walking route ends. Along the way, forests give way to open mountain terrain, with steep climbs, cold nights and a demanding pass crossing to prepare for.

Annapurna Circuit Trek Overview

Our 14-day Annapurna Circuit Trek follows the Marsyangdi Valley from Jagat to Manang, then crosses Thorong La at 5,416 m into Mustang. You finish the walking journey in Jomsom after passing Muktinath and Kagbeni. The route combines mountain villages, forest trails and open high-altitude terrain, with an extra night in Manang before the pass approach.

After arriving in Kathmandu, you drive through Besisahar to Jagat, where the walking route begins. The trail follows the Marsyangdi River through Tal and Dharapani, then climbs steadily towards Chame and Upper Pisang. Pine forests and steep valley sides gradually give way to drier, more open mountain slopes. In the villages, stone houses, Buddhist monasteries and carved prayer walls reflect the traditions of communities living along the route.

From Upper Pisang, you continue to Manang by the higher route through Gyaru or the lower approach through Humde. An acclimatisation day includes a hike towards Chhongkor Viewpoint and Gangapurna Lake, returning to Manang to sleep. You then climb through Yak Kharka to Thorong Phedi, your overnight base before the early start for Thorong La.

The pass crossing is the longest and most demanding walking day. You climb to 5,416 m before descending into Mustang to Muktinath, a pilgrimage site respected by Hindus and Buddhists. The following day takes you through Kagbeni to Jomsom beside the Kali Gandaki. Prepare for walking, uneven ground and cold conditions; acclimatisation reduces risk but cannot guarantee an illness-free ascent.

You stay in mountain teahouses during the trek, with hotels in Kathmandu and Pokhara. The programme covers arrival, eight trekking days, an acclimatisation hike, return travel and departure.

Spend an acclimatisation day in Manang, hiking towards Chhongkor Viewpoint and Gangapurna Lake before returning overnight.

Annapurna Circuit Trek Itinerary — Day by Day

Our 14-day itinerary starts and ends in Kathmandu, with an acclimatisation day in Manang before crossing Thorong La. Times and distances are approximate; route choice, weather, road conditions and pace can affect each stage.

Day 1: Arrival in Kathmandu

Arrival & Welcome — Kathmandu (~1,350 m) — Welcome Dinner

MAIN ACTIVITY Airport pickup, hotel transfer & expedition briefing
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 1,350 m
ACCOMMODATION 3-star hotel in Kathmandu, twin sharing
MEALS Welcome dinner included

Welcome to Nepal. Our representative meets you at Tribhuvan International Airport and transfers you to your hotel in Kathmandu. After checking in, you can rest before meeting your guide for an introduction to the Annapurna Circuit programme. Depending on your arrival time, you can explore nearby Thamel or rest after your flight.

In the evening, we share a welcome dinner and discuss the route, equipment and preparations before departure.

Day 2: Drive to Jagat via Besisahar

Scenic Highway & Jeep Journey — Jagat (~1,300 m) — ~8–9 hrs via Besisahar

DRIVING TIME Approximately 8–9 hours
ROAD ROUTE Prithvi Highway & Marsyangdi Valley via Besisahar
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 1,300 m
ACCOMMODATION Mountain teahouse in Jagat (~1,300 m)
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

After breakfast, we leave Kathmandu by road and travel towards Besisahar. The Prithvi Highway follows river valleys through terraced hills and small towns before we turn into the Marsyangdi Valley. At Besisahar, we continue by jeep towards Jagat, following the rougher road beside the Marsyangdi River.

Roadworks, rain and traffic can affect this long travel day. Jagat is our first mountain overnight stop, with teahouses and stone-paved paths above the river. Settle into your room and prepare your daypack for tomorrow’s first walk towards Dharapani.

Day 3: Trek to Dharapani

Marsyangdi Gorge Ascent — Dharapani (~1,960 m) — ~5–6 hrs | ~12–14 km

WALKING TIME Approximately 5–6 hours
TRAIL DISTANCE Approximately 12–14 km
TRAIL CHARACTER River gorge crossings, stone steps & broad riverbed via Tal
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 1,960 m
ACCOMMODATION Mountain teahouse in Dharapani (~1,960 m)
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Our first walking day follows the Marsyangdi Valley from Jagat towards Dharapani. The route passes river crossings and village paths, with climbs and descents between the mountain villages. We continue through Tal, where the valley opens around a broad riverbed beneath steep slopes.

Beyond the village, the route climbs towards Dharapani, with forest sections and prayer walls along the way. Walk at a comfortable pace, getting used to the terrain. We stay in a teahouse and discuss tomorrow’s stage over dinner with your guide.

Day 4: Trek to Chame

Pine Forest & Manaslu Views — Chame (~2,650 m) — ~5–6 hrs | ~14 km

WALKING TIME Approximately 5–6 hours
TRAIL DISTANCE Approximately 14 km
TRAIL CHARACTER Forest trails via Timang & Koto; views of Manaslu & Annapurna II
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 2,650 m
ACCOMMODATION Mountain teahouse in Chame (~2,650 m)
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We leave Dharapani and climb through forest and hillside villages towards Chame. The trail passes Timang village before Koto, with views towards Manaslu when the weather is clear. Sections follow or cross the road.

Chame is the administrative centre of Manang District, with shops and teahouses beside the Marsyangdi River. After today’s uphill walk, settle into your teahouse room and rest. In clear weather, Annapurna II and Lamjung Himal are visible from paths around the village.

Day 5: Trek to Upper Pisang

Paungda Danda Rock Face — Upper Pisang (~3,300 m) — ~5–6 hrs | ~13 km

WALKING TIME Approximately 5–6 hours
TRAIL DISTANCE Approximately 13 km
TRAIL CHARACTER Pine woodlands, sheer rock amphitheatre & Tibetan-style village
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 3,300 m
ACCOMMODATION Mountain teahouse in Upper Pisang (~3,300 m)
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

From Chame, we follow the valley through pine forest towards Bhratang and Dhikur Pokhari. The curved rock face of Paungda Danda rises beside the route as the mountain landscape gradually opens. The final climb leads to Upper Pisang, a hillside village with stone houses and a Buddhist monastery.

Annapurna II stands across the valley when skies are clear. We stay overnight around 3,300 m, so keep the afternoon gentle and tell your guide about headaches, nausea or other symptoms before dinner.

Day 6: Trek to Manang via Gyaru or Humde

High Panoramic Route — Manang (~3,540 m) — ~6–7 hrs | ~16 km

WALKING TIME Approximately 6–7 hours
TRAIL DISTANCE Approximately 16 km
ROUTE CHOICE High trail via Gyaru & Ngawal (scenic) or lower route via Humde
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 3,540 m
ACCOMMODATION Mountain teahouse in Manang (~3,540 m)
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We continue towards Manang, choosing the higher approach through Gyaru or the lower route through Humde according to conditions and group pace. The higher trail requires more climbing but offers wider views above the valley. Both approaches lead towards Braga and Manang, where fields and stone houses sit below the peaks.

The village has teahouses, shops and monasteries, making it a base before the higher stages. We spend tonight here and remain for another night after tomorrow’s acclimatisation hike.

Day 7: Acclimatisation Day in Manang — Hike to Chhongkor Viewpoint and Gangapurna Lake

Active Acclimatisation Hike — Manang (~3,540 m) — ~3–4 hrs | ~5–6 km

WALKING TIME Approximately 3–4 hours
TRAIL DISTANCE Approximately 5–6 km
MAIN ACTIVITY Acclimatisation hike to Chhongkor Viewpoint & Gangapurna Lake
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 3,540 m
ACCOMMODATION Mountain teahouse in Manang (~3,540 m)
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We spend a second night in Manang to allow more time to adjust to altitude. After breakfast, a shorter hike takes us towards Chhongkor Viewpoint and Gangapurna Lake, with the route adapted to your condition. The viewpoint looks across Manang and the surrounding peaks, while the lake lies below Gangapurna Glacier.

We return to our teahouse to rest overnight. This is an active acclimatisation day; report symptoms before attempting the hike or continuing higher tomorrow.

Day 8: Trek to Yak Kharka

Alpine Pasture Ascent — Yak Kharka (~4,050 m) — ~4–5 hrs | ~10 km

WALKING TIME Approximately 4–5 hours
TRAIL DISTANCE Approximately 10 km
TRAIL CHARACTER Open alpine meadows, juniper scrub & gradual ascent via Gunsang
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 4,050 m
ACCOMMODATION Mountain teahouse in Yak Kharka (~4,050 m)
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We leave Manang and climb towards Gunsang, following the trail into open pasture and juniper scrub. The valley becomes quieter as we gain altitude, with fewer trees and wider views of the mountains when the weather is clear.

The route reaches Yak Kharka, where teahouses stand among grazing slopes. Today’s sleeping altitude rises by 510 m, so walk slowly and keep your guide informed about how you feel. At the lodge, rest before the approach to Thorong Phedi.

Day 9: Trek to Thorong Phedi

Pass Base Camp Approach — Thorong Phedi (~4,540 m) — ~4–5 hrs | ~6–7 km

WALKING TIME Approximately 4–5 hours
TRAIL DISTANCE Approximately 6–7 km
TRAIL CHARACTER Rocky mountain paths, suspension bridge & scree slope traverse
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 4,540 m
ACCOMMODATION Mountain teahouse in Thorong Phedi (~4,540 m)
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

From Yak Kharka, we continue towards Thorong Phedi through open, rocky terrain. The route crosses a side valley and follows narrower sections of hillside before reaching the mountain lodges. Thorong Phedi is our overnight base for tomorrow’s pass crossing. We stay here rather than moving to High Camp tonight.

Spend the afternoon resting and preparing your warm layers, headlamp and water for the early departure. Your guide reviews the crossing plan and checks how you feel. If conditions or symptoms make the ascent unsuitable, the programme must change before we continue higher.

Day 10: Cross Thorong La Pass and Trek to Muktinath

High Pass Crossing — Muktinath (~3,800 m) — Thorong La (5,416 m) (~8–9 hrs | ~16 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 8–9 hours
TRAIL DISTANCE Approximately 16 km
HIGHEST ELEVATION 5,416 m (Thorong La Pass)
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 3,800 m
ACCOMMODATION Mountain teahouse in Muktinath area (~3,800 m)
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We start before sunrise for the climb to Thorong La, carrying warm layers and a headlamp. The ascent passes High Camp before continuing over open mountain ground. Take steady steps and follow your guide as the air becomes noticeably thinner. At 5,416 m, the pass marks the highest point of the trek.

After a brief stop, we descend steeply towards Muktinath in Mustang, losing substantial altitude on a long downhill trail. Muktinath is a pilgrimage site for Hindus and Buddhists; we stay nearby tonight.

Day 11: Trek from Muktinath to Jomsom via Kagbeni

Lower Mustang Valley — Jomsom (~2,750 m) — via Kagbeni (~6–7 hrs | ~18 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 6–7 hours
TRAIL DISTANCE Approximately 18 km
TRAIL CHARACTER Arid plateau descent, medieval Kagbeni alleys & windy Kali Gandaki riverbed
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 2,750 m
ACCOMMODATION Mountain teahouse in Jomsom (~2,750 m)
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We descend from the Muktinath area towards Kagbeni, following the dry hills of Lower Mustang. The route brings us into the Kali Gandaki Valley after yesterday’s high pass crossing. Kagbeni has narrow lanes, old houses and a Buddhist monastery beside the river.

After a break, we continue towards Jomsom along the valley, where strong afternoon winds can make walking tiring. Some sections follow the road. Jomsom is our final trekking overnight stop, with time to rest before tomorrow’s drive.

Day 12: Drive from Jomsom to Pokhara

Kali Gandaki Gorge Scenic Drive — Pokhara (~850 m) — ~8–9 hrs Jeep Journey

DRIVING TIME Approximately 8–9 hours
ROAD ROUTE Kali Gandaki Gorge via Marpha, Tukuche, Kalopani & Beni
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 850 m
ACCOMMODATION 3-star hotel in Pokhara, twin sharing (~850 m)
MEALS Breakfast included (lunch & dinner at own expense)

After breakfast, we leave Jomsom by jeep and follow the Kali Gandaki Valley towards Pokhara. The road passes Marpha, Tukuche and Kalopani as dry mountain slopes give way to greener hills. This is a long journey, with rough stretches and possible delays from traffic, rain or roadworks.

On arrival in Pokhara, check into your hotel and rest. If time allows, you can take a walk near Phewa Lake. Lunch and dinner are at your own expense during this return travel day.

Day 13: Drive or Fly from Pokhara to Kathmandu

Return to Capital — Kathmandu (~1,350 m) — Bus (~6–7 hrs) or Optional Flight (~25–30 min)

TRAVEL TIME Bus: ~6–7 hours / Optional flight: ~25–30 mins
TRAVEL MODE Tourist bus via Prithvi Highway or optional scenic domestic flight
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 1,350 m
ACCOMMODATION 3-star hotel in Kathmandu, twin sharing
MEALS Breakfast included

After breakfast, we return to Kathmandu by tourist bus. An optional flight costs extra and depends on availability. Allow for travel delays caused by weather or road conditions. The road journey follows river valleys towards Kathmandu, with stops along the way.

If flying, allow time for airport formalities and possible delays. Once at your hotel, you can rest, organise your belongings or explore Thamel before tomorrow’s departure.

Day 14: Departure from Kathmandu

Trip Concludes — Kathmandu (~1,350 m) — Tribhuvan Airport Transfer

MAIN ACTIVITY Hotel checkout, airport transfer & final departure
OVERNIGHT No overnight stay (tour concludes)
MEALS Breakfast included

Your Annapurna Circuit Trek ends today. After breakfast, you prepare for your onward journey. Depending on your flight time, you may have an opportunity for a final walk nearby before leaving the hotel for departure.

Our representative transfers you to Tribhuvan International Airport for your departure.

1. Trek Difficulty and Fitness

Demanding mountain circuit; steep climbs, Thorong La crossing (5,416 m) & sustained daily walking.

The Annapurna Circuit does not require technical climbing skills in suitable conditions, but it is demanding. You walk on consecutive days, with climbs and long descents on uneven ground. Thorong La reaches 5,416 m, and the crossing takes around eight to nine hours in favourable conditions.

Previous hiking experience is helpful. First-time trekkers should build endurance before departure and become comfortable walking uphill and downhill regularly with a daypack.

Begin preparing around two to three months beforehand, allowing longer if you are starting from a low fitness level. Gradually combine regular walks, hill hikes and strength exercises. Practise in the boots you will wear on the trek.

2. Altitude and Acclimatisation

Two nights acclimatisation in Manang; gradual ascent; prompt reporting of any altitude symptoms.

We spend two nights in Manang, with a shorter hike on Day 7 before continuing higher. This allows time to adjust, although altitude illness remains possible during the ascent.

Tell your guide promptly about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not continue to a higher sleeping altitude while experiencing symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite resting at the same elevation, descent is necessary. Reaching Thorong La must remain secondary to your health.

3. Medical Conditions and Travel Insurance

Mandatory emergency helicopter evacuation and medical insurance covering up to 5,416 m.

If you have an existing medical condition, seek advice from a healthcare professional familiar with high-altitude travel before booking. Discuss your planned route and any medicines you take.

Arrange travel insurance covering trekking to at least 5,416 metres, emergency helicopter evacuation and medical treatment in Nepal. Check exclusions, excess charges and the insurer’s emergency assistance procedure beforehand. Carry your policy details and emergency contact number during every stage of the trek.

4. Accommodation and Room Facilities

3-star twin-share hotels in Kathmandu & Pokhara; authentic mountain teahouses on the trail.

You stay in twin-sharing three-star hotels in Kathmandu and Pokhara, with breakfast included, and mountain teahouses during the trek. Rooms become simpler as you gain altitude towards the high pass approach.

Expect basic rooms and shared bathroom facilities at Yak Kharka and Thorong Phedi. Private rooms can be limited, and sharing may be necessary during busy periods. Nights above Manang are often cold, even in spring and autumn.

5. Food and Dietary Requirements

Full board teahouse dining (dal bhat, soups, noodles); vegetarian, vegan & allergy diets catered.

Trekking meals are included along the route. Teahouse menus commonly offer dal bhat, noodles, potatoes and soups, with options varying between lodges.

Tell us about allergies or dietary requirements before booking so we can discuss what can be arranged along the route. Explain strict requirements clearly, including any concerns about shared cooking equipment.

6. Drinking Water

Reusable bottles required; boiled or treated drinking water recommended at every lodge.

Carry reusable bottles and drink boiled or appropriately treated water. Stream water and untreated tap water may still contain germs that cause illness.

Boiling is the most reliable way to kill germs. If boiling is unavailable, filtration followed by disinfection offers broader protection than many portable filters alone. Follow the instructions for your water-treatment equipment or tablets.

7. Guide and Porter Support

Licensed English-speaking guide and insured porter support included throughout the journey.

An experienced, English-speaking, licensed trekking guide and porter support are both included. Your porter carries the main bag while you carry daily essentials.

Confirm the porter-sharing arrangement and luggage allowance before leaving Kathmandu.

8. Showers, Charging and Communication

Hot showers, Wi-Fi & device charging available at extra charge; carry a reliable power bank.

Hot showers, Wi-Fi and device charging cost extra, with availability varying between lodges. Mobile reception and internet connections can be unreliable, particularly around Yak Kharka and Thorong Phedi.

Carry a power bank and let family know that you may be out of contact during trekking stages.

9. Permits and Documents

ACAP entry permit included; TIMS card and required documentation fully arranged.

The ACAP entry permit is included. TIMS registration is also required under Nepal Tourism Board rules; confirm its fee coverage with us.

Carry your passport and insurance details. Your Nepal visa is separate from the package.

10. Road Delays and Extra Time

Mountain road transit buffer; optional tourist bus or flight from Pokhara; contingency days advised.

Rough roads, roadworks and heavy rain can delay the transfers to Jagat and from Jomsom. Allow flexibility around return travel before your international departure.

The included Pokhara–Kathmandu journey is by tourist bus; flights are optional. Ask about the flight supplement and allow time in case travel plans change.

Weather and Best Time for the Annapurna Circuit Trek

Spring, from March to May, and autumn, from September to November, are the best seasons for the Annapurna Circuit. Both offer favourable walking conditions and opportunities for mountain views, although rain, cloud, cold weather and snowfall remain possible, particularly around the Thorong La crossing.

Seasonal Weather Conditions

4 Seasons Matrix
Season Months What to Expect
Spring March–May Lower valleys gradually become warmer, with rhododendrons flowering in suitable forest sections. March can still bring snow and ice near Thorong La. April often balances walking temperatures and visibility, while May can bring afternoon cloud or rain.
Autumn September–November Early September can retain monsoon rain. October and November generally offer clearer mountain views after the rains, with cooler mornings and increasingly cold nights above Manang. Popular weeks can be busy, and fresh snowfall is still possible.
Winter December–February Clear days and quieter trails are possible, but cold, short daylight hours, snow and ice make the high crossing more demanding. Heavy snowfall can prevent access to Thorong La, and some mountain lodges may close.
Monsoon June–August Rain affects the lower valley and approach roads, making trails slippery and increasing landslide risks. Manang and Mustang are relatively drier, but cloud, unstable slopes and weather around the pass still require flexible travel plans.
These are seasonal patterns; conditions can vary from year to year and during an individual trek.

How Weather Changes Along the Route

Elevation gradients & trail exposure

The lower Marsyangdi Valley is warmer, with forested slopes beside the river. Conditions become colder and more exposed beyond Upper Pisang, especially during the approach from Manang towards Thorong Phedi.

Around Thorong La, wind and snow can make the crossing difficult even during popular trekking months. The descent towards Muktinath and Jomsom enters drier terrain, where afternoon winds can be strong.

Annapurna Circuit Trek FAQs

General & Route

Can I do this trek without previous trekking experience? ∨
Hiking experience helps because the route includes consecutive walking days and a demanding high pass. First-time trekkers should prepare with hill walks and discuss fitness and altitude concerns before booking. Technical climbing skills are unnecessary in suitable conditions.
What is the highest altitude on this itinerary? ∨
Thorong La Pass at approximately 5,416 metres is the highest point. We cross it on Day 10 and descend to Muktinath afterwards. Thorong Phedi is the previous night’s base; we do not sleep at the pass.
Does this route include Tilicho Lake or Poon Hill? ∨
No. This 14-day route crosses Thorong La and finishes walking at Jomsom. Tilicho Lake features in our 18-day Annapurna Circuit Trek package, while the Classic Annapurna Circuit continues through lower valleys to Ghorepani and Poon Hill.
How does the 17-day package differ from this itinerary? ∨
Our Classic Annapurna Circuit Trek — 17 Days continues beyond Muktinath through Marpha, Tatopani and Ghorepani, including the Poon Hill sunrise hike. This 14-day itinerary instead walks through Kagbeni to Jomsom, then returns to Pokhara by road on Day 12.
Do I need to carry my main trekking bag? ∨
Porter support is included, so you carry a daypack with water, warm layers, medicines and valuables. Your main belongings travel separately with the porter. Confirm the luggage allowance and porter-sharing arrangement before departure from Kathmandu.
Do I need to bring a sleeping bag and down jacket? ∨
We provide a sleeping bag, down jacket and duffel bag for use during the trek. Return them afterwards. Bring your own boots, clothing and personal equipment, and ask about the supplied gear if you have particular requirements.
Do we sleep at Thorong La Pass? ∨
No. You stay at Thorong Phedi on Day 9, cross the pass the following morning and descend to the Muktinath area for the night. The pass is a daytime high point, with the crossing dependent on conditions and health.
Are acclimatisation days complete rest days? ∨
No. The Manang day includes a shorter hike towards Chhongkor Viewpoint and Gangapurna Lake before returning to the same teahouse. Your guide adjusts the outing to conditions and how you feel; report symptoms before starting.
Does being fit protect me from altitude sickness? ∨
No. Good fitness helps with the walking demands, but it does not prevent altitude illness. Fit and experienced trekkers can also become unwell. Follow your guide’s advice and report symptoms promptly instead of assuming fitness will protect you.
What should I do if I feel unwell at altitude? ∨
Tell your guide promptly about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not ascend to a higher sleeping altitude while experiencing symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite rest at the same elevation, descent is necessary.
Is insurance covering trekking to 5,500 metres sufficient? ∨
A 5,500-metre altitude limit covers the planned maximum of 5,416 metres, but altitude alone is insufficient. Confirm cover for this trekking route, emergency helicopter evacuation and medical treatment, including exclusions, policy limits and the insurer’s emergency assistance procedure.
Can I book as a solo traveller? ∨
Yes. You can enquire about joining a group departure or arranging a private trek. Send us your preferred dates so we can check the available arrangements and confirm the price that applies to your booking.
