Everest View Trek Package Overview

Our seven-day Everest View Trek follows the lower Khumbu trail from Lukla to Namche Bazaar, with a hike to Hotel Everest View at around 3,880 m. The route combines Sherpa villages, forests and suspension bridges with a mountain viewpoint visit. You return to Namche to sleep before retracing the trail to Lukla the following day.

After flying to Lukla, you walk through village paths and forested slopes towards Phakding beside the Dudh Koshi River. The next day brings more suspension bridges and a sustained climb to Namche Bazaar. Prayer wheels, mani walls and Buddhist shrines reflect the traditions of the communities along the route. Namche offers shops, cafés and bakeries to explore during your two-night stay, with walking paths above the hillside town.

Day 4 takes you above Namche towards Syangboche and Hotel Everest View. In clear weather, the viewpoint looks towards Everest, Lhotse and Ama Dablam among the surrounding peaks. The walk gains height during the day before returning to Namche. Your last trekking day follows the valley through Monjo and Phakding, with further climbs before Lukla.

The programme includes one acclimatisation day in Namche, combining a hike with another night at the same elevation. You still need to prepare for consecutive walking days, uneven ground and long climbs. The ascent to Namche and the return to Lukla require steady effort. Technical climbing skills are unnecessary, but the shorter itinerary does not remove the effects of altitude.

You stay in mountain teahouses during the trek and a hotel in Kathmandu. The seven-day programme covers your arrival, the walking journey, return travel and departure from Nepal.

Read Before You Book

Before booking your Everest View Trek Package, consider the daily walking, altitude and level of comfort on the route. These details will help you prepare and decide whether this itinerary suits you.

The Everest View Trek follows walking trails and does not require technical climbing skills. The main challenges are steep climbs, descents and uneven ground. The ascent to Namche takes sustained effort, while the return to Lukla is a long walking day with uphill stretches before arrival.

Previous hiking experience is helpful. First-time trekkers should build their endurance before departure and become comfortable walking uphill and downhill with a daypack.

Begin preparing around two to three months beforehand, allowing longer if you are starting from a low fitness level. Gradually combine regular walks, hill hikes and strength exercises. Practise in the boots you will wear on the trek.

The itinerary includes an acclimatisation day in Namche Bazaar. A higher hike and another night in the town allow time to adjust, but altitude illness can still affect you.

Tell your guide immediately about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not continue to a higher sleeping altitude while experiencing symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite resting at the same elevation, descent is necessary. Reaching the viewpoint must remain secondary to your health.

If you have an existing medical condition, seek advice from a healthcare professional familiar with high-altitude travel before booking. Discuss your planned route and any medicines you take.

Arrange insurance covering the full trekking route, including the hike to approximately 3,880 metres, emergency helicopter evacuation and medical treatment in Nepal. Check activity and altitude limits, exclusions, excess charges and the insurer’s assistance procedure. Carry your policy details and emergency contact information.

You stay two nights in a three-star Kathmandu hotel and four nights in teahouses along the route. Confirm room sharing and bathroom arrangements in your quotation before leaving for the mountains.

Teahouse rooms and facilities vary between Phakding, Namche and Lukla. Nights can feel cold, and upgrades are not included. Discuss your room preferences, bathroom needs and bedding beforehand so you know what to expect at each stop.

Tell us about allergies or dietary requirements before booking so we can discuss what can be arranged along the route. Explain any strict requirements clearly, including concerns about shared cooking equipment.

Carry reusable bottles and drink boiled or appropriately treated water. Water from a clear stream or an untreated tap may still contain germs.

Boiling is the most reliable way to kill germs. If boiling is unavailable, filtration followed by disinfection offers broader protection than many portable filters alone. Follow the instructions for your treatment equipment or tablets.

An experienced trekking guide supports the journey, with one porter shared between two trekkers. Porters carry your main luggage; you carry a daypack during walks. Confirm the luggage allowance and any requirements beforehand.

Shower, Wi-Fi and charging arrangements differ between lodges. Confirm which services the quotation covers beforehand, as extras and upgrades may cost more. Connections can be unreliable around Namche. Carry a power bank and let family know that you may be out of contact during the trek.

Weather and Best Time for the Everest View Trek Package

Spring, from March to May, and autumn, from September to November, are generally the main seasons for the Everest View Trek Package. Both offer favourable walking conditions and opportunities for mountain views, although cold weather, cloud and snowfall remain possible around the higher viewpoints.

How Weather Changes Along the Route

Elevation gradients & trail exposure
Lower Valleys to Namche

Walking around Phakding can feel warmer in sunshine. Namche and the Hotel Everest View area are cooler, particularly before sunrise and after sunset.

Exposed Viewpoint Sections

Wind increases the chill on exposed sections. Even during spring and autumn, nights in Namche can be cold. The viewpoint hike reaches higher, where wind, cloud or snow may affect conditions.

Carry warm layers, waterproof clothing and gloves, and adjust the walk if the weather deteriorates.

Everest View Trek Package FAQs

Route & Itinerary

Can I do this trek without previous trekking experience?

Yes, provided you prepare well and have suitable fitness. Previous hiking experience helps, but technical climbing skills are unnecessary. Before departure, practise longer hill walks with a daypack and become comfortable walking uphill and downhill on consecutive days.

Can I see Mount Everest during this trek?

Yes, the viewpoint around Hotel Everest View offers views towards Everest in clear weather. Lhotse, Ama Dablam and other Khumbu peaks may also be visible. Cloud can obscure the mountains, so the itinerary cannot guarantee visibility.

Do we sleep at Hotel Everest View?

No. You visit Hotel Everest View during the day and return to a teahouse in Namche for the night. This package includes two nights in Namche, with the viewpoint hike between them. Overnight accommodation at the hotel is excluded.

Does this shorter trek also reach Everest Base Camp?

No. This itinerary reaches Namche and Hotel Everest View before returning to Lukla. It does not continue to Tengboche, Dingboche, Gorak Shep or Everest Base Camp. Those higher places require additional walking days and acclimatisation arrangements on a longer programme.

What is the highest altitude on this itinerary?

Hotel Everest View, at around 3,880 metres, is the highest planned point. You visit during the Day 4 hike from Namche Bazaar. Namche, at 3,440 metres, is your highest overnight stop, where you spend two nights.

Are acclimatisation days complete rest days?

Day 4 includes a hike above Namche towards Hotel Everest View, followed by another night in the town. The pace and turnaround point depend on weather, trail conditions and how you feel during the walk.

Does being fit protect me from altitude sickness?

No. Good fitness helps with the walking demands, but it does not prevent altitude illness. Fit and experienced trekkers can also become unwell. Follow the acclimatisation schedule and report symptoms immediately instead of assuming fitness will protect you.

What should I do if I feel unwell at altitude?

Tell your guide immediately about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not ascend to a higher sleeping altitude while experiencing symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite rest at the same elevation, descent is necessary.

Do I need to carry my main trekking bag?

A porter carries your main trekking luggage, with one porter shared between two trekkers. You carry your daypack containing water, weather protection, medicines and other daily essentials. Keep your passport, money and valuables with you.

Do I need to bring a sleeping bag and down jacket?

We provide a sleeping bag, down jacket and duffel bag for use during the trip. All three must be returned afterwards. Bring your own boots, clothing, toiletries, medicines and electronics, and check equipment suitability before leaving Kathmandu.

Can I book as a solo traveller?

Yes. The starting price for one person is USD 995. Send us your dates to check availability and confirm the final quotation, accommodation, porter arrangements and any private or group options for your seven-day trek.

Do I need insurance for this shorter trek?

Arrange insurance for the route, including trekking to around 3,880 metres, helicopter evacuation and medical treatment in Nepal. Check activity and altitude limits, exclusions and the assistance procedure. The shorter duration does not remove altitude or evacuation concerns.
