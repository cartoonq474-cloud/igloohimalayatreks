Manaslu Tsum Valley Trek — 20 Days

Combine Tsum Valley’s monastery trails with the Manaslu Circuit on this twenty-day trek. Follow the Budhi Gandaki before entering Tsum to visit Mu Gompa and Ranchen Gompa, then continue through Namrung, Lho and Samagaun towards Larkya La. The route includes an acclimatisation day in Samagaun before the high pass crossing and descent through Bimthang and Tilje. Expect consecutive walking days, steep climbs and basic mountain accommodation, with road journeys connecting the trail to Kathmandu at either end.

Manaslu Tsum Valley Trek Overview

Our twenty-day Manaslu Tsum Valley Trek combines a journey to Mu Gompa and Ranchen Gompa with the Manaslu Circuit. The section follows Tsum’s village and monastery trails before returning towards the Budhi Gandaki. Continue through Namrung, Lho and Samagaun, then cross Larkya La at approximately 5,160 metres before descending to Bimthang and the lower valley.

After travelling by road to Machha Khola, follow the Budhi Gandaki through Jagat and the Philim area. The route turns towards the Siyar Khola and enters Tsum through Chumling. Higher up, fields, mani walls and chortens accompany the approach to Chhokangparo and Nile. Monasteries form part of daily life here, while local non-violence traditions reflect the relationship between local Buddhist practice, wildlife and the surrounding natural mountain environment.

Mu Gompa, around 3,700 metres, marks the upper end of the Tsum section. Descend to Ranchen Gompa before the planned return towards Deng and the main Manaslu trail. Beyond Deng, the route continues through the Nubri area, where Namrung, Lho and Samagaun bring village stays, monastery visits and views towards Manaslu when conditions are clear.

Two nights at Samagaun allow a short acclimatisation walk before continuing to Samdo and Dharmasala. The pass day includes a long ascent over rocky ground and a substantial descent to Bimthang. Forests return on the way to Tilje and Dharapani. Good walking fitness, warm equipment and attention to altitude are important, while the extended Tsum return stage needs advance planning.

You stay in mountain teahouses or lodges during the trek and a Kathmandu hotel. The twenty-day programme covers arrival, monastery trails, the circuit, return road travel and departure.

Manaslu Tsum Valley Trek Itinerary — Day by Day

Our twenty-day itinerary combines Tsum Valley, the Manaslu Circuit and a short acclimatisation hike in Samagaun. Times are approximate; road access, trail conditions, weather and individual progress can affect the walking schedule and arrangements.

Day 1: Arrival in Kathmandu

Arrival — Approximately 1,400 m — Arrival in Kathmandu

Overnight altitude Approximately 1,400 m
Accommodation 3-star hotel in Kathmandu; room sharing as quoted
Meals Arrival-day meals at your own expense

Welcome to Nepal. Our representative meets you at Tribhuvan International Airport and transfers you to your hotel. After settling in, we discuss the trekking programme, review equipment and check documents needed for the Manaslu and Tsum permits.

Depending on your arrival time, explore nearby streets or rest after your flight. Keep your passport, insurance information and trekking gear ready, and confirm tomorrow’s road transfer before leaving for Machha Khola.

Day 2: Drive from Kathmandu to Machha Khola

Scenic Drive — Approximately 900 m — Approximately 8–10 hours

Driving time Approximately 8–10 hours; road conditions and traffic affect timing
Main activity Road transfer to Machha Khola; vehicle arrangements as quoted
Overnight altitude Approximately 900 m
Accommodation Teahouse or lodge in Machha Khola
Meals Hotel breakfast; travel-day lunch and dinner as quoted

We leave Kathmandu by road and travel towards Machha Khola in Gorkha. Towns, cultivated hillsides and river valleys mark the journey before rougher roads approach the trekking area.

Traffic, repairs and wet road conditions can extend the drive, so keep water, medicines and a warm layer accessible. At Machha Khola, settle into the lodge and organise your daypack for tomorrow’s first walking stage beside the Budhi Gandaki.

Day 3: Trek from Machha Khola to Jagat

Trek Stage — Approximately 1,340 m — Approximately 6–7 hours

Walking time Approximately 6–7 hours
Trail character Riverside paths, stone steps, bridges and repeated climbs
Overnight altitude Approximately 1,340 m
Accommodation Teahouse in Jagat
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Our journey follows the Budhi Gandaki towards Jagat, passing lower-valley villages and crossing bridges along the river. Stone paths, shorter climbs and uneven stretches make this a full trekking day.

Forested slopes and cultivated areas accompany sections of the route, with local homes and roadside lodges along the river valley. We take the climbs steadily and allow time for breaks. Jagat is an important checkpoint for the Manaslu region. After reaching the village, settle into the teahouse and prepare for tomorrow’s continued walk towards Chisopani.

Day 4: Trek from Jagat to Chisopani

Trek Stage — Approximately 1,660 m — Approximately 4–5 hours

Walking time Approximately 4–5 hours
Trail character Village paths through the Philim area and wooded hillsides
Overnight altitude Approximately 1,660 m
Accommodation Teahouse in Chisopani; exact lodge stop confirmed before departure
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We continue through the Budhi Gandaki Valley, passing village paths and cultivated slopes towards the Philim area. Bridges link sections of the trail, while forested hills rise above the river and its narrow valley floor.

The route includes climbs and descents before reaching Chisopani, the lower-valley stop named in this programme. Prayer flags and stone buildings add interest around the villages. After arrival, rest at the teahouse and prepare for the following day’s turn towards the Tsum Valley trail.

Day 5: Trek from Chisopani to Chumling

Trek Stage — Approximately 2,386 m — Approximately 6–7 hours

Walking time Approximately 6–7 hours
Trail character Tsum Valley approach through the Lokpa area and Siyar Khola valley
Overnight altitude Approximately 2,386 m
Accommodation Teahouse in Chumling
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We leave the Budhi Gandaki route and follow the approach towards Tsum Valley through the Lokpa area. Forest paths, river crossings and hillside climbs lead towards Chumling above the Siyar Khola.

The trail can be steep and uneven, so take care on narrow sections and give pack animals room to pass. Chumling introduces the village landscape of Tsum, with Buddhist shrines and stone houses around the walking route. After reaching the teahouse, rest before tomorrow’s climb towards the village of Chhokangparo.

Day 6: Trek from Chumling to Chhokangparo

Trek Stage — Approximately 3,010 m — Approximately 4–6 hours

Walking time Approximately 4–6 hours
Trail character Valley paths and a sustained climb into upper Tsum
Overnight altitude Approximately 3,010 m
Accommodation Teahouse in Chhokangparo
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We follow the Siyar Khola through village paths and wooded stretches. A sustained climb leads towards Chhokangparo, where fields and houses spread across a broader part of the upper Tsum Valley.

Mani walls, prayer flags and chortens reflect the valley’s Buddhist traditions. Walk around religious structures respectfully and ask before photographing people. Today brings an increase in sleeping height, so report symptoms early. After checking into the teahouse, keep the afternoon gentle before tomorrow’s walk towards Nile and the monastery route.

Day 7: Trek from Chhokangparo to Nile

Trek Stage — Approximately 3,361 m — Approximately 5–6 hours

Walking time Approximately 5–6 hours
Trail character Upper-valley village paths, fields and open slopes
Overnight altitude Approximately 3,361 m
Accommodation Teahouse in Nile or the nearby Chhule area, as arranged
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We follow the upper valley towards Nile, passing fields, village houses and Buddhist shrines. The route gains height through a more open landscape, with views towards the surrounding mountains when the weather is clear.

Tsum’s villages are known for their Buddhist traditions. Respect customs and give people space around homes and religious sites. After reaching Nile, settle into the lodge and prepare for tomorrow’s walk towards Mu Gompa, the uppermost overnight stop in the Tsum section.

Day 8: Trek from Nile or Chhule to Mu Gompa

Trek Stage — Approximately 3,700 m — Approximately 3–5 hours

Walking time Approximately 3–5 hours; starting lodge and visits affect timing
Main activity Upper-valley walk and monastery visit when open
Overnight altitude Approximately 3,700 m
Accommodation Basic lodge or monastery-area accommodation near Mu Gompa, as arranged
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Leaving the Nile and Chhule area, we walk higher through open slopes and grazing ground towards Mu Gompa. The mountains become more prominent as the valley broadens beneath the high mountain ridges surrounding the monastery.

Mu Gompa is one of Tsum’s main Buddhist centres. Visiting depends on access and activities, so follow local guidance before entering or taking photographs. Tonight’s accommodation is basic, with fewer facilities than lower villages. Rest afterwards and prepare for tomorrow’s return towards Ranchen Gompa.

Day 9: Trek from Mu Gompa to Ranchen Gompa

Trek Stage — Approximately 3,240 m — Approximately 5–6 hours

Walking time Approximately 5–6 hours
Main activity Descend through upper Tsum and visit Ranchen Gompa when open
Overnight altitude Approximately 3,240 m
Accommodation Basic teahouse or lodge near Ranchen Gompa
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We descend from Mu Gompa through the upper valley towards Ranchen Gompa, also commonly written Rachen Gompa. Grazing areas, village paths and Buddhist shrines accompany the return, with changing views as we lose height.

The monastery is an important centre for the Tsum community. Follow visiting customs and ask before photography. After the visit, settle into accommodation and prepare for the extended return towards the Manaslu trail, with tomorrow’s route and overnight arrangements confirmed in advance.

Day 10: Trek from Ranchen Gompa to Deng

Trek Stage — Approximately 1,860 m — Extended linking stage

Walking time Extended linking stage; route and timing must be agreed before booking
Trail character Return from Tsum towards the main Manaslu trail
Planned overnight altitude Approximately 1,860 m
Accommodation Teahouse in Deng, subject to the confirmed walking plan
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

The planned route leaves Ranchen Gompa and returns down the Tsum Valley towards the Chumling and Lokpa areas. It then joins the Budhi Gandaki river corridor before continuing north towards Deng on the Manaslu Circuit.

This is an extended linking stage rather than a routine short descent. Confirm the route, walking time and overnight plan before booking. The operating team must assess trail access and the group’s pace; the agreed arrangements determine how the return from Tsum is completed.

Day 11: Trek from Deng to Namrung

Trek Stage — Approximately 2,630 m — Approximately 6–7 hours

Walking time Approximately 6–7 hours
Trail character River crossings, forest paths and a sustained final climb
Overnight altitude Approximately 2,630 m
Accommodation Teahouse in Namrung
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We follow the Budhi Gandaki through slopes and village paths towards Namrung. River crossings and repeated climbs connect the route, with the final ascent bringing us further into the Manaslu landscape.

Buddhist shrines and carved mani stones become more frequent along the trail. The valley surroundings gradually change as the route enters the Nubri area, which is distinct from the Tsum Valley visited earlier. At Namrung, settle into the teahouse and rest before continuing towards Lho and clearer views of Manaslu.

Day 12: Trek from Namrung to Lho Gham

Trek Stage — Approximately 3,180 m — Approximately 4–6 hours

Walking time Approximately 4–6 hours
Trail character Climbing village trails, fields and forest sections
Overnight altitude Approximately 3,180 m
Accommodation Teahouse in Lho
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We climb from Namrung through village paths and cultivated ground towards Lho, also named Lho Gham in this itinerary. The route passes Buddhist shrines and wooded sections, with the peaks appearing when the weather allows.

Lho is known for views towards Manaslu and for Ribung Monastery above the village. A visit depends on opening and arrival time. Today gains sleeping height, so take the stretches steadily. At the teahouse, rest before tomorrow’s walk through the Shyala area towards Samagaun.

Day 13: Trek from Lho Gham to Samagaun

Trek Stage — Approximately 3,530 m — Approximately 4–6 hours

Walking time Approximately 4–6 hours
Trail character Forest and open village paths through the Shyala area
Overnight altitude Approximately 3,530 m
Accommodation Teahouse in Samagaun
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Leaving Lho, we continue through forest and more open ground towards Shyala and Samagaun. Clear weather offers views of the Manaslu mountains, while grazing areas and village paths accompany the walk.

Samagaun is an important overnight stop before the route climbs towards Samdo and Larkya La. Houses, mani walls and Buddhist traditions reflect the culture of Nubri. After checking into the teahouse, keep the afternoon relaxed. We stay for two nights, allowing tomorrow for a short acclimatisation walk and further rest.

Day 14: Acclimatisation Day with a Small Hike

Trek Stage — Depends on the chosen short hike and turnaround — Approximately 2–4 hours

Walking time Approximately 2–4 hours; route and turnaround depend on health and conditions
Main activity Selected short walk around Samagaun; Birendra Lake is one possible direction
Daytime high point Depends on the chosen short hike and turnaround
Overnight altitude Approximately 3,530 m
Accommodation Same teahouse in Samagaun
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We stay another night in Samagaun and take a short acclimatisation walk. The direction and turnaround depend on health, weather and the group’s progress, with the Birendra Lake area one possible option for exploration.

The purpose is adjustment time and light activity before continuing higher. Return to the same teahouse afterwards, eat well and rest. Discuss any symptoms with the guide and prepare your daypack for tomorrow’s walk towards Samdo, where the valley becomes more open.

Day 15: Trek from Samagaun to Samdo

Trek Stage — Approximately 3,875 m — Approximately 4–5 hours

Walking time Approximately 4–5 hours
Trail character Open upper-valley paths, grazing areas and shorter climbs
Overnight altitude Approximately 3,875 m
Accommodation Teahouse in Samdo
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We leave Samagaun and continue through the upper valley towards Samdo. Trees become fewer as grazing areas, stony slopes and views of the mountains shape the walk at this higher elevation.

Although the stage is shorter than several earlier days, altitude still makes the walking demanding. Keep a steady pace and report any symptoms promptly. Samdo has basic lodges and a strong Tibetan Buddhist cultural influence. After reaching the teahouse, rest and discuss tomorrow’s climb to Dharmasala before the pass crossing.

Day 16: Trek from Samdo to Dharmasala

Trek Stage — Approximately 4,460 m — Approximately 4–5 hours

Walking time Approximately 4–5 hours
Main activity Climb to the final overnight stop before Larkya La
Overnight altitude Approximately 4,460 m
Accommodation Very basic lodge at Dharmasala or Larkya Phedi; sharing as arranged
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We continue towards Dharmasala, also known as Larkya Phedi, the final overnight stop before the pass. The walking route climbs across increasingly rocky ground, with open slopes and glacial scenery replacing the lower valley’s forests.

Sleeping height rises, so walk steadily and report how you feel. Facilities at Dharmasala are basic and nights are cold. Keep warm layers, water and your headlamp ready, then rest after dinner before tomorrow’s early departure for Larkya La and the descent to Bimthang.

Day 17: Cross Larkya La Pass and Descend to Bimthang

Trek Stage — Larkya La Pass, approximately 5,160 m — Approximately 8–10 hours

Walking time Approximately 8–10 hours; snow and trail conditions can extend timing
Main activity High pass crossing and a long descent
Highest point Larkya La Pass, approximately 5,160 m
Overnight altitude Bimthang, approximately 3,590 m
Accommodation Teahouse or lodge in Bimthang
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We start early from Dharmasala and follow the approach towards Larkya La. Altitude makes the ascent demanding, with snow or ice possible on the trail and near the pass.

At approximately 5,160 metres, Larkya La is the highest point of the trek. Clear weather offers views across the surrounding mountains and glaciers. The guide assesses conditions and the group’s progress before continuing towards the descent.

Beyond the pass, descend carefully over steep, uneven ground towards Bimthang. This long day finishes at a lower elevation, with pastureland and mountain lodges below the peaks.

Day 18: Trek from Bimthang to Tilje

Trek Stage — Approximately 2,295 m — Approximately 6–8 hours

Walking time Approximately 6–8 hours
Trail character Long descent through pastures and forest into the Dudh Khola valley
Overnight altitude Approximately 2,295 m
Accommodation Teahouse in Tilje
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We descend from Bimthang through open grazing ground before entering forest along the lower route. Rhododendron and other trees bring a change from the rocky pass approach, while the Dudh Khola valley leads towards warmer, greener hills and village paths.

The walking still includes uneven stones, steps and climbs between the downhill stretches. Take care when tired after the pass crossing, using poles where helpful. After reaching Tilje, settle into the teahouse and organise your belongings for the walk to Dharapani and road return.

Day 19: Trek to Dharapani and Drive to Kathmandu

Scenic Drive — Approximately 1,400 m — Approximately 2–3 hours

Walking time Approximately 2–3 hours; road access may affect the pickup point
Driving time Full-day road journey, often 8–10 hours via Besisahar; delays are possible
Main activity Walk from Tilje to Dharapani, then return by road
Overnight altitude Approximately 1,400 m
Accommodation 3-star hotel in Kathmandu
Meals Breakfast and trail lunch; Kathmandu farewell dinner

We walk down from Tilje towards Dharapani, where we meet the Annapurna Circuit corridor. After reaching the pickup point, board the vehicle for the road journey to Kathmandu.

The drive continues through the Besisahar area, with timing affected by road conditions and traffic. After reaching your hotel, rest and prepare for international departure. A farewell dinner is included, with its timing arranged around the day’s road journey.

Day 20: Departure from Kathmandu

Departure — Kathmandu (1,400 m / 4,593 ft) — Airport Transfer & Departure

Main activity Departure from Nepal and airport transfer
Meals Breakfast

Your twenty-day Manaslu Tsum Valley Trek ends today. After breakfast, prepare for your onward journey. Depending on your departure time, there may be an opportunity for a final walk near the hotel before leaving Kathmandu.

Our representative accompanies you to Tribhuvan International Airport for your departure.

Trek Difficulty and Fitness

This is a challenging trek because of its length, repeated climbs and altitude. Forest paths, stone steps and rocky slopes feature on consecutive days. Larkya La adds a demanding ascent and long descent, while the extended return from Tsum requires an agreed walking plan before departure.

Previous trekking helps. Build endurance for walks on consecutive days, with a daypack, and practise handling sustained uphill and downhill sections before departure.

Begin preparing around two to three months beforehand, allowing longer if you are starting from a low fitness level. Gradually combine regular walks, hill hikes and strength exercises. Practise in the boots you will wear on the trek.

Altitude and Acclimatisation

Two nights at Samagaun provide additional time for a short acclimatisation walk. The route also includes substantial sleeping-height gains in Tsum and before Dharmasala, requiring assessment before continuing higher.

Tell your guide promptly about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not continue to a higher sleeping altitude while experiencing symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite resting at the same elevation, descent is necessary. Crossing Larkya La must remain secondary to your health.

Medical Conditions and Travel Insurance

Discuss heart conditions, respiratory issues or joint problems with a doctor before booking. Carry an adequate supply of personal prescription medicines, blister supplies, rehydration salts and basic remedies. Keep them in your daypack, not in the duffel carried by a porter.

Comprehensive travel insurance is mandatory. Your policy must cover emergency medical care, high-altitude trekking up to 5,200 m, helicopter evacuation and unexpected itinerary changes. Check altitude limits carefully, as standard leisure travel policies frequently exclude treks above 3,000 m or 4,000 m.

Accommodation and Room Facilities

You stay in local teahouses with twin-bedded rooms, foam mattresses and shared facilities in most villages. Rooms are unheated; the dining room heater provides the main warmth in the evening.

In Tsum Valley, lodges are simpler than those on popular routes. At Mu Gompa, options are very basic, occasionally including simple dorm-style space if private rooms are unavailable. Dharmasala before the pass has basic stone rooms or tents with communal dining.

Single rooms cannot be guaranteed, particularly during peak seasons in small settlements or at Dharmasala. Where single rooms are unavailable, trekkers share with another group member of the same gender.

Food and Dietary Requirements

Meals are prepared from local lodge menus, featuring dal bhat, noodle dishes, soups, potatoes and porridge. Vegetarian options are widely available and often preferable above Namrung, where fresh meat is scarce.

Tsum Valley has a cultural commitment to non-violence; meat is neither produced nor served in much of the valley. Plan for predominantly vegetarian meals while in Tsum.

Inform us of severe allergies or strict dietary requirements when booking. We will advise what lodges can accommodate and what supplemental food you should bring.

Drinking Water

Avoid untreated stream or tap water. Purchase boiled water from lodges, or treat tap water with purification tablets, drops or a reliable filtration system.

Bring at least two reusable water bottles or a hydration bladder with an insulated tube to prevent freezing at higher camps. Avoid relying on single-use plastic bottles, which create litter in fragile mountain valleys.

Guide and Porter Support

A licensed, English-speaking trekking guide accompanies your group throughout the route. The guide oversees pacing, monitors altitude health, manages lodge arrangements and coordinates transport.

Porters carry main duffels (up to 10–12 kg per trekker, typically sharing one porter between two trekkers). Porters walk ahead to ensure luggage arrives at the night's stop. Trekkers carry a daypack with water, warm layers, camera and valuables.

Showers, Charging and Communication

Hot showers (gas or solar) are available in some lower lodges for an additional charge (typically 300–600 NPR). At higher camps, hot water may be provided in a bucket, or unavailable during freezing weather.

Device charging is available in dining rooms for a fee (200–500 NPR per charge or power bank). Charging speeds are slow, and solar systems may fail during overcast weather. Bring a high-capacity power bank and keep electronics warm inside your sleeping bag at night.

Mobile coverage (NTC and Ncell) is patchy and disappears entirely in parts of Tsum Valley and above Samdo. Some lodges offer paid Wi-Fi cards, but connections are slow and intermittent. Prepare for days without reliable digital communication.

Permits and Documents

This combined route requires multiple permits: the Manaslu Restricted Area Permit (RAP), the Tsum Valley Restricted Area Permit (RAP), the Manaslu Conservation Area Project (MCAP) permit, and the Annapurna Conservation Area Project (ACAP) permit for the descent from Dharapani to Besisahar.

Both Manaslu and Tsum Valley are government-designated restricted areas. Regulations require a minimum of two trekkers accompanied by a licensed Nepali guide; independent trekking is prohibited. Igloo arranges all permits in Kathmandu before departure.

Provide a clear passport copy (valid for at least 6 months) and digital passport-sized photographs upon booking to ensure permits are processed promptly.

Road Delays and Extra Time

The drive between Kathmandu and Machha Khola, and the return from Dharapani to Besisahar and Kathmandu, involves unpaved mountain roads subject to landslides, roadworks and slow traffic. Travel times can exceed estimates.

We strongly recommend planning at least one or two buffer days in Kathmandu at the end of the trip before your international flight home to accommodate any unforeseen road or trail delays.

How Weather Changes Along the Route

Elevation gradients & trail exposure

Walking beside the Budhi Gandaki can feel warm in sunshine. Conditions become colder in upper Tsum and beyond Samagaun, especially at Dharmasala, before sunrise and around the Larkya La crossing.

During spring and autumn, nights at Dharmasala can fall below freezing. Wind makes exposed ground feel colder, and snow can linger near the pass. Carry warm layers, weather protection and suitable footwear for changing trail conditions throughout the journey.

Carry warm layers, sun protection, and reliable weather protection accessible in your daypack throughout the route.

Which Season Should You Choose?

Optimal timing for trek priorities

Choose spring for warming lower-valley days and flowering forests. Choose autumn, particularly October or November, if mountain visibility is your priority. Both require preparation for cold and snow higher up.

Winter and monsoon need greater flexibility and current route information.

Manaslu Tsum Valley Trek FAQs

Find clear answers regarding restricted permits, valley trails, high-pass crossings, and acclimatisation for this 20-day Himalayan expedition.

Sanctuary & Circuit Route

Can I do this trek without previous trekking experience?

Previous trekking helps because this programme combines long days, basic facilities and a high pass. Build endurance before departure and discuss your experience with the company. The route needs fitness, preparation for altitude and a realistic walking plan.

How long is the return from Tsum?

The supplied programme links Ranchen Gompa directly with Deng. This is an extended return, and its route, walking time and overnight plan require confirmation. Agree the arrangements before booking, allowing the team to assess access and realistic daily distances.

Are acclimatisation days complete rest days?

Day 14 includes a short walk around Samagaun followed by another night in the village. The direction and turnaround depend on health and conditions. This programme keeps the acclimatisation outing short before tomorrow’s higher stage.

How does this differ from the Tsum Valley Trek?

The Tsum Valley Trek focuses on the valley’s villages and monasteries before returning south. This combined programme also adds the Manaslu Circuit through Namrung, Lho, Samagaun and Samdo, followed by the Larkya La crossing and descent towards Bimthang and Dharapani.

Which permits are needed for this combined trek?

The route needs Manaslu and Tsum restricted-area permits plus conservation-area entry for Manaslu and Annapurna. Igloo’s published inclusions name Manaslu restricted entry, MCAP and ACAP. Confirm the separate Tsum permit coverage and application arrangements before booking.

Do I need to carry my main trekking bag?

One porter is shared between two trekkers and carries main luggage. You carry your daypack with water, layers, medicines and essentials. Confirm the luggage allowance before departure and keep your passport and valuables with you.

Do I need to bring a sleeping bag and down jacket?

Bring your sleeping bag, down jacket and main luggage bag. Any loan or rental arrangement needs confirmation in your quotation. Pack personal boots, clothing, toiletries and electronics, and check equipment suitability for cold nights before leaving Kathmandu.

Can I book as a solo traveller?

A starting rate is published for one person. Send your dates so the company can confirm permit arrangements, guide support and the departure options. Your quotation sets out the services and price for your booking.

What is the highest altitude on this itinerary?

Larkya La is the highest point at approximately 5,160 metres. Dharmasala, around 4,460 metres, is the highest overnight stop before the crossing. Mu Gompa, approximately 3,700 metres, is the highest overnight point within the Tsum section.

Is insurance covering trekking to 5,000 metres sufficient?

A 5,000-metre limit falls below the planned Larkya La height of approximately 5,160 metres. Ask your insurer to cover the full trekking route, emergency helicopter evacuation and necessary medical treatment, including relevant altitude limits, exclusions and assistance procedures.

Does being fit protect me from altitude sickness?

Fitness helps with sustained walking, but it does not prevent altitude illness. Fit and experienced trekkers can also become unwell. Follow the guide’s assessment and report any symptoms promptly, especially during climbs towards upper Tsum, Samdo and Dharmasala.

What should I do if I feel unwell at altitude?

Tell your guide promptly about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not ascend to a higher sleeping altitude while experiencing symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite rest at the same elevation, descent is necessary.
