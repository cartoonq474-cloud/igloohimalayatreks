Mera Peak Climbing (18 Days)

Approach Mera Peak through Paiya, Panggom and the Hinku Valley on our eighteen-day trekking and climbing journey. Walk through forests to Kothe, Thaknak and Khare, where an acclimatisation and training day prepares you for the glacier route. Continue to Mera High Camp before a summit attempt, with a contingency day available if conditions delay the climb. Return through Kothe and Thuli Kharka, crossing Zatrwa La before reaching Lukla. The programme includes Kathmandu sightseeing, preparation and return flights.

Mera Peak Climbing Trip Overview

Our eighteen-day Mera Peak Climbing trip approaches the mountain through the Hinku Valley, with a summit attempt after acclimatisation and training in Khare. The route combines village paths and forest walking with glacier travel above the lodges. Mera is published at 6,476 m, although the intended summit must be confirmed before booking your climbing programme.

After sightseeing and equipment checks in Kathmandu, you fly to Lukla and walk south towards Paiya. Continue through Panggom and Ningsow before following forest trails to Chhatra Khola. The approach includes repeated climbs and descents, rather than a continuous uphill walk. Beyond Kothe, the Hinku Valley opens towards Thaknak and Khare, bringing closer mountain views and colder nights as the route gains height above the lower forested slopes.

In Khare, you spend another night at the same elevation while practising with climbing equipment. The next stage crosses onto the glacier and continues to Mera High Camp. An early summit attempt follows, with a descent back to Khare. The contingency day provides flexibility if weather or climbing conditions affect the attempt and return journey.

This is a climbing trip with long days at altitude. You need strong fitness, previous trekking experience and preparation for crampons, ropes and an ice axe. Training in Khare helps you practise these skills, while the guide assesses conditions and the group’s progress. A summit attempt depends on health and weather; reaching the top can never be guaranteed in advance.

You stay in Kathmandu hotels and mountain lodges, with tented accommodation at High Camp. The return crosses Zatrwa La before reaching Lukla for your flight back to Kathmandu.

Mera Peak Climbing Trip Itinerary — Day by Day

Our eighteen-day itinerary includes Kathmandu preparation, the Hinku Valley approach, Khare training and a contingency day. Times and distances are approximate; health, weather and glacier conditions can change the daily programme and summit plans.

Day 1: Arrival in Kathmandu

Arrival & Airport Transfer — Kathmandu, around 1,400 m — 3-Star Hotel Check-in

MAIN ACTIVITY Arrival and airport transfer
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 1,400 m
ACCOMMODATION 3-star hotel in Kathmandu; room sharing as quoted
MEALS Arrival-day meals at your own expense

Welcome to Nepal. Our representative meets you at Tribhuvan International Airport and takes you to your hotel. After checking in, we discuss the climbing programme, preparations and any equipment questions you have before your journey to Mera.

Depending on your arrival time, you can rest after the flight or take a walk nearby. Keep your travel documents and climbing equipment ready for tomorrow’s sightseeing and preparation day in Kathmandu.

Day 2: Kathmandu Sightseeing and Trek Preparation

Cultural Sightseeing & Gear Inspection — Kathmandu, around 1,400 m — Expedition Briefing

MAIN ACTIVITY Kathmandu sightseeing and final climbing preparations
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 1,400 m
ACCOMMODATION 3-star hotel in Kathmandu
MEALS Breakfast; other city meals at your own expense

Today combines Kathmandu sightseeing with final preparations for the mountain journey. Planned visits include Swayambhunath, Boudhanath and Pashupatinath, introducing the valley’s Buddhist and Hindu heritage before the climb.

Later, review your personal climbing gear with the team. Check boot and crampon compatibility, clothing and equipment fit, and confirm your luggage allowance. Prepare for tomorrow’s early departure, with the flight airport and transfer time confirmed by our team.

Day 3: Fly to Lukla and Trek to Paiya

Scenic Flight to Lukla & Approach Trek — Paiya, approximately 2,730 m — 5 hrs Trek (7 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 5 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 7 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 2,730 m
ACCOMMODATION Lodge in Paiya
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

The journey begins with a flight to Lukla at approximately 2,860 m. Seasonal airline operations may require departure from Manthali Airport in Ramechhap, reached by road from Kathmandu before flying.

After landing, meet the mountain team and start towards Paiya. The route heads away from the main Namche trail, following forested slopes with climbs, descents and bridge crossings. Today introduces the walking rhythm of the lower approach, where changes in height continue throughout the day. Check into your lodge in Paiya for your first night.

Day 4: Trek from Paiya to Panggom

Forest Ridge Trail & Valley Views — Panggom, approximately 2,846 m — 6 hrs Trek (7–8 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 6 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 7–8 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 2,846 m
ACCOMMODATION Lodge in Panggom
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We leave Paiya on a trail that descends before climbing through forest. Watch your footing on damp or muddy sections, and follow the guide across the changing ground.

The approach continues over wooded hillsides towards Panggom. Breaks give you time to drink and adjust layers as the temperature changes between shaded paths and open sections. On arrival, settle into your lodge and prepare for another day of mixed climbing and descending. The route continues towards Ningsow tomorrow, still below the higher Hinku Valley camps.

Day 5: Trek from Panggom to Ningsow

Wooded Slopes & Rhododendron Trails — Ningsow, approximately 2,863 m — 5–6 hrs Trek (9 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 5–6 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 9 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 2,863 m
ACCOMMODATION Lodge in Ningsow
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Leaving Panggom, the trail rises before dropping through wooded slopes. The day combines climbs and descents, so similar overnight elevations do not mean an entirely level walk between the two villages.

Rhododendron forest lines parts of the route, with mountain views possible where the trees open. We continue towards Ningsow at a steady pace and stop for the night. Use the remaining afternoon to dry clothing, refill water and rest before tomorrow’s longer forest stage towards Chhatra Khola in the valley.

Day 6: Trek from Ningsow to Chhatra Khola

Wilderness Trails towards Hinku Valley — Chhatra Khola, approximately 2,800 m — 7 hrs Trek (10 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 7 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 10 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 2,800 m
ACCOMMODATION Lodge in Chhatra Khola
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Today is a longer stage through forested country towards Chhatra Khola. The paths cross streams and uneven ground as the route moves towards the Hinku Valley, with shaded sections that can stay damp after rainfall.

Keep water and a rain layer within reach, and take care on roots, stones and narrow paths. The walking requires attention even before the higher elevations. After reaching Chhatra Khola, we check into the lodge and rest for the onward journey towards Kothe tomorrow.

Day 7: Trek from Chhatra Khola to Kothe

Ascend Hinku River Valley — Kothe, approximately 3,691 m — 6 hrs Trek (10–11 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 6 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 10–11 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 3,691 m
ACCOMMODATION Lodge in Kothe
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We continue towards Kothe through the lower Hinku Valley. Forested paths follow the river corridor, with bamboo and pine along sections of the approach. The route gains height today, bringing a substantial increase from last night’s sleeping elevation.

Your guide monitors the pace and how you feel as we move higher. Tell the team about headaches, nausea or unusual fatigue. On arrival in Kothe, rest at the lodge and prepare for the next stage towards Thaknak tomorrow morning.

Day 8: Trek from Kothe to Thaknak

Alpine Valley & Glacial Moraine — Thaknak, approximately 4,358 m — 4–5 hrs Trek (7 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 4–5 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 7 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 4,358 m
ACCOMMODATION Lodge in Thaknak
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Beyond Kothe, we continue along the Hinku Valley towards Thaknak. The trees thin as the route enters more open ground, with river views and rocky sections beneath the surrounding mountains ahead.

Although the stage is shorter than yesterday, the higher elevation increases the effort required. Walk slowly and tell your guide how you are feeling. We spend the night in Thaknak, also written as Tangnag, before continuing towards Khare. Weather and the group’s health determine how the onward plan is managed.

Day 9: Trek from Thaknak to Khare

Approach Mera Base Camp — Khare, approximately 5,045 m — 3–4 hrs Trek (7 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 3–4 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 7 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 5,045 m
ACCOMMODATION Lodge in Khare
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We leave Thaknak and follow the valley towards Khare, passing ground shaped by the Dig Glacier. The path climbs more steeply as the route approaches the lodges, with views of Mera’s northern slopes when the weather is clear.

Khare is our base for acclimatisation and equipment practice. After settling into the lodge, rest and prepare your climbing kit. Tomorrow’s programme stays at the same elevation, allowing time for training before moving towards the glacier and Mera High Camp.

Day 10: Acclimatisation and Training in Khare

Technical Glacier Skills & Acclimatisation — Khare, approximately 5,045 m — Climbing Practice

MAIN ACTIVITY Acclimatisation, equipment checks and climbing practice
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 5,045 m
ACCOMMODATION Lodge in Khare
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We remain in Khare for acclimatisation and practical climbing preparation. Your guide checks the equipment and leads a training session on using crampons, ropes and an ice axe, with the location chosen according to conditions.

Use this time to practise movements and discuss anything unfamiliar. Training helps you prepare, while your health and ability still determine whether you continue higher. Repack for High Camp, keep essential layers accessible and rest before tomorrow’s glacier stage begins.

Day 11: Climb from Khare to Mera High Camp

Glacier Ascent via Mera La — Mera High Camp, approximately 5,780 m — 6–8 hrs (6 km)

CLIMBING TIME Approximately 6–8 hours; glacier conditions and pace affect timing
ROUTE DISTANCE Approximately 6 km; the glacier route may vary
TERRAIN Glacier, snow and rocky ground
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 5,780 m
ACCOMMODATION Tented camp at Mera High Camp
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

The route from Khare leads towards Mera La and onto the glacier before continuing to High Camp. Wear the climbing equipment required by your guide and follow the team’s rope arrangements. Snow, ice and visibility influence progress throughout this ascent.

High Camp sits above Khare, making this a substantial rise in sleeping altitude. Your guide assesses whether conditions and health allow the move. At camp, organise your equipment and rest for the early start. The climb proceeds only when the team considers it appropriate.

Day 12: Summit Attempt on Mera Peak and Return to Khare

Summit Push (6,476 m) & Descent — Mera Peak Summit (6,476 m) / Khare (5,045 m) — 10–15 hrs

CLIMBING TIME Allow approximately 10–15 hours; the published itinerary estimates 10–12 hours
TOTAL ROUTE DISTANCE Approximately 12–13 km; snow and route conditions may change this
HIGHEST POINT Mera Peak, published as 6,476 m; confirm the intended summit before booking
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Khare, approximately 5,045 m
ACCOMMODATION Lodge in Khare, if the planned descent is completed
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We begin before dawn, following the climbing guide across snow and glacier terrain. The ascent requires steady movement with crampons and ropes, while cold and altitude make progress demanding.

The final approach may include a steeper section, with rope protection arranged for the conditions. Your guide decides whether to continue, pause or turn back. Clear weather can reveal Everest, Lhotse, Makalu, Cho Oyu and Kanchenjunga.

After the attempt, descend carefully to High Camp and continue towards Khare. Keep enough energy for the return; the day ends only when the descent is completed.

Day 13: Contingency Day

Weather & Route Reserve Buffer — Khare, approximately 5,045 m — Contingency Day

MAIN ACTIVITY Reserve time for weather or climbing delays
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Normally Khare, approximately 5,045 m; depends on use of the reserve day
ACCOMMODATION Lodge in Khare or tented camp if the climbing plan requires it
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

This day provides flexibility if weather or climbing conditions delay the summit attempt. Your guide decides how to use it, considering the group’s health, route conditions and the time required for a careful descent.

If the attempt and descent run to plan, the day can be used for rest in Khare. If the schedule changes, the overnight location may also change. Confirm any additional services or costs before extending beyond the agreed programme for your trip.

Day 14: Trek from Khare to Kothe

Descend Hinku Valley — Kothe, approximately 3,691 m — 6 hrs Trek (11 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 6 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 11 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 3,691 m
ACCOMMODATION Lodge in Kothe
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We leave Khare and follow the valley back through Thaknak towards Kothe. The route loses height, bringing you from the open upper valley towards the lower forested sections again.

The descent still requires careful footing on loose stones and uneven paths. Take breaks and keep a steady pace as tired legs recover from the climbing stage. After reaching Kothe, settle into the lodge. Tomorrow’s route turns towards Thuli Kharka, beginning the return across the hills towards Lukla for departure.

Day 15: Trek from Kothe to Thuli Kharka

Uphill Climb through Juniper Ridges — Thuli Kharka, approximately 4,300 m — 6–7 hrs Trek (10–11 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 6–7 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 10–11 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 4,300 m
ACCOMMODATION Lodge in Thuli Kharka
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We leave the lower Hinku Valley route and climb towards Thuli Kharka. Forested sections lead onto higher ground, with rhododendron and juniper along parts of the hillside as the path rises away from the river.

The climb brings another gain in sleeping altitude, so keep a measured pace despite the recent descent. On arrival, rest and organise warm clothing for tomorrow’s crossing of Zatrwa La. Conditions on the pass can be colder and windier than those around the lodge.

Day 16: Trek to Lukla via Zatrwa La Pass

Cross Zatrwa La Pass (4,610 m) & Descend — Zatrwa La (4,610 m) / Lukla (2,860 m) — 7–8 hrs (13 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 7–8 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 13 km
HIGHEST POINT Zatrwa La Pass, approximately 4,610 m
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 2,860 m
ACCOMMODATION Lodge in Lukla
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Our final trekking stage climbs towards Zatrwa La before descending to Lukla. The pass reaches approximately 4,610 m, with snow or ice possible according to recent weather and season of travel.

Follow the guide across the pass and take care on the descent. The trail drops towards greener slopes and the lodges of Lukla, ending the circuit. After checking in, organise your bags for tomorrow’s flight and confirm departure arrangements with the team before the final mountain night of this trip.

Day 17: Fly from Lukla and Return to Kathmandu

Mountain Flight to Kathmandu & Farewell Dinner — Kathmandu, approximately 1,400 m — Celebration

MAIN ACTIVITY Lukla flight to Kathmandu, or Manthali followed by road travel
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 1,400 m
ACCOMMODATION 3-star hotel in Kathmandu
MEALS Breakfast and farewell dinner; lunch at your own expense

We prepare for the return flight from Lukla. Depending on airline operations, you may fly directly to Kathmandu or land at Manthali and continue by road to Kathmandu.

After reaching your hotel, the remaining time is available for rest or shopping nearby. We meet for a farewell dinner in Kathmandu. Flight timing depends on weather and airline operations, so keep onward travel plans flexible around possible delays.

Day 18: Farewell and Airport Transfer

Departure & Airport Transfer — Kathmandu, approximately 1,400 m — Final Departure

MAIN ACTIVITY International departure and airport transfer
MEALS Breakfast

Your eighteen-day Mera Peak journey ends today. After breakfast, prepare for your onward flight. Depending on your departure time, there may be an opportunity for a final walk nearby before leaving your hotel in Kathmandu.

Our representative accompanies you to Tribhuvan International Airport for your departure.

Read Before You Book

Before booking your Mera Peak climbing trip, consider the walking, glacier travel, altitude and camping. These details help you decide whether the eighteen-day programme suits your experience and personal preparation for climbing.

If you have questions or need personalised advice, feel free to email or WhatsApp our expedition team directly.

1. Climb Difficulty and Fitness

Challenging peak climb with sustained effort above 6,000 m.

Mera Peak requires long trekking days followed by glacier travel with crampons, ropes and an ice axe. Summit day can last ten to fifteen hours, including the descent. Fitness and equipment skills are essential, even where the slope appears gentle. Cold, altitude and fatigue increase effort.

Previous trekking experience is important. Discuss your climbing background with us before booking, including experience wearing crampons and moving on snow with ropes.

Begin preparing several months beforehand, allowing longer if you need to build fitness or learn climbing skills. Combine hill walking, endurance work and strength exercises. Practise with your pack and arrange suitable instruction before the mountain journey begins.

2. Altitude and Acclimatisation

Acclimatisation schedule with Khare training and reserve days.

Day 10 provides acclimatisation and training in Khare. Moving to High Camp still brings a large rise in sleeping altitude; your guide must assess your health before you continue.

Tell your guide immediately about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not continue to a higher sleeping altitude while experiencing symptoms of altitude illness. Worsening symptoms or severe breathlessness require urgent assessment and descent. The summit attempt must remain secondary to your health and recovery.

3. Medical Conditions and Travel Insurance

Mandatory mountaineering insurance with helicopter evacuation above 6,000 m.

If you have an existing medical condition, seek advice from a healthcare professional familiar with high-altitude travel before booking. Discuss your planned route and any medicines you take.

Travel insurance must cover mountaineering with ropes and crampons above 6,000 metres, including the intended summit elevation, emergency helicopter evacuation and medical treatment. Confirm cover for this exact itinerary with your insurer. Carry policy details and check exclusions, excess charges and assistance procedures.

4. Accommodation and Room Facilities

3-star Kathmandu hotels, mountain lodges, and High Camp tents.

The package includes three nights in a three-star Kathmandu hotel, mountain lodges during trekking and tented accommodation at High Camp. Lodge rooms generally have shared bathrooms; confirm room and tent allocation.

At High Camp, expect a cold tented night with fewer facilities than the lodges. Confirm the supplied camping equipment and sleeping bag rating before departure. Private rooms and tents depend on arrangements and availability throughout the programme.

5. Food and Dietary Requirements

Hearty mountain meals, seasonal fruit, and climbing rations.

Trekking and climbing meals are included, with seasonal fruit after trekking dinners. Tea, coffee and boiled water are supplied on climbing days.

Tell us about allergies or dietary requirements before booking so we can discuss what can be arranged along the route. Explain any strict requirements clearly, including concerns about shared cooking equipment.

6. Drinking Water

Boiled and treated drinking water protocols along the Hinku route.

Carry reusable bottles and drink boiled or appropriately treated water. Water from a clear stream or an untreated tap may still contain germs.

Boiling is the most reliable way to kill germs. If boiling is unavailable, filtration followed by disinfection offers broader protection than many portable filters alone. Follow the instructions for your treatment equipment or tablets.

7. Guide and Porter Support

Licensed high-altitude climbing guides and dedicated porter crew.

Government-licensed, English-speaking trekking and climbing guides support the programme, with porters carrying agreed luggage. You carry essentials. Confirm climbing supervision, staffing and baggage allowances before departure, including porter allocation and summit day support.

8. Showers, Charging and Communication

Lodge facilities, cold-weather battery management, and mobile connectivity.

Hot showers cost extra outside Lukla; confirm Lukla arrangements in your quotation. Wi-Fi and device charging are also excluded. Availability and connections vary along the route, with limited facilities at High Camp. Carry spare batteries and tell family that contact may be unavailable on climbing days.

9. Permits and Documents

NMA Mera Peak permit, Makalu Barun National Park & Khumbu permits.

We arrange the Mera Peak climbing permit, Makalu Barun National Park entry and Khumbu municipality permit. Carry your passport, climbing insurance details and copies of important documents. Your Nepal visa remains your responsibility before entering Nepal.

10. Flight Delays and Extra Time

Lukla flight weather buffers and Ramechhap seasonal routing.

Lukla flights can be delayed or cancelled because of weather. During peak periods, flights may use Manthali Airport in Ramechhap, requiring a road transfer.

Day 13 provides climbing contingency, with no separate Kathmandu flight buffer. Allow flexibility around onward travel and discuss costs if delays extend the programme.

Weather and Best Time for the Mera Peak Climbing Trip

Spring, from March to May, and autumn, from September to November, are the main seasons for Mera Peak climbing. Lower trails and glacier camps have different conditions, while wind, snowfall and visibility can affect the summit attempt in either season despite generally favourable periods.

Seasonal Weather Conditions

Year-round trail climate breakdown

Spring

March–May
PEAK SEASON

Lower forest trails gradually warm, with rhododendrons possible along the approach. March can retain winter snow and severe cold higher up. Later spring brings longer daylight, but cloud, wind and snowfall can still affect the glacier route and summit attempt.

Autumn

September–November
BEST VISIBILITY

Early September may retain monsoon rain and cloud. Later autumn often offers clearer mountain views, with colder nights along the approach and at High Camp. Snowfall and wind remain possible during climbing.

Winter

December–February
QUIET & COLD

Expect severe cold, short daylight and possible snow or ice on approach paths and Zatrwa La. High Camp and summit conditions require careful assessment. Clear skies alone do not make winter climbing suitable.

Monsoon

June–August, sometimes extending into September
LOW SEASON / RAIN

Rain and cloud affect lower trails, mountain visibility and Lukla flights. Snowfall higher up and poor visibility can disrupt glacier travel. Discuss conditions and operating feasibility before planning a climb during the monsoon months.

These are seasonal patterns; conditions can vary from year to year and during an individual climb.

How Weather Changes Along the Route

The forest approach can feel warm in sunshine, while Khare and High Camp are much colder. The summit attempt begins before dawn, when low temperatures and wind increase exposure considerably.

Even during spring and autumn, temperatures at High Camp can fall well below freezing. Wind and slow movement on the glacier can increase cold exposure. Keep suitable insulated layers and hand protection accessible, with equipment checked before leaving Khare.

Which Season Should You Choose?

Spring Recommendation: Mild daylight temperatures, blooming rhododendrons, and active wildlife across lower approach valleys.
Autumn Recommendation: Crystal-clear post-monsoon atmospheric clarity, sharp panoramic views, and stable high-altitude conditions.

Choose spring for gradually warming lower trails and longer daylight. Choose autumn, particularly October or November, for generally clearer mountain visibility. Conditions still determine the climb; winter and monsoon require specialist planning and greater flexibility around weather and route access.

Mera Peak Climbing FAQs

Climbing & Route

Can I attempt Mera without any previous climbing experience?

Discuss your experience with us before booking. Strong trekking fitness and familiarity with climbing equipment are important. Khare training provides practice with crampons, ropes and an ice axe, but cannot replace all the preparation needed for this climb.

What is the highest altitude on this itinerary?

The package publishes Mera Peak at 6,476 metres. Mera has several summits, so confirm the intended summit before booking. Your highest planned overnight stop is Mera High Camp at 5,780 metres, followed by descent to Khare.

Can I see Mount Everest from Mera Peak?

Clear weather can reveal Everest, Lhotse, Makalu, Cho Oyu and Kanchenjunga. Visibility depends on cloud and conditions on the day. Your guide decides whether the climb can proceed; views and reaching the summit are not guaranteed.

Is training a complete rest day?

Day 10 combines acclimatisation with equipment checks and climbing instruction. You practise using crampons, ropes and an ice axe. The session depends on conditions and your health. You stay in Khare overnight before continuing higher.

Do we use tents during the climb?

Yes. The planned High Camp night uses tented accommodation, while trekking nights are normally in lodges. Weather or the contingency day can change this pattern. Confirm tent sharing, supplied camping equipment and any additional nights before finalising your quotation.

How does the contingency day work around the summit?

Day 13 provides flexibility if weather or climbing conditions delay the planned attempt. Its use and overnight location depend on the situation. It does not guarantee another attempt or remove the need for extra travel time after Lukla flight delays.

Is ordinary trekking insurance sufficient for this climb?

Your policy must cover mountaineering with ropes and crampons above 6,000 metres, the intended summit elevation, emergency helicopter evacuation and medical treatment. Confirm the itinerary with your insurer, including exclusions and how to obtain assistance during the climb.

Does being fit protect me from altitude sickness?

No. Fitness helps with physical effort but does not prevent altitude illness. The route reaches high sleeping elevations and then climbs higher. Follow your guide’s assessment, report symptoms immediately and avoid continuing upward because you feel physically strong.

What should I do if I feel unwell at altitude?

Tell your guide immediately about headache, nausea, dizziness or fatigue. Stop further ascent while symptoms persist. Worsening symptoms, confusion or breathlessness at rest require urgent assessment and descent. Never attempt the summit just to stay on schedule.

Do I need to carry my main trekking bag?

Porters carry agreed trekking luggage, while you carry water, layers and daily essentials in a daypack. Confirm baggage allowances and who carries climbing equipment above Khare, since arrangements are not detailed in the published package.

Do I need to bring a sleeping bag and down jacket?

A sleeping bag, down jacket and duffel bag are provided for trip use. Confirm warmth, fit and return arrangements. Personal climbing items such as compatible boots, crampons, harness and helmet need separate confirmation before you leave Kathmandu.

Can I book as a solo traveller?

The starting price for one person is USD 2,855. Send your preferred dates and climbing experience so we can confirm availability, staffing, accommodation arrangements and the final quotation. A solo booking includes guided climbing support.
