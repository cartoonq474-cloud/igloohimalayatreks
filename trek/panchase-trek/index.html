Panchase Trek (8 Days)

Walk through the villages and forests above Pokhara on our eight-day Panchase Trek. You stay in Bhumdi and Panchase Bhanjyang before an early climb to Panchase View Point, around 2,500 m, for mountain views in clear weather. The route continues through Bhadaure to Naudanda, followed by a drive back to Pokhara. With four walking days, village lodges and tourist bus journeys between Kathmandu and Pokhara, expect uphill paths, stone steps and downhill stretches between the overnight stops. The Panchase Trek is an ideal introduction to Himalayan village walking, offering rich birdlife, tranquil sacred forests, and sweeping Himalayan panoramas from an accessible ridge west of Pokhara. Perfect for trekkers seeking genuine village hospitality, cultural warmth, and natural beauty without extreme high-altitude hazards.

Panchase Trek Overview

Our eight-day Panchase Trek explores the forested hills west of Pokhara, with four walking days linking Bhumdi, Panchase Bhanjyang, Bhadaure and Naudanda. An early hike to Panchase View Point, around 2,500 m, is the highest part of the route. Clear weather offers views towards Annapurna, Dhaulagiri and Machhapuchhre, with village lodges providing your overnight accommodation.

After arriving in Kathmandu, you travel to Pokhara by tourist bus and stay there before the walking begins. A short local drive brings you to the starting trail towards Bhumdi. Paths climb through farmland, wooded slopes and village areas above the Pokhara Valley. Views of Phewa Lake and the surrounding hills appear along parts of the approach, while Bhumdi provides your first overnight stay in a village lodge.

From Bhumdi, the route continues through forest towards Panchase Bhanjyang. Rhododendron and oak trees cover sections of the trail, with clearings offering mountain views when conditions allow. The walk includes uphill stretches and uneven ground. At around 2,100 m, Panchase Bhanjyang is your highest overnight stop and the starting point for the following morning’s hike.

We make an early climb to Panchase View Point before descending towards Bhadaure. The viewpoint can offer a broad mountain outlook at sunrise, although cloud may hide the peaks. Stone steps, forest paths and terraced slopes form part of the descent. After a night in Bhadaure, you walk to Naudanda and return by road to Pokhara, completing the trekking section.

Three village nights and four city hotel nights complete the programme. Tourist buses connect Kathmandu and Pokhara in both directions, with departure from Kathmandu on the eighth day.

Read Before You Book

Before choosing this Panchase itinerary, consider the four walking days, hill terrain, lodge facilities and road journeys. These details help you prepare and decide whether the programme suits your fitness and expectations.

1. Trek Difficulty and Fitness

The Panchase Trek is easy to moderate, with four walking days on uphill paths, steps and uneven ground. The forest stage and viewpoint day require several hours of walking, followed by descents. Prepare for repeated climbs and downhill sections, especially if you have limited walking experience.

Previous hiking experience is helpful. First-time trekkers should build endurance before departure and become comfortable walking uphill and downhill regularly with a daypack.

Prepare with regular walks and hill hikes before departure, allowing longer if you have limited walking experience. Include simple strength exercises, practise in your trekking boots and become comfortable descending steps with a daypack before beginning the trek.

2. Altitude and Acclimatisation

The highest overnight stop is Panchase Bhanjyang, around 2,100 m. You visit the viewpoint around 2,500 m before descending. No separate acclimatisation day is scheduled within this hill itinerary.

The lower elevations reduce the altitude demands compared with higher mountain treks, but they do not guarantee freedom from illness. Report headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue to your guide. Avoid ascent with altitude illness, and descend if symptoms worsen despite rest at the same elevation.

3. Medical Conditions and Travel Insurance

If you have an existing medical condition, seek advice from a healthcare professional before booking. Discuss the walking terrain, planned elevations, trekking days and any medicines you take.

Travel insurance is strongly recommended and excluded from the price. Choose cover suitable for trekking to approximately 2,500 metres, medical treatment and emergency evacuation in Nepal. Check exclusions, excess charges and the insurer’s assistance procedures. Carry policy details and emergency contacts while travelling.

4. Accommodation and Room Facilities

Your programme includes two Kathmandu hotel nights, two in Pokhara and three lodge nights. Bhumdi, Panchase Bhanjyang and Bhadaure each have one overnight stay during the walking section of this itinerary.

Village lodges generally have twin-sharing rooms, simple furnishings and shared bathrooms. Confirm the bedding arrangements before packing, and bring sleeping equipment suitable for your season. Private rooms and specific bathroom facilities depend on availability at each lodge.

5. Food and Dietary Requirements

Breakfast, lunch and dinner are included during the village trek, with tea or coffee. Lodge menus commonly offer dal bhat and noodles.

Tell us about allergies or dietary requirements before booking so we can discuss what can be arranged along the route. Explain strict requirements clearly, including any concerns about shared cooking equipment.

6. Drinking Water

Carry reusable bottles and drink boiled or appropriately treated water. Stream water and untreated tap water can still contain germs that cause illness.

Boiling is the most reliable way to kill germs. If boiling is unavailable, filtration followed by disinfection offers broader protection than many portable filters alone. Follow the instructions for your water-treatment equipment or tablets.

7. Guide and Porter Support

Your package includes an experienced guide and one porter for every two trekkers. Confirm baggage limits and the solo porter arrangement before packing. Carry water, weather protection, medicines and essentials in your daypack.

8. Showers, Charging and Communication

Hot showers, Wi-Fi and device charging may cost extra, with availability varying between lodges. Mobile coverage and internet connections can be unreliable in wooded hills. Carry a charged power bank and let family know that contact may be limited. Ask about facilities at each overnight stop.

9. Permits and Documents

Before departure, ask us to confirm any applicable entry permits, trekking documents and charges for this Panchase route. Carry your valid passport, insurance details and document copies. Your Nepal visa remains your responsibility throughout the trip.

10. Travel Delays and Extra Time

Rain can make forest paths slippery and reduce mountain visibility. Roadworks, traffic and weather can also affect tourist bus journeys between Kathmandu and Pokhara.

Day 7 returns to Kathmandu before departure on Day 8. Keep flexibility in your plans, and discuss arrangements if road disruption delays the return.

Weather and Best Time for the Panchase Trek

Spring, from March to May, and autumn, from September to November, are generally the preferred seasons for Panchase. Both offer opportunities for mountain views and comfortable hill walking, although cloud, rain and cold mornings remain possible around Panchase Bhanjyang and the early viewpoint hike.

Seasonal Weather Conditions

March–May

Spring

Walking days become warmer, with rhododendron flowers possible along forest sections. Mornings at Panchase Bhanjyang can remain cool. Haze or afternoon cloud may reduce mountain visibility, especially later in the season. Carry rain protection.

September–November

Autumn

Early September can retain monsoon rain. October and November generally offer clearer mountain views, with colder mornings and evenings as winter approaches. Cloud and rain remain possible, so sunrise visibility is not guaranteed.

December–February

Winter

Clear days are possible, but the early viewpoint hike can be cold. Shorter daylight and occasional snow or ice require care on steps and shaded paths. Confirm weather, trail access and lodge availability before departure.

June–August (into Sep)

Monsoon

Frequent rain can make forest paths muddy and slippery, with leeches possible in damp areas. Cloud often hides mountain views. Heavy rain and road disruption may affect both walking and tourist bus journeys.

These are seasonal patterns; conditions can vary from year to year and during an individual trek.

How Weather Changes Along the Route

Pokhara and Bhumdi

Pokhara and Bhumdi can feel warm in sunshine. Lower valley trails pass through open farmland where humidity and midday sun are felt.

Forest Trails

Forest paths offer shade under dense rhododendron and oak canopy, keeping daytime walking conditions pleasantly cool and protected.

Panchase Bhanjyang

Panchase Bhanjyang sits on an open ridge at 2,100 m and becomes noticeably cooler after sunset with brisk evening breezes.

Viewpoint Climb

The viewpoint climb at 2,500 m can feel colder before daylight and in wind. Keep a warm layer and waterproof jacket within easy reach.

Which Season Should You Choose?

Choose Spring for Wildflowers

Choose spring (March to May) for warming days and flowering rhododendrons along forest paths.

Choose Autumn for Pure Mountain Clarity

Choose autumn, especially October or November, if crisp mountain visibility towards Annapurna and Dhaulagiri matters most.

Winter for Quiet Trails

Panchase Trek FAQs

General & Route

Can I do this trek without previous trekking experience? ∨

Yes, with suitable fitness and preparation. This itinerary has four walking days, including uphill paths, steps and a longer viewpoint day. Practise consecutive hill walks with a daypack before departure, and discuss your walking experience with us beforehand.

What is the highest altitude on this itinerary? ∨

Panchase View Point, approximately 2,500 metres, is visited during the day. Panchase Bhanjyang, around 2,100 metres, is the highest overnight stop. After the early viewpoint hike, you descend to Bhadaure, around 1,670 metres, for the night.

Can we see the Annapurna mountains from Panchase? ∨

Yes, when visibility is clear. Panchase View Point offers views towards the Annapurna and Dhaulagiri ranges, with Machhapuchhre visible. Cloud and haze can hide the mountains, so neither sunrise colours nor clear views can be guaranteed.

Do we sleep at the Panchase viewpoint? ∨

No. You stay at Panchase Bhanjyang before the early viewpoint hike, then descend to Bhadaure for the next night. The hilltop visit is part of a walking day, with no overnight accommodation planned at the viewpoint in this programme.

Is the Panchase Trek suitable for younger children? ∨

It can suit families whose children are comfortable with several hours of hill walking. The viewpoint day includes a climb and descent. Discuss ages, walking experience and pace with us so we can assess the route and arrangements.

How does Panchase differ from the Poon Hill Trek? ∨

Panchase explores lower hills above Pokhara, reaching approximately 2,500 metres with three lodge nights. Poon Hill reaches around 3,210 metres and follows an Annapurna route through Ghorepani. Compare the days, climbs, accommodation and time available before choosing between these journeys.

Does this trek need acclimatisation days? ∨

No separate acclimatisation day is scheduled. The highest overnight stop is around 2,100 metres, with a daytime hike to approximately 2,500 metres. Walk steadily and report discomfort rather than assuming the lower elevations prevent illness.

What should I do if I feel unwell while trekking? ∨

Tell your guide about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Stop and discuss what to do next. Avoid ascent with altitude illness, and descend if symptoms worsen despite rest at the same elevation. Seek treatment when needed.

Do I need to carry my main trekking bag? ∨

A porter carries your main luggage, with one porter for every two trekkers. Confirm the baggage allowance before packing. You carry a daypack with water, warm layers, medicines, valuables and essentials during the walking day.

Do I need to bring a sleeping bag and warm jacket? ∨

Bring a warm jacket for cool mornings and evenings. Ask about lodge bedding before deciding on a sleeping bag or liner. Pack your own walking gear and discuss any equipment arrangements with us before departure from Kathmandu.

Can I book as a solo traveller? ∨

Yes. The solo rate is from USD 655 per person. Send us your dates so we can confirm availability and the quotation, including room arrangements, porter allocation and any supplement that applies to your departure.

Do I need travel insurance for this trek? ∨

We strongly recommend it. Choose a policy covering the hill trek, medical treatment and emergency evacuation in Nepal. The package excludes insurance, and evacuation coordination does not make rescue costs free. Check activity and altitude exclusions before departure.
