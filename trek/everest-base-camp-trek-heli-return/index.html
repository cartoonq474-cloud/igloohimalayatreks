Everest Base Camp Trek with Helicopter Return (12 Days)

Trek through the Khumbu to Everest Base Camp and Kala Patthar, then return from Gorak Shep to Lukla by helicopter. This twelve-day journey follows the classic approach through Namche Bazaar, Tengboche and Dingboche, with acclimatisation days in Namche and Dingboche before the higher stages. After the Kala Patthar hike, your helicopter shuttle replaces the walk back down the valley. Stay overnight in Lukla before the onward flight and return to Kathmandu, followed by departure the next day.

EBC Helicopter Return Trek Overview

Our twelve-day Everest Base Camp Trek Helicopter Return follows the Khumbu trail to Everest Base Camp at 5,364 m and the Kala Patthar viewpoint. You walk through Namche, Tengboche and Dingboche before reaching Gorak Shep. After the viewpoint hike, a helicopter takes you to Lukla, replacing the return walk through the villages and river valley.

After flying to Lukla, you follow the Dudh Koshi valley through Phakding, crossing suspension bridges before climbing to Namche Bazaar. Beyond Namche, the trail follows the hillside, descends to the river and climbs again to Tengboche. Its Buddhist monastery and views of Ama Dablam make this a memorable stop. Along the route, you pass Sherpa villages where monasteries, prayer wheels and mani walls reflect the community’s Buddhist traditions.

As you gain altitude, forested paths give way to open slopes and increasingly rocky ground. Continue through Dingboche and Lobuche to Gorak Shep, your overnight base for visiting Everest Base Camp. The next morning includes Kala Patthar before a helicopter shuttle to Lukla. Spend the night there before the onward flight and journey to Kathmandu.

We include acclimatisation days in Namche and Dingboche, with shorter hikes and another night at each elevation. The helicopter reduces return walking, while the outward journey still requires consecutive walking days, steep climbs and sustained effort at altitude. Technical climbing skills are not normally required. Prepare for the walking and discuss your experience, health concerns and flight flexibility before departure.

You stay in mountain lodges during the trek and a three-star hotel in Kathmandu. The twelve-day programme includes two Kathmandu nights and nine lodge nights along the route.

Everest Base Camp Trek with Helicopter Return — Day-by-Day Itinerary

Our twelve-day itinerary includes two acclimatisation days, Everest Base Camp and Kala Patthar before the chartered helicopter shuttle directly to Lukla. Walking times and distances are approximate; weather, trail conditions and individual pace can affect daily schedules.

Day 1: Arrival in Kathmandu

Airport Arrival & Hotel Transfer — Kathmandu (1,400 m) — Trip Briefing & Equipment Check

OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 1,400 m
ACCOMMODATION 3-star hotel in Kathmandu; room sharing as quoted
MEALS Arrival-day meals at your own expense

Welcome to Nepal. Our representative meets you at Tribhuvan International Airport and accompanies you to your hotel. After you settle in, we go through the trekking programme, check your essential equipment and discuss any questions you have.

Depending on your arrival time, you can explore the nearby streets of Thamel or rest after your flight. We also help with any final preparations before you leave for the Everest region.

Day 2: Fly to Lukla and Trek to Phakding

Mountain Flight & River Walk — Phakding (2,610 m) — 3–4 hrs (8 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 3–4 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 8 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE 2,610 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Phakding
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Your mountain journey begins with a flight to Lukla at 2,860 m. Depending on airline operations and the season, the flight may depart from Kathmandu or Manthali Airport in Ramechhap.

After landing, you meet your trekking team and begin walking towards Phakding. The trail passes village homes, prayer wheels and forested slopes, with downhill stretches and shorter climbs along the way. Sections follow the Dudh Koshi River, introducing you to the valley you will explore over the coming days. We spend the night in Phakding.

Day 3: Trek from Phakding to Namche Bazaar

Sagarmatha National Park & Hillary Bridge — Namche Bazaar (3,440 m) — 6–7 hrs (10–11 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 6–7 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 10–11 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE 3,440 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Namche Bazaar
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We follow the Dudh Koshi through villages and pine forests, passing Benkar and Monjo before entering Sagarmatha National Park. Your guide handles the permit checks along the route.

Suspension bridges connect the valley trails, followed by a sustained uphill walk towards Namche. This final climb is the most demanding part of the day, so we allow time for breaks. Namche’s shops, lodges and houses spread across the hillside, making it an important trading town and a useful place to spend the next two nights.

Day 4: Acclimatisation Day in Namche Bazaar

Hike to Hotel Everest View — Namche Bazaar (3,440 m) [High Point: 3,880 m] — 3–4 hrs (5–6 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 3–4 hours
HIKE DISTANCE Approximately 5–6 km
DAYTIME HIGH POINT Around 3,880 m if visiting Hotel Everest View
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE 3,440 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Namche Bazaar
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We stay another night in Namche and take a shorter hike above the town. A usual option is the trail towards Hotel Everest View, where clear weather offers views of Everest and nearby peaks.

Your guide chooses the walk according to conditions and how the group is feeling. Afterwards, we return to Namche for the night. The afternoon gives you time to rest, visit the shops or stop at a café before continuing towards Tengboche tomorrow.

Day 5: Trek from Namche Bazaar to Tengboche

Valley Traverses & Monastic Highlands — Tengboche (3,860 m) — 5–6 hrs (10 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 5–6 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 10 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 3,860 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Tengboche
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Leaving Namche, we follow the hillside trail before descending towards Phunki Tenga. Mountain views accompany sections of the walk, while the river marks the low point before the climb to Tengboche.

The uphill trail passes through pine and rhododendron forest. Once we reach the village, you can settle into the teahouse and explore the surroundings. Tengboche’s Buddhist monastery stands against the mountain backdrop of Ama Dablam. If it is open to visitors, your guide explains the visiting customs before you enter.

Day 6: Trek from Tengboche to Dingboche

Pangboche & Imja Valley — Dingboche (4,410 m) — 6–7 hrs (11 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 6–7 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 11 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE 4,410 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Dingboche
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We descend through forest towards Debuche, cross the Imja Khola and continue through Pangboche. As the route gains height, the trees become fewer and the valley opens into slopes with low vegetation and stone buildings.

The trail passes Somare before reaching Dingboche in the Imja Valley. Today brings a noticeable gain in sleeping altitude, so we keep a steady pace throughout the walk. After checking into your teahouse, the remaining afternoon is available for rest before tomorrow’s acclimatisation hike.

Day 7: Acclimatisation Day in Dingboche

Ridge Hike towards Nangkartshang — Dingboche (4,410 m) — 3–4 hrs (4–5 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 3–4 hours
HIKE DISTANCE Approximately 4–5 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE 4,410 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Dingboche
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Our second acclimatisation day includes a hike above Dingboche followed by another night in the village. The usual direction is towards the Nangkartshang viewpoint, with the turnaround point determined by conditions and the group’s progress.

The hillside gives you a broader view of Dingboche and the surrounding valley. We then return to the teahouse, leaving the afternoon for rest and preparation. Tomorrow, the route continues towards Lobuche, where the terrain becomes increasingly rocky and glacial.

Day 8: Trek from Dingboche to Lobuche

Thukla Pass Memorials & Khumbu Moraine — Lobuche (4,940 m) — 5–6 hrs (8 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 5–6 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 8 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 4,940 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Lobuche
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We leave Dingboche and cross open ground towards Thukla. After a break, the trail climbs more steeply to the memorial area above the village. The stone memorials commemorate climbers and Sherpas who lost their lives in the mountains.

Beyond this section, we continue beside the Khumbu Glacier towards Lobuche. Uneven ground and increasing altitude make the walking more demanding, even where the gradient eases. We settle into the teahouse and prepare for tomorrow’s journey to Everest Base Camp.

Day 9: Trek from Lobuche to Gorak Shep and Visit Everest Base Camp

Glacial Moraine & Expedition Foothold — Gorak Shep (5,164 m) [EBC: 5,364 m] — 7–8 hrs (12 km)

WALKING TIME Approximately 7–8 hours
TOTAL TREK DISTANCE Approximately 12 km, including the base camp visit and return to Gorak Shep
HIGHEST POINT Everest Base Camp, 5,364 m
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Gorak Shep, approximately 5,164 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Gorak Shep
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We start from Lobuche and follow the rocky trail towards Gorak Shep. After a break, we continue to Everest Base Camp, crossing uneven glacial ground along the Khumbu Glacier.

Reaching base camp brings you into the landscape used by Everest expedition teams. During the spring climbing season, tents and expedition activity may be visible. At other times, the glacier and surrounding mountains define the visit.

After spending time at base camp, we retrace the trail to Gorak Shep. Tonight’s stop puts us close to the starting point for tomorrow’s Kala Patthar hike.

Day 10: Hike Kala Patthar and Take the Helicopter Shuttle to Lukla

Summit Viewpoint & Aerial Helicopter Shuttle — Lukla (2,860 m) [Kala Patthar: 5,545 m] — 3–4 hrs hike + Heli

HIKING TIME Allow approximately 3–4 hours for the return hike; pace and conditions vary
TRANSPORT Helicopter shuttle from Gorak Shep to Lukla; timing confirmed by the operating team
HIGHEST POINT Kala Patthar viewpoint, approximately 5,545 m
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Lukla, approximately 2,860 m
ACCOMMODATION Lodge in Lukla with an attached bathroom
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner during the trekking programme

We make an early start for Kala Patthar. The climb above Gorak Shep is steep in places, and the altitude makes it demanding. In clear weather, the viewpoint offers close views of Everest, Nuptse, Pumori and the surrounding Khumbu peaks.

After the hike, return to Gorak Shep for breakfast and prepare for the helicopter shuttle. Follow the operating team’s pickup, seating and baggage instructions. Weather determines whether the flight proceeds. After landing in Lukla, check into your lodge before tomorrow’s onward flight to Kathmandu.

Day 11: Fly from Lukla and Return to Kathmandu

Fixed-Wing Mountain Flight — Kathmandu (1,400 m) — Hotel Check-in & Rest

MAIN ACTIVITY Flight to Kathmandu, or Manthali followed by road travel
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 1,400 m
ACCOMMODATION 3-star hotel in Kathmandu
MEALS Lukla breakfast as quoted; city meals at your own expense

We prepare for the return flight from Lukla. Depending on the operating schedule, you may fly directly to Kathmandu or land at Manthali Airport and continue by road.

After reaching your hotel, the remaining time is available for rest or shopping. Organise your belongings and confirm tomorrow’s airport transfer arrangements with our team. Flight timing depends on weather and airline operations, so allow flexibility around your return.

Day 12: Departure from Kathmandu

Airport Transfer & International Departure — Tribhuvan International Airport (KTM)

MAIN ACTIVITY International departure and airport transfer
MEALS Kathmandu breakfast only if included in your hotel arrangement

Your twelve-day Everest journey ends today. Prepare for your international departure and confirm the transfer time with our team. Depending on your flight schedule, there may be time for a final walk near the hotel.

Our representative accompanies you to Tribhuvan International Airport for your departure.

Before booking your EBC helicopter return journey, consider the walking, altitude and flight arrangements. These details help you prepare and decide whether this twelve-day itinerary suits your fitness, experience and travel plans.

1. Trek Difficulty and Fitness

Challenging outward trek over steep paths with helicopter flight return from Gorak Shep.

The outward trek involves steep paths, stone steps and uneven ground on consecutive days. The helicopter reduces return walking, while Everest Base Camp and Kala Patthar still require sustained effort at high altitude. Technical climbing skills are not normally required, but fitness and preparation remain essential.

Previous hiking experience is helpful. First-time trekkers should build their endurance before departure and become comfortable walking uphill and downhill with a daypack.

Begin preparing around two to three months beforehand, allowing longer if you are starting from a low fitness level. Gradually combine regular walks, hill hikes and strength exercises. Practise in the boots you will wear on the trek.

2. Altitude and Acclimatisation

Acclimatisation schedule in Namche and Dingboche; safety and health always take priority.

Our itinerary includes acclimatisation days in Namche Bazaar and Dingboche. These give you time to adjust before continuing higher, but altitude illness can still affect you, regardless of fitness.

Tell your guide immediately about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not continue to a higher sleeping altitude while experiencing symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite resting at the same elevation, descent is necessary. Reaching Base Camp must remain secondary to your health.

3. Medical Conditions and Travel Insurance

Mandatory travel insurance covering trekking up to 5,545 m and emergency medical evacuation.

If you have an existing medical condition, seek advice from a healthcare professional familiar with high-altitude travel before booking. Discuss your planned route and any medicines you take.

Travel insurance is compulsory for this package. Confirm trekking cover to approximately 5,545 metres, emergency helicopter evacuation and medical treatment in Nepal. The planned shuttle does not replace rescue cover. Check exclusions, excess charges and the assistance procedure; carry policy details and contacts.

4. Accommodation and Room Facilities

2 hotel nights in Kathmandu plus 9 lodge nights with attached bathrooms where available.

The package includes two nights in a three-star Kathmandu hotel and nine lodge nights. Attached bathrooms are included in Phakding, Namche and Lukla. Confirm room sharing and Kathmandu breakfasts before departure.

Higher on the route, expect simpler rooms and shared bathroom facilities. Comfort varies between lodges, and private rooms can be limited during busy periods. Be prepared for cold nights and fewer facilities around Lobuche and Gorak Shep.

5. Food and Dietary Requirements

Nutritious full-board mountain meals (breakfast, lunch, dinner) with dietary requirements handled.

Breakfast, lunch and dinner are included during the trek. Teahouse menus commonly offer dal bhat, noodles, potatoes, soups and other straightforward meals.

Tell us about allergies or dietary requirements before booking so we can discuss what can be arranged along the route. Explain any strict requirements clearly, including concerns about shared cooking equipment.

6. Drinking Water

Boiled or treated drinking water using reusable bottles; single-use plastic bottles discouraged.

Carry reusable bottles and drink boiled or appropriately treated water. Water from a clear stream or an untreated tap may still contain germs.

Boiling is the most reliable way to kill germs. If boiling is unavailable, filtration followed by disinfection offers broader protection than many portable filters alone. Follow the instructions for your treatment equipment or tablets.

7. Guide and Porter Support

Experienced government-licensed guide and porter support shared between two trekkers.

An experienced English-speaking guide leads the trek, with porters carrying your agreed luggage allowance. You carry your daypack. Confirm porter allocation, baggage limits and the team’s return travel arrangements before leaving for Lukla.

8. Showers, Charging and Communication

Services vary by elevation; carry a power bank and prepare for remote connectivity.

Shower, Wi-Fi and charging arrangements vary between lodges; confirm which services are included in your quotation. Connections can be unreliable higher in the valley. Carry a power bank, and tell family that you may be out of contact during parts of the walk and return journey.

9. Permits and Documents

Sagarmatha National Park and Khumbu Pasang Lhamu entry fees arranged and included in full.

We arrange the Sagarmatha National Park permit and Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality entry permit as part of the package. Carry your passport, insurance details and copies of important documents. Your Nepal visa remains your responsibility.

10. Flight Delays and Extra Time

Weather flexibility required for Lukla flights; buffer days recommended in Kathmandu.

Lukla flights can be delayed or cancelled because of weather. During peak periods, flights may use Manthali Airport in Ramechhap, requiring a road transfer.

There is no separate reserve day. The helicopter is scheduled for Day 10, followed by Lukla flights on Day 11 before Day 12 departure.

Weather and Best Time for the EBC Helicopter Return Trek

Spring, from March to May, and autumn, from September to November, are generally the main seasons for this Everest helicopter return trek. Both offer opportunities for mountain views and favourable walking, although weather can affect upper trails, the helicopter shuttle and onward Lukla flights.

Lower trails gradually warm, with rhododendrons on forested sections. March can retain winter snow higher up. April and May bring longer daylight, while afternoon cloud or haze can affect views and flights.

QUIET & COLD

Expect severe cold, short daylight and possible snow or ice on upper trails. Check lodge availability, access to Kala Patthar and flight feasibility before choosing winter dates. Clear days do not guarantee operations.

Monsoon

June–August, sometimes extending into September
LOW SEASON / RAIN

Rain and cloud affect lower trails, mountain visibility and flight operations. Slippery paths can slow progress. Helicopter availability does not remove weather restrictions, so allow flexibility and discuss the operating plan.

These are seasonal patterns; conditions can vary from year to year and during an individual trek.

How Weather Changes Along the Route

Walking around Phakding and Namche Bazaar can feel relatively warm in sunshine. Conditions become colder as you continue towards Dingboche, Lobuche and Gorak Shep, particularly before sunrise and after sunset.

Even during spring and autumn, nights on the higher route can fall below freezing. Exposed sections, including the climb to Kala Patthar, can also feel colder in the wind. Carry warm layers and weather protection whichever month you choose.

Which Season Should You Choose?

Spring Recommendation: Mild daylight temperatures, blooming rhododendrons, and active wildlife across lower approach valleys.
Autumn Recommendation: Crystal-clear post-monsoon atmospheric clarity, sharp panoramic views, and stable high-altitude conditions.

Choose spring if you prefer gradually warming days and flowering forests on the lower trail. Choose autumn, particularly October or November, if mountain visibility is your main priority. Winter and monsoon require greater flexibility and preparation for less comfortable conditions.

Everest Base Camp Trek with Helicopter Return FAQs

General & Route

Can I do this trek without previous trekking experience?

Yes, provided you prepare well and have suitable fitness. Previous hiking experience helps, but technical climbing skills are unnecessary. Before departure, practise longer hill walks with a daypack and become comfortable walking uphill and downhill on consecutive days.

What is the highest altitude on this itinerary?

The highest planned point is Kala Patthar at approximately 5,545 metres, above Everest Base Camp at 5,364 metres. Your highest overnight stop is Gorak Shep at approximately 5,164 metres, where you stay after visiting Base Camp.

Can I see Mount Everest from Base Camp?

Everest’s summit is generally hidden behind nearby ridges from Base Camp itself. Kala Patthar provides the clearer view of Everest that many travellers expect. Our itinerary includes this viewpoint, although mountain visibility depends on the weather.

Are acclimatisation days complete rest days?

They usually include a shorter hike above Namche Bazaar or Dingboche, followed by a return to the same village to sleep. Your guide adjusts the hike according to conditions and how the group is feeling.

Is our return helicopter private or shared?

Confirm the allocation in your quotation. The package includes a Gorak Shep–Lukla shuttle, but the published prices do not specify private or shared use. Ask about seating, baggage limits, staff travel arrangements and extra charges before confirming your booking.

Does our return helicopter fly directly back to Kathmandu?

The helicopter shuttle takes you from Gorak Shep to Lukla on Day 10. After an overnight stay, Day 11 uses an onward flight to Kathmandu or Manthali, with road travel to Kathmandu if landing there. Weather may affect either stage.

Is insurance covering trekking to 5,500 metres sufficient?

A limit of 5,500 metres falls below the planned Kala Patthar elevation of approximately 5,545 metres. Ask your insurer to confirm coverage for the full route, emergency helicopter evacuation and medical treatment, including any relevant exclusions or conditions.

Does being fit protect me from altitude sickness?

No. Good fitness helps with the walking demands, but it does not prevent altitude illness. Fit and experienced trekkers can also become unwell. Follow the acclimatisation schedule and report symptoms immediately instead of assuming fitness will protect you.

What should I do if I feel unwell at altitude?

Tell your guide immediately about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not ascend to a higher sleeping altitude while experiencing symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite rest at the same elevation, descent is necessary.

Do I need to carry my main trekking bag?

Porters carry your trekking bag within the agreed allowance. You carry water, weather protection, medicines and essentials in a daypack. Confirm porter allocation and helicopter baggage limits, keeping your passport, money and valuables with you.

Do I need to bring a sleeping bag and down jacket?

Confirm any loan equipment in your quotation. Otherwise, arrange your own sleeping bag, down jacket and trekking bag before departure. Check suitable warmth, rental availability, equipment condition and baggage limits for both aircraft types with us beforehand.

Can I book as a solo traveller?

The starting price for one person is USD 3,105. Send your travel dates so we can check availability and confirm guide, porter and helicopter arrangements, accommodation and the final quotation for your planned twelve-day journey.
