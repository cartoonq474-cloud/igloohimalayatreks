Nar Phu Valley Trek Overview

Our Nar Phu Valley Trek follows the Annapurna trail to Koto before entering the valleys of Phu and Nar. You walk through forests and narrow gorges towards stone villages above 4,000 m. The journey includes a day in Phu and crosses Kang La at around 5,320 m before descending to Ngawal on the Annapurna Circuit.

After driving from Kathmandu to Dharapani, you begin walking along the Marsyangdi Valley towards Koto. Here, the route turns away from the main circuit and follows the Nar Phu River through a gorge. Forest paths, bridges and uphill sections lead towards Meta. Beyond Meta, the valley becomes more open and rocky as you approach Phu, where stone houses, prayer flags and monasteries reflect the village’s Tibetan Buddhist traditions.

You spend another day in Phu before retracing part of the valley towards Nar Phedi. The next stage climbs to Nar, with mani walls and stone buildings marking the approach. After a night in the village, an early start leads towards Kang La, followed by a long descent to Ngawal on the circuit’s northern side.

The day in Phu combines village exploration with another night at the same elevation. This gives you additional time to adjust, although the itinerary still includes substantial altitude gains. Kang La requires a long climb and descent, with cold, wind and possible snow affecting the trail. Previous hiking experience and preparation for sustained mountain walking are helpful on this route.

You stay in local teahouses and lodges during trekking and a 3-star hotel in Kathmandu. Ground transport connects the walking route with Kathmandu through Dharapani, Humde and Besisahar.

Trek Difficulty and Fitness

The trek is moderate to challenging, combining several walking days with nights above 4,000 m. Kang La reaches approximately 5,320 m and requires a long climb and descent. Narrow gorge paths, rocky ground and steep sections add to the effort, particularly when weather affects the trail.

Previous mountain hiking is helpful. Beginners should discuss fitness and experience before booking, if this would be their first trek above 4,000 m.

Begin preparing around two to three months beforehand, allowing longer if you are starting from a low fitness level. Gradually combine regular walks, hill hikes and strength exercises. Practise in the boots you will wear on the trek.

Altitude and Acclimatisation

Day 6 combines exploration in Phu with time to acclimatise. Significant altitude gains remain, particularly from Koto to Meta. Discuss the ascent and any additional time you need beforehand.

Tell your guide promptly about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not climb higher while experiencing symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite resting at the same elevation, descent is necessary. Fitness does not prevent altitude illness, and your condition must guide decisions throughout.

Medical Conditions and Travel Insurance

If you have an existing medical condition, seek advice from a healthcare professional familiar with high-altitude travel before booking. Discuss the route, sleeping altitudes and medicines you take.

Arrange insurance covering trekking through approximately 5,320 metres, emergency helicopter evacuation and medical treatment in Nepal. Check exclusions, excess charges and the insurer’s assistance procedure before departure. Carry your policy and emergency contacts. Insurance, evacuation and medical expenses are excluded from the package.

Accommodation and Room Facilities

You stay in a 3-star hotel in Kathmandu for two nights, with local teahouses and lodges during trekking. Nar Phedi has simple accommodation near its monastery. Confirm the room arrangements beforehand.

Facilities become more limited deeper in the valley, particularly around Phu, Nar and Nar Phedi. Ask about sleeping arrangements and bathroom facilities before departure. Prepare for cold rooms and nights above 4,000 m, keeping warm layers accessible.

Food and Dietary Requirements

Meals commonly include dal bhat, noodles, soup and vegetable dishes. Choices become limited deeper in the valley; dietary arrangements need advance discussion.

Tell us about allergies or dietary requirements before booking so we can discuss what can be arranged along the route. Explain strict requirements clearly, including concerns about shared cooking equipment beforehand.

Drinking Water

Carry reusable bottles and drink boiled or appropriately treated water. Clear stream water and untreated tap water can contain germs that cause illness.

Boiling is the most reliable way to kill germs. If boiling is unavailable, filtration followed by disinfection offers broader protection than many portable filters alone. Follow the instructions for your treatment products and equipment.

Guide and Porter Support

A licensed, English-speaking guide and one porter for every two trekkers are included. Staff wages, accommodation, meals and insurance are covered. Confirm the porter’s baggage allowance and solo allocation before packing your bag.

Showers, Charging and Communication

Hot showers, Wi-Fi and device charging cost extra where available. Facilities vary between lodges, and connections can be unreliable deeper in the valley. Carry a power bank and explain to family that contact may be limited. Ask your guide about available services at each overnight stop.

Permits and Documents

The package includes the Nar Phu Restricted Area Permit, ACAP and applicable charges. We arrange the documentation. Agency and guide requirements also apply to individual travellers. Carry your passport, permits and insurance details throughout the journey.

Travel Delays and Extra Time

Rain, landslides and roadworks can affect the transfers. Snow, wind and visibility can affect Kang La, so allow flexibility around the published walking programme.

Allow extra time before your international departure. Additional accommodation, meals or transport may cost extra when delays or changes require services outside the programme.

Altitude Profile of the Nar Phu Valley Trek

The route climbs from Dharapani through Koto and Meta to Phu, where you stay for two nights. After Nar Phedi, you climb to Nar before crossing Kang La and descending to Ngawal. The pass is higher than the overnight stops. Day 11’s overnight remains unconfirmed.

Weather and Best Time for the Nar Phu Valley Trek

Spring, from March to May, and autumn, from September to November, are generally the best seasons for the Nar Phu Valley Trek. Both offer opportunities for mountain views and more favourable walking conditions, although cold, wind, rain and snowfall remain possible around Kang La.

Seasonal Weather Conditions

Season Months What to Expect
Spring March–May Lower sections become warmer, while snow and ice can remain near Kang La. March may retain winter conditions; later spring brings warmer days, with afternoon cloud and showers possible. Prepare for cold mornings above the villages.
Autumn September–November Early September can retain monsoon rain on lower trails and roads. October and November generally offer clearer views, with colder nights later in the season. Check the forecast and conditions before the Kang La crossing.
Winter December–February Cold nights, snow and ice can make Kang La difficult or prevent a crossing. Lodge availability and access also need checking. Discuss the complete route and available alternatives before planning a winter departure.
Monsoon June–August, sometimes extending into September Nar and Phu are relatively dry compared with Nepal’s wetter southern slopes, but lower trails and access roads can still receive rain. Landslides and transport delays remain possible. Clear views and a pass crossing are not guaranteed.

These are seasonal patterns; conditions can vary from year to year and during an individual trek.

How Weather Changes Along the Route

Lower valley walks can feel warmer in sunshine, while Phu and Nar are colder after sunset. Kang La is more exposed, with wind adding to the cold during the crossing.

Even during spring and autumn, the villages and pass can feel cold before dawn. Winter brings a greater chance of snow and freezing conditions around Kang La. Carry warm layers and weather protection whichever season you choose for trekking.

Which Season Should You Choose?

Choose spring for warming days and returning vegetation on lower forest sections. Choose autumn, particularly October or November, if mountain visibility is your priority. Winter and monsoon need greater flexibility around the pass, access roads and availability of village accommodation.

Frequently Asked Questions for Nar Phu Valley Trek

General Information

Can I do this trek without previous trekking experience? ∨
A fit beginner may consider it after discussing preparation and experience with us. However, Nar Phu involves nights above 4,000 m and a demanding Kang La crossing. Previous mountain hiking helps you judge the sustained walking and altitude.
What is the highest altitude on this itinerary? ∨
Kang La Pass, at approximately 5,320 metres, is the highest point. You cross it on Day 9 before descending to Ngawal. Nar, around 4,100 metres, is the highest planned overnight stop in the planned trekking route.
Does this route include crossing Thorong La Pass? ∨
No. This route crosses Kang La and descends to Ngawal before finishing the walking section at Humde. Thorong La belongs to different Annapurna Circuit itineraries. We can discuss those options if you want a longer route.
What is planned for Day 11 on this itinerary? ∨
Day 11 is not published and remains awaiting confirmation. Day 10 ends in Besisahar, while Day 12 returns to Kathmandu. This does not confirm an extra rest day or overnight stay; we will clarify the complete programme before your booking.
Do we stay overnight at Kang La? ∨
No. Kang La is a daytime crossing, followed by the descent to Ngawal for the night. You sleep in Nar before the crossing. The highest planned overnight stop is around 4,100 metres, below the pass at approximately 5,320 metres.
Are acclimatisation days complete rest days? ∨
No. The day combines village walks and exploration with another night in Phu. Your guide adjusts the activity to conditions and how you feel. Rest remains important before continuing towards Nar Phedi the following day.
Do I need to carry my main trekking bag? ∨
A porter carries your main trekking bag, with one porter for every two travellers. You carry a daypack with water, layers, medicines and valuables. Confirm the baggage allowance and porter allocation before packing for departure.
Do I need to bring a sleeping bag and down jacket? ∨
Complimentary use of a sleeping bag, down jacket and duffel bag is included where applicable. Confirm availability, suitability and any return arrangements before departure. Bring your own boots, clothing and other personal gear for the mountain conditions.
Is insurance covering trekking to 5,000 metres sufficient? ∨
No. Kang La reaches approximately 5,320 metres, above that limit. Arrange cover for the full planned altitude, medical treatment and emergency helicopter evacuation in Nepal. Check exclusions, excess charges and the insurer’s assistance procedure before starting the journey.
Does being fit protect me from altitude sickness? ∨
No. Good fitness helps with the walking demands, but it does not prevent altitude illness. Fit and experienced trekkers can also become unwell. Follow your guide’s advice and report symptoms promptly instead of assuming fitness will protect you.
What should I do if I feel unwell at altitude? ∨
Tell your guide promptly about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not ascend higher while experiencing symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite resting at the same elevation, descent is necessary. Seek appropriate medical help.
Can I book as a solo traveller? ∨
Yes. The solo rate starts from USD 1,305. Individual travellers still need agency permit arrangements and a licensed guide. Send your dates so we can confirm the itinerary, porter allocation, room arrangements and final quotation.
