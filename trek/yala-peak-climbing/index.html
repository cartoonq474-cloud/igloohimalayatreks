Yala Peak Climbing — 12 Days

Trek through the Langtang Valley and attempt Yala Peak on this twelve-day guided journey. Follow forest trails through Lama Hotel and Langtang Village to Kyanjin Gompa, where an acclimatisation day comes before the climb. Continue to a supported tent camp, practise using climbing equipment and prepare for the summit attempt. Afterwards, return through Kyanjin Gompa and Syabrubesi. The programme combines village teahouses, a base camp night and mountain climbing, with road travel from Kathmandu at either end.

Yala Peak Climbing Package Overview

Our twelve-day Yala Peak Climbing package combines the Langtang Valley approach with an acclimatisation day at Kyanjin Gompa and a supported night at base camp. The planned summit attempt reaches approximately 5,520 metres before descending to Kyanjin Gompa. Forest trails, Tamang village life and changing mountain scenery form part of the journey to the climb.

After travelling by road to Syabrubesi, you follow the Langtang Khola through forest and cross bridges towards Lama Hotel. The route climbs towards Langtang Village, where rebuilt houses and teahouses stand beneath the surrounding peaks. Prayer wheels, mani walls and grazing areas accompany the approach to Kyanjin Gompa. Spend two nights here before base camp, with a day for an acclimatisation walk chosen according to health and conditions.

From Kyanjin Gompa, continue over open slopes and rough ground to Yala Peak Base Camp, around 4,800 metres. A supported tented night brings you closer to the climbing route. The climbing guide checks equipment and provides practical instruction before the summit attempt. The following day includes the climb and a descent back to Kyanjin Gompa.

Yala Peak requires strong walking fitness and preparation for altitude, cold and a long summit day. Rocky ground may hold snow or ice, making suitable equipment and guide supervision essential. The acclimatisation day provides adjustment time, while sleeping-height gains remain substantial. The guide assesses health and conditions before continuing. Summit plans may change when weather, snow or progress requires it.

Accommodation combines Kathmandu hotels, valley teahouses and a supported base camp. The twelve-day programme covers arrival, the approach trek, climbing preparations, the summit attempt, return travel and departure.

Yala Peak Climbing Package Itinerary — Day by Day

Our twelve-day itinerary follows the Langtang Valley to Yala Peak, with acclimatisation and a tented base camp before the summit attempt. Times are approximate; weather, trail conditions, health and progress can affect the schedule.

Day 1: Arrival in Kathmandu

Arrival — Approximately 1,400 m — Arrival in Kathmandu

Overnight altitude Approximately 1,400 m
Accommodation 3-star hotel in Kathmandu; room sharing as quoted
Meals Arrival-day meals at your own expense

Welcome to Nepal. Our representative meets you at Tribhuvan International Airport and transfers you to your hotel. After settling in, we review the programme, discuss the route and check equipment questions before departure for the Langtang region.

Depending on your arrival time, explore nearby streets or rest after your flight. Keep documents and personal equipment ready, and confirm tomorrow's transfer to the bus station for the journey towards Syabrubesi.

Day 2: Drive from Kathmandu to Syabrubesi

Drive — Approximately 1,503 m — 7–8 hours

Driving time Approximately 7–8 hours; road conditions and traffic affect timing
Main activity Taxi to the bus station, then public bus to Syabrubesi
Overnight altitude Approximately 1,503 m
Accommodation Teahouse in Syabrubesi
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

After a taxi transfer to the bus station, take the public bus towards Syabrubesi. The journey follows winding roads through hills, towns and river valleys north of Kathmandu.

Traffic, road repairs and weather can affect arrival, so keep water, medicines and a warm layer accessible. At Syabrubesi, settle into the teahouse and organise your daypack before tomorrow's walking stage through the lower Langtang Valley towards Lama Hotel.

Day 3: Trek from Syabrubesi to Lama Hotel

Trek — Approximately 2,470 m — 6–7 hours

Walking time Approximately 6–7 hours
Trek distance Approximately 11 km
Overnight altitude Approximately 2,470 m
Accommodation Teahouse at Lama Hotel
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Our walking journey begins from Syabrubesi and follows the Langtang Khola towards Lama Hotel. Bridges link forest paths, with climbs and uneven sections making this a full first trekking day.

The route passes wooded slopes and riverside sections, with bamboo and other forest growth lining parts of the valley. Watch for langur monkeys and listen for birds among the trees. Take the steeper stretches steadily, allowing time for breaks. We reach Lama Hotel and settle into the teahouse before tomorrow's continued ascent towards Langtang Village.

Day 4: Trek from Lama Hotel to Langtang Village

Trek — Approximately 3,430 m — 6–7 hours

Walking time Approximately 6–7 hours
Trek distance Approximately 14 km
Overnight altitude Approximately 3,430 m
Accommodation Teahouse in Langtang Village
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We continue beside the river and climb through forest towards the upper Langtang Valley. Trees gradually become fewer, with grazing areas and mountain views opening around the trail as we approach the rebuilt Langtang Village.

The village has rebuilt since the 2015 earthquake. Respect local privacy and follow guidance near memorial areas. Today gains substantial sleeping height from Lama Hotel, so walk steadily and report symptoms early. After reaching the teahouse, rest before tomorrow's shorter walk towards Kyanjin Gompa.

Day 5: Trek to Kyanjin Gompa

Trek — Approximately 3,870 m — 3–4 hours

Walking time Approximately 3–4 hours
Trek distance Approximately 7 km
Overnight altitude Approximately 3,870 m
Accommodation Teahouse in Kyanjin Gompa
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We leave Langtang Village and continue through open grazing areas towards Kyanjin Gompa. Prayer wheels, mani walls and Buddhist monuments accompany the walk, with mountains becoming prominent as the valley broadens.

After arriving, check into the teahouse for two nights before the climb. The remaining time allows rest, a short village walk or a monastery visit when open. Keep the afternoon gentle while adjusting to the higher sleeping elevation, and prepare for tomorrow's acclimatisation outing above the village with your guide.

Day 6: Acclimatisation Day at Kyanjin Gompa

Acclimatisation — Around 4,773 m at upper Kyanjin Ri or 4,984 m at Tserko Ri, if reached — Roughly 4–5 hours for upper Kyanjin Ri or 7–8 hours for Tserko Ri

Walking time Roughly 4–5 hours for upper Kyanjin Ri or 7–8 hours for Tserko Ri; shorter turnarounds vary
Main activity Selected acclimatisation walk and return to Kyanjin Gompa
Daytime high point Around 4,773 m at upper Kyanjin Ri or 4,984 m at Tserko Ri, if reached
Overnight altitude Approximately 3,870 m
Accommodation Same teahouse in Kyanjin Gompa
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We stay another night at Kyanjin Gompa and take an acclimatisation walk. Kyanjin Ri or Tserko Ri are possible directions, with the route and turnaround chosen according to health, weather and the group's progress.

Clear conditions offer views across the Langtang mountains and nearby glaciers. The full Tserko Ri outing is longer and more demanding than Kyanjin Ri. Return to the teahouse afterwards, rest and discuss tomorrow's base camp approach and the equipment needed for climbing.

Day 7: Trek to Yala Peak Base Camp

Trek — Approximately 4,800 m — 5–6 hours

Walking time Approximately 5–6 hours
Main activity Base camp approach, equipment checks and supervised climbing practice
Overnight altitude Approximately 4,800 m
Accommodation Supported tent camp at Yala Peak Base Camp
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Leaving Kyanjin Gompa, we climb through open grazing ground and increasingly rocky terrain towards Yala Peak Base Camp. The route gains substantial sleeping height, so steady pacing and attention to how you feel remain important throughout the approach.

At camp, settle into the tent and work with the climbing guide on equipment checks and practical instruction. Practise using the supplied gear under supervision, then review the summit plan, weather, turnaround arrangements and preparations for an early start tomorrow.

Day 8: Summit Yala Peak and Return to Kyanjin Gompa

Summit Attempt — Yala Peak, approximately 5,520 m if reached — 9–11 hours for the climb and full return to Kyanjin Gompa

Walking and climbing time Approximately 9–11 hours for the climb and full return to Kyanjin Gompa; conditions may extend timing
Main activity Guided summit attempt, descent to base camp and return to Kyanjin Gompa
Highest point Yala Peak, approximately 5,520 m if reached
Overnight altitude Kyanjin Gompa, approximately 3,870 m
Accommodation Teahouse in Kyanjin Gompa
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We leave base camp early for the summit attempt. The route crosses rocky ground and may include snow or ice, with the guide choosing techniques and equipment for conditions.

Climb at a steady pace and follow the guide's instructions. Health, weather and progress determine whether continuing is appropriate. If the summit is reached in clear conditions, views extend towards Shishapangma, Langtang Lirung and neighbouring mountains.

After the attempt, descend carefully to base camp before continuing to Kyanjin Gompa. This is a long day, ending with a teahouse night at a lower elevation.

Day 9: Trek from Kyanjin Gompa to Lama Hotel

Trek — Approximately 2,470 m — 6–7 hours

Walking time Approximately 6–7 hours
Trail character Long descent through Langtang Village and back into forest
Overnight altitude Approximately 2,470 m
Accommodation Teahouse at Lama Hotel
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We leave Kyanjin Gompa and retrace the Langtang Valley through Langtang Village. Open grazing areas give way to wooded slopes as we descend alongside the river towards Lama Hotel.

This is another full walking day, with long descents after the climb. Take care on uneven stones and steps, using poles if helpful. The forest brings a change from the open terrain around base camp. At Lama Hotel, settle into the teahouse and rest before tomorrow's final stage towards Syabrubesi.

Day 10: Trek from Lama Hotel to Syabrubesi

Trek — Approximately 1,503 m — 5–6 hours

Walking time Approximately 5–6 hours
Trail character Forest paths, bridges and uneven riverside sections
Overnight altitude Approximately 1,503 m
Accommodation Teahouse in Syabrubesi
Meals Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Our final trekking day follows the lower Langtang Valley towards Syabrubesi. Forest paths, bridges and riverside sections link the route back to the town where the approach to Yala Peak began.

The trail loses height overall, with shorter climbs along the way. Watch your footing on wet stones and steps, especially when tired after several mountain days. Warmer conditions and village activity accompany the lower route. On reaching Syabrubesi, check into the teahouse and organise your bags for tomorrow's return bus.

Day 11: Drive from Syabrubesi to Kathmandu

Drive — Approximately 1,400 m — 7–8 hours

Driving time Approximately 7–8 hours; road conditions and traffic affect timing
Main activity Public bus to Kathmandu and private taxi transfer to the hotel
Overnight altitude Approximately 1,400 m

After breakfast, take the public bus from Syabrubesi to Kathmandu. The return journey passes river valleys, cultivated hillsides and towns before reaching the city and your hotel transfer.

A private taxi takes you from the bus station to your hotel. Depending on arrival time, rest or explore nearby streets. A Nepali farewell dinner is included for the evening. Confirm tomorrow's airport transfer and prepare for international departure.

Day 12: Final Departure from Kathmandu

Departure — Kathmandu, 1,400 m — Final Departure from Kathmandu

Our representative accompanies you to Tribhuvan International Airport for your departure.

Climb Difficulty and Fitness

The approach includes consecutive walking days, steep paths and altitude gain. Above Kyanjin Gompa, the climb adds exposed rocky ground with possible snow or ice. The summit attempt and descent form a long day. Suitable equipment, guide supervision and preparation for cold conditions are essential throughout.

Previous mountain trekking experience helps. First-time climbers should discuss fitness and background, then learn the required equipment skills under the climbing guide’s supervision.

Begin preparing around two to three months beforehand, allowing longer if you are starting from a low fitness level. Gradually combine regular walks, hill hikes and strength exercises. Practise in the boots you will wear on the trek.

Altitude and Acclimatisation

An acclimatisation day at Kyanjin Gompa provides adjustment time. The ascent to Langtang Village and the move to base camp still gain substantial sleeping height, requiring assessment before continuing.

Tell your guide promptly about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not continue to a higher sleeping altitude while experiencing symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite resting at the same elevation, descent is necessary. The planned summit attempt remains secondary to your health.

Medical Conditions and Travel Insurance

If you have an existing medical condition, seek advice from a healthcare professional familiar with high-altitude travel before booking. Discuss your planned route and any medicines you take.

Travel insurance is required for this climb. Confirm cover for mountaineering with the equipment, the full route and agreed summit height, emergency helicopter evacuation and medical treatment. A trekking-only policy may exclude climbing. Check exclusions and assistance procedures, and carry your policy details.

Accommodation and Room Facilities

Accommodation includes two Kathmandu hotel nights, eight teahouse nights and one supported tent-camp night at base camp. Teahouses usually offer twin rooms, with room sharing and upgrades confirmed in your quotation.

Facilities become simpler higher in the valley, and shared bathrooms are common. At Yala Peak Base Camp, you sleep in a tent with camping equipment supplied. Bring a sleeping bag and prepare for cold conditions after sunset.

Food and Dietary Requirements

Trekking and climbing meals include breakfast, lunch and dinner. Teahouse menus commonly offer dal bhat, noodles, potatoes, soups and other simple meals.

Tell us about allergies or dietary requirements before booking so we can discuss what can be arranged along the route. Explain any strict requirements clearly, including concerns about shared cooking equipment.

Drinking Water

Carry reusable bottles and drink boiled or appropriately treated water. Purification tablets are supplied; follow their instructions when treating water along the route.

Boiling is the most reliable way to kill germs. If boiling is unavailable, filtration followed by disinfection offers broader protection than many portable filters alone. Follow the instructions for your treatment equipment or tablets.

Guides and Porter Support

A licensed trekking guide accompanies the approach and return. A certified climbing guide supports the upper stage from Kyanjin Gompa. One porter serves two trekkers; confirm climbing ratios, luggage allowances and equipment arrangements.

Showers, Charging and Communication

Hot showers, Wi-Fi and device charging cost extra, with availability varying between teahouses. Mobile reception and power supplies can be unreliable higher in Langtang, especially around base camp. Carry a power bank and spare headlamp batteries, and prepare for periods without reliable contact during the journey.

Permits and Documents

We arrange Langtang National Park entry, TIMS and required documentation for the agreed route. Carry your passport, insurance details and copies of important documents. Confirm current climbing formalities before departure; your Nepal visa remains your responsibility.

Road Delays and Extra Time

Road journeys can take longer because of traffic, repairs, weather or landslide damage. Snow, wind and visibility can also change the planned climbing schedule.

The twelve-day itinerary has no summit contingency day. Allow flexibility before international departure; extra nights or changed transport outside the services may cost more.

How Weather Changes Along the Route

Elevation gradients & trail exposure

Walking through the lower Langtang Valley can feel warm in sunshine. Conditions grow colder at Kyanjin Gompa, while base camp and the climbing slopes bring exposure to wind and snow.

Even in spring and autumn, base camp nights can fall below freezing. An early climbing start adds darkness and wind exposure. Carry warm layers, insulated gloves and weather protection, and follow the climbing guide’s advice about conditions and equipment.

Carry warm layers, sun protection, and reliable weather protection accessible in your daypack throughout the route.

Which Season Should You Choose?

Optimal timing for trek priorities

Choose spring for warmer approach days and flowering forest trails. Choose autumn, particularly October or November, if mountain visibility is your priority. The summit attempt depends on local conditions in either season.

Winter and monsoon require more planning and flexibility.

Yala Peak Climbing Package FAQs

Answers to the most common questions about the 12-day Yala Peak climbing itinerary, technical gear, altitude acclimatisation, and base camp logistics.

Climbing & Route

Can I attempt Yala Peak without previous climbing experience?

Previous mountain trekking experience helps, although the package includes instruction before the climb. First-time climbers should discuss their fitness and background with the company. The guide checks equipment skills and decides whether the conditions suit a summit attempt.

What is the highest altitude on this itinerary?

The planned Yala Peak summit is approximately 5,520 metres. Base camp, at around 4,800 metres, is the highest overnight stop. Confirm the exact high point for your agreed climbing route and match your insurance cover accordingly.

Is reaching the summit guaranteed on this climb?

The summit attempt depends on health, weather, snow and progress. The climbing guide sets the turnaround and may change the plan when necessary. The twelve-day itinerary has no separate contingency day for another attempt after delays.

Where do we sleep before summit day?

You spend one night in a supported tent camp at Yala Peak Base Camp before the summit attempt. Camping equipment is included. Bring your own suitable sleeping bag unless your quotation provides a confirmed alternative arrangement for the trip.

Are acclimatisation days complete rest days?

Day 6 includes a selected walk above Kyanjin Gompa before returning to the same teahouse. Kyanjin Ri or Tserko Ri may be considered, with a shorter turnaround chosen when health, weather or progress requires it.

Does being fit protect me from altitude sickness?

Fitness helps with sustained walking and climbing, but it does not prevent altitude illness. Fit and experienced climbers can also become unwell. Follow the guide’s assessment and report symptoms promptly rather than relying on fitness to protect you.

What should I do if I feel unwell at altitude?

Tell your guide promptly about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not ascend to a higher sleeping altitude while experiencing symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite rest at the same elevation, descent is necessary.

How does this differ from the Langtang Valley Trek?

Both journeys approach Kyanjin Gompa through Langtang Valley. This package adds a supported base camp night, climbing equipment, practical instruction and a guided Yala Peak summit attempt. The added altitude, camp and climbing day require preparation beyond ordinary valley walking.

Do I need to carry my main trekking bag?

A porter carries agreed main luggage, with one porter shared between two trekkers. You carry water, warm layers, medicines and daily essentials. Confirm how climbing equipment is carried, along with weight allowances, before leaving Kathmandu.

Do I need to bring a sleeping bag and down jacket?

Yes. Sleeping bags, down jackets, boots and trekking clothing are personal items unless your quotation states otherwise. Listed technical climbing gear is supplied. Confirm helmet arrangements, boot compatibility and equipment sizing before departure, including any rental needs.

Can I book as a solo traveller?

The starting rate for one person is USD 1,715. Send your dates so we can confirm availability, climbing-guide arrangements and porter support. The final quotation states your included services and any equipment or accommodation changes.

Does trekking-only insurance cover the Yala Peak climb?

Ask your insurer to confirm cover for mountaineering with the planned equipment and full route, including the agreed summit height, medical treatment and helicopter evacuation. Check activity exclusions, excess charges and emergency assistance procedures before purchasing a policy.
