Lobuche East Climbing Trip Overview

Our 16-day Lobuche East Climbing Trip follows the Khumbu trail through Namche and Dingboche before a planned Kala Patthar hike and Lobuche East attempt. Kathmandu sightseeing, two acclimatisation days and one contingency day form part of the programme. Confirm the Everest Base Camp visit and the detailed climbing arrangements with us before making your booking.

After flying to Lukla, you follow the Dudh Koshi valley through Phakding, crossing suspension bridges before climbing to Namche Bazaar. Beyond Namche, the trail follows the hillside, descends to the river and climbs again to Tengboche. Its Buddhist monastery and views of Ama Dablam make this a memorable stop. Along the route, you pass Sherpa villages where monasteries, prayer wheels and mani walls reflect the community’s Buddhist traditions.

Beyond Dingboche, the route continues through Thukla to Lobuche. The next stage combines Kala Patthar with the approach to Lobuche Base Camp. Your climbing team prepares you for the planned attempt before descending towards Pheriche. One contingency day allows flexibility around the climb, followed by a return journey through Namche Bazaar and Phakding to Lukla.

Acclimatisation days in Namche and Dingboche combine shorter hikes with additional nights at the same elevation. Lobuche East adds steep rock, snow or ice and fixed-rope climbing to the walking route. Strong fitness and suitable climbing preparation are necessary. Equipment practice supports the attempt, while your guide reviews conditions and your progress before deciding whether to continue up the mountain.

You stay in teahouses, a base-camp tent and a Kathmandu hotel. The 16-day programme covers arrival, sightseeing, the trekking and climbing stages, return travel and departure from Nepal.

Lobuche East Climbing Trip Itinerary — Day by Day

Our 16-day programme includes Kathmandu sightseeing, two acclimatisation days, Kala Patthar, a Lobuche East attempt and one contingency day. Confirm the Everest Base Camp visit, sunrise approach and climbing schedule before finalising your booking.

Day 1: Arrival in Kathmandu

Arrival & Airport Transfer — Kathmandu, approx. 1,400 m — 3-Star Hotel Check-in & Trip Briefing

MAIN ACTIVITY Arrival in Kathmandu & Airport Transfer
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 1,400 m
ACCOMMODATION 3-star hotel in Kathmandu
MEALS Arrival-day meals at own expense

Welcome to Nepal. Our representative meets you at Tribhuvan International Airport and accompanies you to your hotel. After you settle in, we go through the trekking programme, check your essential equipment and discuss any questions you have.

Depending on your arrival time, you can explore the nearby streets of Thamel or rest after your flight. We also help with any final preparations before you leave for the Everest region.

Day 2: Kathmandu Sightseeing Tour

Sightseeing & Pre-Climb Prep — Kathmandu, approx. 1,400 m — Gear Checks & Heritage Stupas

MAIN ACTIVITY Kathmandu sightseeing tour
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 1,400 m
ACCOMMODATION 3-star hotel in Kathmandu
MEALS Breakfast; lunch and dinner at own expense

Spend the day exploring Kathmandu, with visits to temples, stupas or historic squares according to your agreed sightseeing programme. The city offers another perspective before entering the Khumbu.

Confirm the sightseeing stops, transport, entry fees and guide arrangements in your quotation. Leave time to check trekking and climbing equipment, organise agreed rentals and discuss the next day’s flight arrangements with our team before returning to your hotel.

Day 3: Fly to Lukla and Trek to Phakding

Lukla Mountain Flight & Trek — Phakding, 2,610 m — 3–4 Hours / 8 km Dudh Koshi Valley

WALKING TIME Approximately 3–4 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 8 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE 2,610 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Phakding
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Your mountain journey begins with a flight to Lukla at 2,860 m. Depending on airline operations and the season, the flight may depart from Kathmandu or Manthali Airport in Ramechhap.

After landing, you meet your trekking team and begin walking towards Phakding. The trail passes village homes, prayer wheels and forested slopes, with downhill stretches and shorter climbs along the way. Sections follow the Dudh Koshi River, introducing you to the valley you will explore over the coming days. We spend the night in Phakding.

Day 4: Trek from Phakding to Namche Bazaar

Sagarmatha National Park Climb — Namche Bazaar, 3,440 m — 5–6 Hours / 10–11 km Trading Hub

WALKING TIME Approximately 5–6 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 10–11 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE 3,440 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Namche Bazaar
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We follow the Dudh Koshi through villages and pine forests, passing Benkar and Monjo before entering Sagarmatha National Park. Your guide handles the permit checks along the route.

Suspension bridges connect the valley trails, followed by a sustained uphill walk towards Namche. This final climb is the most demanding part of the day, so we allow time for breaks. Namche’s shops, lodges and houses spread across the hillside, making it an important trading town and a useful place to spend the next two nights.

Day 5: Acclimatisation Day in Namche Bazaar

Acclimatisation & Panoramic Hike — Namche Bazaar, 3,440 m — Viewpoint Hike to Hotel Everest View (3,880 m)

WALKING TIME Approximately 3–4 hours
HIKE DISTANCE Approximately 5–6 km
DAYTIME HIGH POINT Around 3,880 m if visiting Hotel Everest View
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE 3,440 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Namche Bazaar
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We stay another night in Namche and take a shorter hike above the town. A usual option is the trail towards Hotel Everest View, where clear weather offers views of Everest and nearby peaks.

Your guide chooses the walk according to conditions and how the group is feeling. Afterwards, we return to Namche for the night. The afternoon gives you time to rest, visit the shops or stop at a café before continuing towards Tengboche tomorrow.

Day 6: Trek from Namche Bazaar to Tengboche

Scenic Ridge & Valley Descent — Tengboche, approx. 3,860 m — 5–6 Hours / 10–11 km Buddhist Monastery

WALKING TIME Approximately 5–6 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 10–11 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Around 3,860 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Tengboche
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Leaving Namche, we follow the hillside trail before descending towards Phunki Tenga. Mountain views accompany sections of the walk, while the river marks the low point before the climb to Tengboche.

The uphill trail passes through pine and rhododendron forest. Once we reach the village, you can settle into the teahouse and explore the surroundings. Tengboche’s Buddhist monastery stands against the mountain backdrop of Ama Dablam. If it is open to visitors, your guide explains the visiting customs before you enter.

Day 7: Trek from Tengboche to Dingboche

Forest Descent & Alpine Ascent — Dingboche, 4,410 m — 5–6 Hours / 11–12 km Imja Valley Lodge

WALKING TIME Approximately 5–6 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 11–12 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE 4,410 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Dingboche
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We descend through forest towards Debuche, cross the Imja Khola and continue through Pangboche. As the route gains height, the trees become fewer and the valley opens into slopes with low vegetation and stone buildings.

The trail passes Somare before reaching Dingboche in the Imja Valley. Today brings a noticeable gain in sleeping altitude, so we keep a steady pace throughout the walk. After checking into your teahouse, the remaining afternoon is available for rest before tomorrow’s acclimatisation hike.

Day 8: Acclimatisation in Dingboche and Hike to Nagarjung Ri

High-Altitude Acclimatisation — Dingboche, 4,410 m — Hike towards Nagarjung Ri (approx. 5,000 m)

WALKING TIME Approximately 4–5 hours, adjusted to conditions and health
HIKE DISTANCE Approximately 4–5 km
DAYTIME OBJECTIVE Nagarjung Ri / Nangkartshang viewpoint; turnaround height depends on conditions
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE 4,410 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Dingboche
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Our second acclimatisation day includes a hike towards Nagarjung Ri above Dingboche, followed by another night in the village. Your guide chooses the turnaround point according to conditions, your health and the group’s progress today.

The hillside gives you a broader view of Dingboche and the surrounding valley. We then return to the teahouse, leaving the afternoon for rest and preparation. Tomorrow, the route continues towards Lobuche, where the terrain becomes increasingly rocky and glacial.

Day 9: Trek from Dingboche to Lobuche

Glacial Moraine & Memorial Ridge — Lobuche, approx. 4,910 m — 5–6 Hours / 8–9 km Thukla Pass

WALKING TIME Approximately 5–6 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 8–9 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Around 4,910 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Lobuche
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We leave Dingboche and cross open ground towards Thukla. After a break, the trail climbs more steeply to the memorial area above the village. The stone memorials commemorate climbers and Sherpas who lost their lives in the mountains.

Beyond this section, we continue beside the Khumbu Glacier towards Lobuche. Uneven ground and increasing altitude make the walking more demanding, even where the gradient eases. At the teahouse, review tomorrow’s Kala Patthar approach and climbing-camp arrangements with your guide.

Day 10: Sunrise at Kala Patthar and Trek to Lobuche Base Camp

Kala Patthar Viewpoint & Camp Approach — Lobuche Base Camp, approx. 4,950 m — Kala Patthar (5,545 m) & Tented Camp

MAIN ACTIVITY Kala Patthar hike and approach to Lobuche Base Camp
START AND DURATION Confirm the full approach, departure time and sunrise arrangements before booking
HIGHEST DAYTIME POINT Kala Patthar, approximately 5,545 m
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Lobuche Base Camp, approximately 4,950 m as listed; confirm actual camp location
ACCOMMODATION Tented camp
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

The day combines the approach through Gorak Shep, a Kala Patthar hike and the walk towards Lobuche Base Camp. Confirm the departure time and sunrise arrangements with us beforehand. Walking at this elevation requires steady pacing, suitable clothing and preparation.

After the viewpoint hike, return towards Lobuche and continue to the agreed climbing camp. Your climbing team checks equipment and explains the techniques needed for the attempt. Confirm the camp location and preparation time before finalising this stage of your trekking and climbing programme.

Day 11: Attempt Lobuche East Summit and Descend to Pheriche

Summit Push & Long Alpine Descent — Pheriche, approx. 4,240 m — Lobuche East Summit (6,119 m) via Fixed Lines

MAIN ACTIVITY Lobuche East summit attempt and descent towards Pheriche
WALKING & CLIMBING TIME A long climbing and descent day; confirm the full stage with the climbing team
HIGHEST PLANNED POINT Lobuche East summit objective, approximately 6,119 m
TERRAIN Rock, steep snow or ice and fixed-rope sections
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Pheriche, approximately 4,240 m if the planned descent is completed
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Pheriche under the main schedule
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Begin before dawn if conditions and your climbing guide’s assessment allow the attempt. The route involves steep ground, snow or ice and fixed ropes, requiring suitable equipment and concentration.

Your guide reviews the route and your progress throughout the ascent. The planned objective is Lobuche East, but reaching it is never guaranteed. Agree the summit objective, turnaround arrangements and full descent plan before leaving camp.

After the attempt, descend carefully to camp and continue towards Pheriche as planned. Conditions and progress determine the overnight stop; conserve energy for the full return journey.

Day 12: Contingency Day

Climbing Reserve & Schedule Flexibility — Contingency Location (Pheriche / High Camp) — Weather & Acclimatisation Buffer

MAIN ACTIVITY Reserve time for changes around the climbing stage
WALKING & CLIMBING TIME Depends on the revised climbing or descent plan
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Depends on where the climbing schedule places the group
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse or tent according to the agreed plan
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

This day allows flexibility if weather, route conditions or the group’s progress change the climbing schedule. Your guide decides how to use it. A reserve day does not guarantee that another attempt will be suitable or possible afterwards.

If the original plan holds, the day can be used for rest or adjustment of the return journey. If it is needed for climbing, confirm the subsequent descent and overnight arrangements. The return flight and departure remain dependent on progress.

Day 13: Trek to Namche Bazaar

Upper Valley Descent & Pine Forest Trails — Namche Bazaar, 3,440 m — 6–7 Hours / 20 km Return Stage

WALKING TIME Approximately 6–7 hours from Pheriche; revised starts change the stage
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 20 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE 3,440 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Namche Bazaar
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

After the climbing stage, continue down the valley through Pangboche towards Tengboche when the schedule allows. Forests gradually reappear as you leave the rocky terrain of the upper Khumbu.

The day combines long descents with an uphill section after crossing the river near Phunki Tenga. From there, we follow the hillside towards Namche. Although we finish much lower than we started, this remains a long walking day. Once back in town, you have time to rest at the teahouse.

Day 14: Trek from Namche Bazaar to Lukla

Final Trail Stage & Suspension Bridges — Lukla, 2,860 m — 6–7 Hours / 18–20 km Final Mountain Night

WALKING TIME Approximately 6–7 hours
TREK DISTANCE Approximately 18–20 km
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE 2,860 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Lukla
MEALS Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Our final trekking day follows the route down from Namche through Jorsalle, Monjo and Phakding. You cross familiar suspension bridges and return through the forested sections of the Dudh Koshi valley.

The approach to Lukla includes uphill stretches, so there is still some effort ahead after the main descent. On arrival, we check into the teahouse for the last night in the mountains. The evening gives you time to organise your belongings and spend time with your guide and porter team.

Day 15: Fly Back to Kathmandu

Lukla Mountain Flight & City Return — Kathmandu, approx. 1,400 m — Hotel Return & Leisure Afternoon

MAIN ACTIVITY Flight from Lukla and onward travel to Kathmandu
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 1,400 m
ACCOMMODATION 3-star hotel in Kathmandu
MEALS Breakfast; lunch and dinner at own expense

We prepare for the return flight from Lukla. Depending on the operating schedule, you may fly directly to Kathmandu or land at Manthali Airport and continue by road.

After reaching your hotel, the remaining time is available for rest, shopping or exploring nearby streets. Check your luggage and onward travel arrangements before tomorrow’s departure. Flight timing remains dependent on the weather, airline operations and the return schedule.

Day 16: Departure from Kathmandu

International Departure & Airport Transfer — Kathmandu Departure — Final Farewell & Tribhuvan Airport Transfer

MAIN ACTIVITY Departure from Nepal
MEALS Breakfast

Your 16-day Lobuche East Climbing Trip ends today. After breakfast, you prepare for your onward journey. Depending on your departure time, there may be an opportunity for a final walk nearby before leaving the hotel.

Our representative accompanies you to Tribhuvan International Airport for your departure.

Read Before You Book

Before booking your Lobuche East Climbing Trip, consider daily walking, altitude, climbing equipment and summit-day effort. These details help you prepare and decide whether the programme suits your experience, fitness and expectations.

If you have questions or need preparation advice, feel free to email or WhatsApp our expedition team directly.

1. Trek Difficulty and Fitness

Very challenging combining trekking with a technical alpine climb above 6,000 m.

The Lobuche East Climbing Trip combines consecutive trekking days with a technical summit attempt above 6,000 metres. Expect steep rock, snow or ice and fixed-rope sections. The planned return to Pheriche adds a substantial descent after climbing, so endurance and concentration are essential throughout the day.

Previous mountain experience is valuable. Discuss your trekking background and climbing skills with us beforehand, including any practice needed with crampons and ropes.

Build endurance through hill walks and longer days carrying a daypack. Add strength work and practise using your boots and layers. Develop climbing skills before travelling, then review equipment and techniques with your guide before attempting the summit.

2. Altitude and Acclimatisation

Strategic acclimatisation in Namche and Dingboche before the 6,119 m summit.

Our itinerary includes acclimatisation days in Namche Bazaar and Dingboche. These give you time to adjust before continuing higher, but altitude illness can still affect you, regardless of fitness.

Tell your guide immediately about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not continue to a higher sleeping altitude while experiencing symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite resting at the same elevation, descent is necessary. Attempting the summit must remain secondary to your health.

3. Medical Conditions and Travel Insurance

Mandatory policy covering technical mountaineering up to 6,119 m with helicopter evacuation.

If you have an existing medical condition, seek advice from a healthcare professional familiar with high-altitude travel before booking. Discuss your planned route and any medicines you take.

Arrange insurance covering trekking and Lobuche East mountaineering to approximately 6,119 metres, including fixed ropes, snow, ice, medical treatment and emergency evacuation. Check activity exclusions, altitude limits, excess charges and the insurer’s assistance procedure. Carry policy details and emergency contacts throughout the journey.

4. Accommodation and Room Facilities

3 hotel nights in Kathmandu, mountain teahouses, and tented base camp.

You have three planned Kathmandu hotel nights, teahouses along the approach and a tented night at Lobuche Base Camp. The contingency-night location depends on the climbing schedule; confirm accommodation before booking.

Attached bathrooms are included at Phakding, Namche and Lukla. Higher lodges offer simpler rooms, while climbing camp uses tents. Confirm room and tent sharing, bedding and facilities before departure. Nights can be cold, including inside your accommodation.

5. Food and Dietary Requirements

Full board on trek, Kathmandu breakfast, hot tea/coffee, and fresh evening fruit.

Mountain meals include breakfast, lunch and dinner, three cups of tea or coffee daily and seasonal fruit after dinner during trekking stages.

Tell us about allergies or dietary requirements before booking so we can discuss what can be arranged along the route. Explain any strict requirements clearly, including concerns about shared cooking equipment.

6. Drinking Water

Drink boiled or properly treated water; avoid untreated tap and glacial streams.

Carry reusable bottles and drink boiled or appropriately treated water. Water from a clear stream or an untreated tap may still contain germs.

Boiling is the most reliable way to kill germs. If boiling is unavailable, filtration followed by disinfection offers broader protection than many portable filters alone. Follow the instructions for your treatment equipment or tablets.

7. Guide and Porter Support

Licensed trekking guide, climbing-team support, and dedicated porter team.

An English-speaking trekking guide and porters support the approach. Your climbing team provides instruction and route guidance. Confirm climbing-guide qualifications, staffing, porter allocation and any equipment charges with us before finalising your booking.

8. Showers, Charging and Communication

Attached bathrooms at lower stops; solar charging and Wi-Fi available at lodges.

Attached bathrooms are included in Phakding, Namche and Lukla. Confirm shower, charging and Wi-Fi allowances before departure. Laundry and phone calls are personal expenses. Facilities vary, while mobile reception and internet connections can be unreliable. Carry a power bank and prepare for periods without reliable contact.

9. Permits and Documents

All official permits arranged: Lobuche East climbing permit, Sagarmatha NP, and Khumbu entry.

We arrange the Lobuche East climbing permit, national park permit and local entry fee. Carry your passport, insurance details and copies of important documents. Confirm any additional paperwork before departure; your Nepal visa remains your responsibility.

10. Flight Delays and Extra Time

Contingency planning for Lukla mountain flights and Ramechhap road transfers.

Lukla flights can be delayed or cancelled because of weather. During peak periods, flights may use Manthali Airport in Ramechhap, requiring a road transfer.

Your return flight is on Day 15, before departure on Day 16. The mountain contingency day does not guarantee protection against Lukla flight delays.

How Weather Changes Along the Route

Elevation-driven temperature drops and micro-climates as you journey from Lukla to Lobuche East Summit (6,119 m).

LOWER KHUMBU • 2,610 M – 3,440 M

Lower Trails (Phakding & Namche Bazaar)

Phakding and Namche can feel warm in sunshine. The upper Khumbu, base camp and climbing route are colder, especially after sunset. The summit attempt starts before dawn, when exposure increases.

Warm daytime sunshine & sheltered pine valleys
GLACIAL & SUMMIT ZONE • 4,950 M – 6,119 M

High Camp & Summit Slopes (Lobuche East)

The summit attempt begins in darkness, when temperatures can be well below freezing. Wind increases exposure on the upper slopes. Use suitable mountaineering boots, insulated gloves and layers, with equipment checked before departure and again before the planned climb.

Sub-zero pre-dawn summit push, high wind chill & technical ice

Which Season Should You Choose?

Choose spring or autumn after discussing your experience, dates and current climbing conditions with us. Spring offers longer daylight and warming lower trails; autumn often brings clear mountain mornings. Winter and monsoon attempts require flexibility, preparation and careful route assessment.

SPRING CLIMBING • MARCH TO MAY

Choose Spring for Longer Days & Warming Trails

Spring offers longer daylight and warming lower trails. Lower forested trails feature blooming rhododendrons, while higher elevations enjoy progressively milder climbing windows toward May.

Best for: Climbers who prefer longer summit daylight windows, warmer lower approaches, and active pre-monsoon climbing energy.
AUTUMN CLIMBING • SEPTEMBER TO NOVEMBER

Choose Autumn for Clear Mountain Mornings

Autumn often brings clear mountain mornings. October and November provide exceptionally stable weather, crisp skies, and outstanding summit visibility across the Everest giants.

Best for: Climbers and photographers seeking crystal-clear panoramic views, dry rocky ridges, and peak atmospheric stability.

Lobuche East Climbing Trip FAQs

General & Route

Is this climb suitable if I have never climbed? ∨

Discuss your experience and preparation with us before booking. Lobuche East requires strong fitness and the ability to use mountaineering equipment under guidance. Previous mountain experience and technical practice help you prepare; camp instruction cannot replace that preparation.

What is the highest altitude on this itinerary? ∨

The summit objective is Lobuche East at approximately 6,119 metres. Kala Patthar reaches approximately 5,545 metres. The listed base camp is around 4,950 metres; confirm the actual climbing camp and contingency-night location before assessing overnight elevations.

Does this include an Everest Base Camp visit? ∨

The package name includes Everest Base Camp, but a visit is not assigned to a specific day. Confirm the visit and any changes needed to the route with us before booking; it should not be assumed.

Can we reach Kala Patthar for sunrise? ∨

Confirm the overnight stop and departure plan with us. Kala Patthar is approached through Gorak Shep, while the previous night is listed at Lobuche. The complete hike, sunrise timing and onward journey to climbing camp need an agreed plan.

Are acclimatisation days complete rest days? ∨

They usually include a shorter hike above Namche Bazaar or Dingboche, followed by a return to the same village to sleep. Your guide adjusts the hike according to conditions and how the group is feeling.

Does being fit protect me from altitude sickness? ∨

No. Good fitness helps with the walking demands, but it does not prevent altitude illness. Fit and experienced trekkers can also become unwell. Follow the acclimatisation schedule and report symptoms immediately instead of assuming fitness will protect you.

What should I do if I feel unwell at altitude? ∨

Tell your guide immediately about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not ascend to a higher sleeping altitude while experiencing symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite rest at the same elevation, descent is necessary.

Do I need to carry my main trekking bag? ∨

Porters carry your main trekking luggage. Confirm the allocation and allowance before departure. You carry your daypack containing water, weather protection, medicines and other daily essentials. Keep your passport, money, valuables and documents with you.

Do I need to bring personal equipment for the summit attempt? ∨

Personal mountaineering boots, crampons, a harness, helmet, ice axe and rope devices need separate arrangements. We provide loan sleeping bags, down jackets and duffel bags. Confirm personal equipment, rental costs, compatibility and fit with us before departure.

Can I book as a solo traveller? ∨

Send us your dates to discuss possible group or private arrangements. A solo price is not published. We will confirm availability, climbing support, porter allocation and a written quotation for your planned sixteen-day programme beforehand.

Is trekking insurance sufficient for Lobuche East climbing? ∨

Check your policy. Cover must include Lobuche East mountaineering to approximately 6,119 metres, fixed ropes, snow, ice, medical treatment and emergency evacuation. Trekking cover may exclude climbing or stop below the summit altitude, so confirm with your insurer.

What happens if weather prevents the planned summit attempt? ∨

Day 12 provides contingency time around the climb. Your guide decides whether to wait, change the plan or descend. Using this day can alter overnight stops and the return journey. It does not guarantee a second attempt or flight protection.
