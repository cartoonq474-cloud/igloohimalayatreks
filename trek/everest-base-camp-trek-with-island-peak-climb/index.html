Everest Base Camp Trek with Island Peak Climbing — 19 Days

Combine Kathmandu sightseeing with Everest Base Camp, Kala Patthar and an Island Peak attempt on this 19-day journey through the Khumbu. The trek follows Namche Bazaar, Tengboche and Dingboche before visiting Base Camp and returning towards the Imja Valley. Continue through Chhukung to a tented night at Island Peak Base Camp, with equipment practice before climbing. Acclimatisation days in Namche and Dingboche precede glacier travel, steep snow or ice and fixed-rope climbing on the planned summit day.

EBC Island Peak Climb Overview

Our 19-day EBC Island Peak Climb combines Kathmandu sightseeing and trek preparation with the Everest Base Camp trail and a planned Island Peak attempt. Visit Base Camp and hike Kala Patthar before returning towards Dingboche and Chhukung. Continue to a tented base camp for equipment practice and the climb, supported by a separate climbing guide.

After flying to Lukla, you follow the Dudh Koshi valley through Phakding, crossing suspension bridges before climbing to Namche Bazaar. Beyond Namche, the trail follows the hillside, descends to the river and climbs again to Tengboche. Its Buddhist monastery and views of Ama Dablam make this a memorable stop. Along the route, you pass Sherpa villages where monasteries, prayer wheels and mani walls reflect the community’s Buddhist traditions.

Beyond Dingboche, follow the trail through Lobuche to Gorak Shep for the Everest Base Camp visit. Hike Kala Patthar the next morning, then descend to Dingboche. The route continues up the Imja Valley to Chhukung and Island Peak Base Camp. After the summit attempt, return through Chhukung, Tengboche and Namche before the final Lukla stage.

Acclimatisation days in Namche and Dingboche give you additional nights at the same elevation before continuing higher. Island Peak adds glacier travel, steep snow or ice and fixed-rope climbing to the walking journey. Strong fitness and suitable climbing preparation are necessary. Your guide provides equipment practice at base camp, then reviews conditions and your progress before deciding whether to climb.

You stay in teahouses, a base-camp tent and a Kathmandu hotel. The 19-day programme covers your arrival, the trekking and climbing route, return travel and departure from Nepal.

Visit Everest Base Camp at 5,364 m, then hike Kala Patthar before continuing towards the Imja Valley and the Island Peak approach.

EBC Island Peak Climb Itinerary — Day by Day

Our 19-day itinerary includes Kathmandu sightseeing, Everest Base Camp, Kala Patthar and an Island Peak attempt, with two acclimatisation days. Walking and climbing times are approximate; weather, route conditions, health and pace affect timings.

Day 1: Arrival in Kathmandu

Trek stage — Approximately 1,400 m — 3-star hotel in Kathmandu

OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 1,400 m
ACCOMMODATION 3-star hotel in Kathmandu, twin sharing
MEALS INCLUDED Arrival-day meals at own expense

Welcome to Nepal. Our representative meets you at Tribhuvan International Airport and accompanies you to your hotel. After you settle in, we go through the trekking programme, check your essential equipment and discuss any questions you have.

Depending on your arrival time, you can explore the nearby streets of Thamel or rest after your flight. We also help with any final preparations before you leave for the Everest region.

Day 2: Kathmandu Sightseeing and Trek Preparation

Kathmandu sightseeing, trek briefing and equipment checks — Approximately 1,400 m — Planned Kathmandu hotel; confirm the additional night in your quotation

MAIN ACTIVITY Kathmandu sightseeing, trek briefing and equipment checks
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 1,400 m
ACCOMMODATION Planned Kathmandu hotel; confirm the additional night in your quotation
MEALS INCLUDED Breakfast; lunch and dinner at own expense

Spend the day sightseeing in Kathmandu, with time set aside for trek preparation. Your visits fit around the briefing, equipment checks and arrangements for the journey into Khumbu.

We review the route, flight arrangements and climbing equipment with you. Check boot fit, warm layers and rental needs. Confirm the sightseeing stops, transport, entry fees and guide arrangements in your quotation, then organise your kit before tomorrow’s departure.

Day 3: Fly to Lukla and Trek to Phakding

Approximately 3–4 hours — 2,610 m — Teahouse in Phakding

TIME ESTIMATE Approximately 3–4 hours
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE 2,610 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Phakding
MEALS INCLUDED Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Your mountain journey begins with a flight to Lukla at 2,860 m. Depending on airline operations and the season, the flight may depart from Kathmandu or Manthali Airport in Ramechhap.

After landing, you meet your trekking team and begin walking towards Phakding. The trail passes village homes, prayer wheels and forested slopes, with downhill stretches and shorter climbs along the way. Sections follow the Dudh Koshi River, introducing you to the valley you will explore over the coming days. We spend the night in Phakding.

Day 4: Trek from Phakding to Namche Bazaar

Approximately 6–7 hours — 3,440 m — Teahouse in Namche Bazaar

TIME ESTIMATE Approximately 6–7 hours
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE 3,440 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Namche Bazaar
MEALS INCLUDED Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We follow the Dudh Koshi through villages and pine forests, passing Benkar and Monjo before entering Sagarmatha National Park. Your guide handles the permit checks along the route.

Suspension bridges connect the valley trails, followed by a sustained uphill walk towards Namche. This final climb is the most demanding part of the day, so we allow time for breaks. Namche’s shops, lodges and houses spread across the hillside, making it an important trading town and a useful place to spend the next two nights.

Day 5: Acclimatisation Day in Namche Bazaar

Approximately 3–4 hours — 3,440 m — Teahouse in Namche Bazaar

TIME ESTIMATE Approximately 3–4 hours
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE 3,440 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Namche Bazaar
MEALS INCLUDED Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Around 3,880 m if visiting Hotel Everest View

We stay another night in Namche and take a shorter hike above the town. A usual option is the trail towards Hotel Everest View, where clear weather offers views of Everest and nearby peaks.

Your guide chooses the walk according to conditions and how the group is feeling. Afterwards, we return to Namche for the night. The afternoon gives you time to rest, visit the shops or stop at a café before continuing towards Tengboche tomorrow.

Day 6: Trek from Namche Bazaar to Tengboche

Approximately 5–6 hours — Around 3,860 m — Teahouse in Tengboche

TIME ESTIMATE Approximately 5–6 hours
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Around 3,860 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Tengboche
MEALS INCLUDED Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Leaving Namche, we follow the hillside trail before descending towards Phunki Tenga. Mountain views accompany sections of the walk, while the river marks the low point before the climb to Tengboche.

The uphill trail passes through pine and rhododendron forest. Once we reach the village, you can settle into the teahouse and explore the surroundings. Tengboche’s Buddhist monastery stands against the mountain backdrop of Ama Dablam. If it is open to visitors, your guide explains the visiting customs before you enter.

Day 7: Trek from Tengboche to Dingboche

Approximately 5–6 hours — 4,410 m — Teahouse in Dingboche

TIME ESTIMATE Approximately 5–6 hours
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE 4,410 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Dingboche
MEALS INCLUDED Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We descend through forest towards Debuche, cross the Imja Khola and continue through Pangboche. As the route gains height, the trees become fewer and the valley opens into slopes with low vegetation and stone buildings.

The trail passes Somare before reaching Dingboche in the Imja Valley. Today brings a noticeable gain in sleeping altitude, so we keep a steady pace throughout the walk. After checking into your teahouse, the remaining afternoon is available for rest before tomorrow’s acclimatisation hike.

Day 8: Acclimatisation Day in Dingboche

Approximately 4–5 hours, adjusted to conditions and health — 4,410 m — Teahouse in Dingboche

TIME ESTIMATE Approximately 4–5 hours, adjusted to conditions and health
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE 4,410 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Dingboche
MEALS INCLUDED Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Our second acclimatisation day includes a hike above Dingboche followed by another night in the village. The usual direction is towards the Nangkartshang viewpoint, with the turnaround point determined by conditions and the group’s progress.

The hillside gives you a broader view of Dingboche and the surrounding valley. We then return to the teahouse, leaving the afternoon for rest and preparation. Tomorrow, the route continues towards Lobuche, where the terrain becomes increasingly rocky and glacial.

Day 9: Trek from Dingboche to Lobuche

Approximately 5–6 hours — Around 4,910 m — Teahouse in Lobuche

TIME ESTIMATE Approximately 5–6 hours
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Around 4,910 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Lobuche
MEALS INCLUDED Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We leave Dingboche and cross open ground towards Thukla. After a break, the trail climbs more steeply to the memorial area above the village. The stone memorials commemorate climbers and Sherpas who lost their lives in the mountains.

Beyond this section, we continue beside the Khumbu Glacier towards Lobuche. Uneven ground and increasing altitude make the walking more demanding, even where the gradient eases. We settle into the teahouse and prepare for tomorrow’s journey to Everest Base Camp.

Day 10: Trek to Gorak Shep, Visit Everest Base Camp and Return

Approximately 7–8 hours — Gorak Shep, approximately 5,164 m — Teahouse in Gorak Shep

TIME ESTIMATE Approximately 7–8 hours
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Gorak Shep, approximately 5,164 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Gorak Shep
MEALS INCLUDED Breakfast, lunch and dinner

We start from Lobuche and follow the rocky trail towards Gorak Shep. After a break, we continue to Everest Base Camp, crossing uneven glacial ground along the Khumbu Glacier.

Reaching base camp brings you into the landscape used by Everest expedition teams. During the spring climbing season, tents and expedition activity may be visible. At other times, the glacier and surrounding mountains define the visit.

After spending time at base camp, we retrace the trail to Gorak Shep. Tonight’s stop puts us close to the starting point for tomorrow’s Kala Patthar hike.

Day 11: Hike to Kala Patthar and Trek Back to Dingboche

Allow approximately 8–10 hours for the full hike and descent — Dingboche, approximately 4,410 m — Teahouse in Dingboche

TIME ESTIMATE Allow approximately 8–10 hours for the full hike and descent
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Dingboche, approximately 4,410 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Dingboche
MEALS INCLUDED Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Gorak Shep–Kala Patthar–Lobuche–Dingboche

Kala Patthar viewpoint, approximately 5,545 m

We make an early start for Kala Patthar. The climb above Gorak Shep is steep in places, and the altitude makes it demanding. In clear weather, the viewpoint offers close views of Everest, Nuptse, Pumori and the surrounding Khumbu peaks.

After the hike, we return to Gorak Shep for breakfast and continue towards Dingboche. The route passes through Lobuche and the Thukla area before returning to the Imja Valley. It is another substantial walking day, ending with an overnight stay at a lower elevation.

Day 12: Trek from Dingboche to Chhukung

Approximately 3–4 hours — Approximately 4,730 m — Teahouse in Chhukung

TIME ESTIMATE Approximately 3–4 hours
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 4,730 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Chhukung
MEALS INCLUDED Breakfast, lunch and dinner

From Dingboche, follow the Imja Valley towards Chhukung. The stage still gains height, crossing open ground with views towards Lhotse, Ama Dablam and Island Peak when the weather allows. Walk steadily as the altitude increases.

We reach Chhukung and settle into a teahouse for the night. The afternoon gives you time to rest and organise your personal climbing equipment. Discuss any remaining kit questions with the team before tomorrow’s approach to base camp, where practical preparation precedes the attempt.

Day 13: Trek to Island Peak Base Camp and Climbing Preparation

Approximately 3–4 hours, followed by climbing preparation — Island Peak Base Camp, approximately 5,080 m — Tented camp at Island Peak Base Camp

TIME ESTIMATE Approximately 3–4 hours, followed by climbing preparation
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Island Peak Base Camp, approximately 5,080 m
ACCOMMODATION Tented camp at Island Peak Base Camp
MEALS INCLUDED Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Leaving Chhukung, follow rocky trails towards Island Peak Base Camp. The approach crosses open ground and moraine below the mountain. Although the walking stage is shorter, the altitude adds effort. We reach camp and settle into the tents.

Your climbing guide leads equipment checks and practical preparation, including crampons, an ice axe and rope systems. Check your boots, harness and warm layers, then organise your kit for the early start. After dinner, rest before tomorrow’s planned summit attempt.

Day 14: Attempt Island Peak Summit and Return to Chhukung

Trek stage — Chhukung, approximately 4,730 m — Teahouse in Chhukung

OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Chhukung, approximately 4,730 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Chhukung
MEALS INCLUDED Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Approximately 10–12 hours or longer for the climb and return to Chhukung

Island Peak summit, approximately 6,189 m

Rocky approach, glacier travel, steep snow or ice and fixed-rope sections

We start before dawn, following the climbing guide over rocky ground towards the glacier. Warm clothing, headtorches and correctly fitted equipment are essential for the long ascent and descent.

The route involves glacier travel and fixed ropes on steep snow or ice before the summit ridge. Your guide assesses conditions and progress throughout. If continuing becomes unsuitable, we turn back rather than force the attempt.

After the attempt, descend carefully to base camp and continue to Chhukung for the night. Save energy for the full return; reaching the summit is never guaranteed.

Day 15: Trek from Chhukung to Tengboche

Approximately 5–6 hours or longer, depending on pace — Approximately 3,860 m — Teahouse in Tengboche

TIME ESTIMATE Approximately 5–6 hours or longer, depending on pace
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 3,860 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Tengboche
MEALS INCLUDED Breakfast, lunch and dinner

From Chhukung, follow the Imja Valley down to Dingboche and continue towards Pangboche. The trail returns through familiar villages as we lose height. After the demanding climbing day, maintain a steady pace and allow time for breaks along the route.

Trees become more common as we descend through the valley towards Debuche. The final approach to Tengboche includes an uphill section, despite the overall loss of altitude. Settle into your teahouse and rest, with monastery visits depending on your energy, local access and time.

Day 16: Trek from Tengboche to Namche Bazaar

Approximately 5–6 hours — 3,440 m — Teahouse in Namche Bazaar

TIME ESTIMATE Approximately 5–6 hours
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE 3,440 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Namche Bazaar
MEALS INCLUDED Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Leaving Tengboche, descend through forest towards Phunki Tenga. After crossing the river, the trail climbs again before following the hillside towards Namche, with mountain views when the weather allows.

This stage combines descents with uphill sections, so there is still effort ahead as we return to the lower valley. Once in Namche, check into your teahouse. The rest of the afternoon gives you time to rest, visit nearby shops or organise your belongings before tomorrow’s final walk to Lukla.

Day 17: Trek from Namche Bazaar to Lukla

Approximately 7–8 hours — 2,860 m — Teahouse in Lukla

TIME ESTIMATE Approximately 7–8 hours
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE 2,860 m
ACCOMMODATION Teahouse in Lukla
MEALS INCLUDED Breakfast, lunch and dinner

Our final trekking day follows the route down from Namche through Jorsalle, Monjo and Phakding. You cross familiar suspension bridges and return through the forested sections of the Dudh Koshi valley.

The approach to Lukla includes uphill stretches, so there is still some effort ahead after the main descent. On arrival, we check into the teahouse for the last night in the mountains. The evening gives you time to organise your belongings and spend time with your guide and porter team.

Day 18: Fly from Lukla and Return to Kathmandu

Flight from Lukla and onward travel to Kathmandu — Approximately 1,400 m — 3-star hotel in Kathmandu

MAIN ACTIVITY Flight from Lukla and onward travel to Kathmandu
OVERNIGHT ALTITUDE Approximately 1,400 m
ACCOMMODATION 3-star hotel in Kathmandu
MEALS INCLUDED Breakfast and farewell dinner

We prepare for the return flight from Lukla. Depending on the operating schedule, you may fly directly to Kathmandu or land at Manthali Airport and continue by road.

After reaching your hotel, the remaining time is free for rest, shopping or exploring nearby areas. We share a farewell dinner in Kathmandu, bringing the trekking programme to a close. Flight timing remains dependent on weather and airline operations.

Day 19: Farewell and Airport Transfer

Departure from Nepal — Khumbu Region — Lodge

MAIN ACTIVITY Departure from Nepal
MEALS INCLUDED Breakfast

Your 19-day EBC Island Peak Climb ends today. After breakfast, you prepare for your onward journey. Depending on your departure time, there may be an opportunity for a final walk nearby before leaving the hotel.

Our representative accompanies you to Tribhuvan International Airport for your departure.

Read Before You Book

Before booking your EBC Island Peak Climb, consider daily walking, altitude, climbing equipment and summit-day effort. These details help you prepare and decide whether the programme suits your experience, fitness and expectations.

1. Trek Difficulty and Fitness

The EBC Island Peak Climb combines consecutive trekking days with a technical summit attempt above 6,000 metres. The climbing route involves glacier travel, steep snow or ice and fixed-rope sections. Summit day includes the full return to Chhukung, requiring endurance and concentration throughout the long descent.

Previous mountain experience is valuable. Discuss your trekking background and climbing skills with us beforehand, including any practice needed with crampons and ropes.

Build endurance through hill walks and longer days carrying a daypack. Add strength work and practise using your boots and layers. Develop climbing skills before travelling, then review equipment and techniques with your guide before attempting the summit.

2. Altitude and Acclimatisation

Our itinerary includes acclimatisation days in Namche Bazaar and Dingboche. These give you time to adjust before continuing higher, but altitude illness can still affect you, regardless of fitness.

Tell your guide immediately about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not continue to a higher sleeping altitude while experiencing symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite resting at the same elevation, descent is necessary. Attempting the summit must remain secondary to your health.

3. Medical Conditions and Travel Insurance

If you have an existing medical condition, seek advice from a healthcare professional familiar with high-altitude travel before booking. Discuss your planned route and any medicines you take.

Arrange insurance covering trekking and Island Peak mountaineering to approximately 6,189 metres, including glacier travel, fixed ropes, medical treatment and emergency evacuation. Check activity exclusions, altitude limits, excess charges and the insurer’s emergency assistance procedure. Carry policy details and emergency contacts throughout Nepal.

4. Accommodation and Room Facilities

The itinerary has three Kathmandu hotel nights, fourteen guesthouse nights and one tented night at Island Peak Base Camp. Confirm the additional city night’s hotel arrangements and cost with your quotation.

Attached toilets are included at Lukla, Phakding and Namche. Higher lodges have simpler rooms, while base camp uses tented facilities. Rooms and tents can be cold at night. Confirm room arrangements, bedding and any upgrades before departure.

5. Food and Dietary Requirements

Breakfast, lunch and dinner are included during trekking and climbing. Meals include tea or coffee, with seasonal fruit and Snickers after dinner.

Tell us about allergies or dietary requirements before booking so we can discuss what can be arranged along the route. Explain any strict requirements clearly, including concerns about shared cooking equipment.

6. Drinking Water

Carry reusable bottles and drink boiled or appropriately treated water. Water from a clear stream or an untreated tap may still contain germs.

Boiling is the most reliable way to kill germs. If boiling is unavailable, filtration followed by disinfection offers broader protection than many portable filters alone. Follow the instructions for your treatment equipment or tablets.

7. Guide and Porter Support

Our government-licensed, English-speaking trekking guide is first-aid trained. An additional trekking guide joins groups above seven. A separate climbing guide supports the summit attempt, while porters carry your main bags during the approach.

8. Showers, Charging and Communication

Attached toilets are included in Lukla, Phakding and Namche. Ask about shower, Wi-Fi and charging allowances before departure. Laundry and phone calls are personal expenses. Facilities vary, and mobile reception can be unreliable. Carry a power bank and tell family you may be out of contact.

9. Permits and Documents

We arrange the Island Peak climbing permit, national park permit and local entry fee. Carry your passport, insurance details and document copies. Check climbing documentation with us before departure; your Nepal visa remains your own responsibility.

10. Flight Delays and Extra Time

Lukla flights can be delayed or cancelled because of weather. During peak periods, flights may use Manthali Airport in Ramechhap, requiring a road transfer.

Your return flight is on Day 18, before departure on Day 19. Discuss flexibility and potential costs if weather delays flights or the climb.

How Weather Changes Along the Route

Elevation gradients: Valley approaches (2,600m) to summit push (6,189m)

Phakding and Namche can feel warm in sunshine. The upper Khumbu, Imja Valley and base camp are colder, especially after sunset. The summit attempt starts before dawn, when exposure increases.

The summit attempt begins in darkness, when temperatures can be well below freezing. Wind increases exposure on the upper slopes. Use suitable mountaineering boots, insulated gloves and layers, with equipment checked before departure and again before the planned climb.

Which Season Should You Choose?

Choose spring or autumn after discussing your experience, dates and current climbing conditions with us. Spring offers longer daylight and warming lower trails; autumn often brings clear mountain mornings. Winter and monsoon attempts require flexibility, preparation and careful route assessment.

EBC Island Peak Climb FAQs

Frequently asked questions regarding fitness requirements, high-altitude acclimatisation, technical climbing gear, and booking arrangements.

General & Route

What is the highest altitude on this itinerary? ∨

The planned summit is Island Peak at approximately 6,189 metres. Gorak Shep, around 5,164 metres, is your highest overnight stop. Island Peak Base Camp is approximately 5,080 metres, and you return to Chhukung after the attempt.

Can I see Mount Everest from Base Camp? ∨

Everest’s summit is generally hidden behind nearby ridges from Base Camp itself. Kala Patthar provides the clearer view of Everest that many travellers expect. Our itinerary includes this viewpoint, although mountain visibility depends on the weather.

Do we sleep at Everest Base Camp? ∨

No. You visit Base Camp during the day and return to a teahouse in Gorak Shep for the night. The following morning includes the planned Kala Patthar hike before descending towards Dingboche. Camping at Base Camp is not included.

Are acclimatisation days complete rest days? ∨

They usually include a shorter hike above Namche Bazaar or Dingboche, followed by a return to the same village to sleep. Your guide adjusts the hike according to conditions and how the group is feeling.

Is this climb suitable if I have never climbed? ∨

Discuss your experience and preparation with us before booking. Island Peak requires strong fitness and the ability to use mountaineering equipment under guidance. Equipment practice is included, while mountain experience and training help prepare you for the climb.

Do I need to bring personal equipment for the summit attempt? ∨

Personal mountaineering boots, crampons, a harness, helmet, ice axe and rope devices need separate arrangements. We provide loan sleeping bags, down jackets and duffel bags. Confirm personal equipment, rental costs, compatibility and fit with us before departure.

What happens if weather prevents the Island Peak attempt? ∨

Your climbing guide reviews conditions and discusses whether to wait, change the plan or turn back. The programme has no extra summit day. Additional mountain days need revised arrangements and may cost more, with return flights and departure also reconsidered.

Does being fit protect me from altitude sickness? ∨

No. Good fitness helps with the walking demands, but it does not prevent altitude illness. Fit and experienced trekkers can also become unwell. Follow the acclimatisation schedule and report symptoms immediately instead of assuming fitness will protect you.

What should I do if I feel unwell at altitude? ∨

Tell your guide immediately about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not ascend to a higher sleeping altitude while experiencing symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite rest at the same elevation, descent is necessary.

Is standard trekking insurance enough for Island Peak? ∨

Check your policy. Cover must include Island Peak mountaineering to approximately 6,189 metres, glacier travel, fixed ropes, medical treatment and emergency evacuation. Trekking cover may exclude climbing activities or stop below the summit altitude, so ask your insurer.

Do I need to carry my main trekking bag? ∨

A porter carries your main trekking luggage, with one porter shared between two trekkers. You carry your daypack containing water, weather protection, medicines and other daily essentials. Keep your passport, money and valuables with you.

Can I book as a solo traveller? ∨

Yes. Published solo rates are available. Send us your dates so we can discuss group or private arrangements, then confirm climbing-guide support, porter allocation, equipment costs and a written quotation for your full nineteen-day programme.
