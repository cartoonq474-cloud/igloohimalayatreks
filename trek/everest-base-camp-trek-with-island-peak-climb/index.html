Everest Base Camp Trek with Island Peak Climb Overview

The Everest Base Camp Trek with Island Peak Climb (19 Days) is widely celebrated as the ultimate Himalayan adventure—combining the historic pilgrimage to Everest Base Camp (5,364 m) and sunrise over Kala Patthar (5,545 m) with the thrill of summiting an authentic 6,189-meter Himalayan mountain, Island Peak (Imja Tse).

Rising like an island in a vast sea of ice in the Chhukung Valley beneath the sheer 3,000-meter south face of Lhotse, Island Peak is the ideal first 6,000-meter peak for ambitious trekkers. What sets our 19-day itinerary apart is its uncompromising acclimatization strategy. Rather than rushing directly to the mountain, you complete the full Everest Base Camp circuit first, spending 10 days ascending progressively to Namche Bazaar, Tengboche, Dingboche, Lobuche, and Gorak Shep.

By the time you drop into the Chhukung Valley on Day 11 to attend our hands-on technical glacier clinic, your body is fully adapted to extreme altitudes. Equipped with mechanical ascenders (jumars), 12-point crampons, and ropes, you will ascend the thrilling 45-degree headwall and traverse the exposed knife-edge summit ridge under the direct care of our certified IFMGA/NNMGA Sherpa climbing guides, greeting sunrise from 6,189 meters with panoramic vistas of Lhotse, Makalu, and Ama Dablam.

Summit Island Peak (Imja Tse, 6,189 m / 20,305 ft)

Conquer one of the Himalayas' most iconic trekking peaks with thrilling fixed-rope climbing, 45-degree headwall ascents, and an exhilarating knife-edge summit ridge.

Touch Everest Base Camp (5,364 m) Landmark

Stand at the foot of the legendary Khumbu Icefall, surrounded by towering seracs, expedition camps, and the 7,861-meter sheer rock face of Nuptse.

Golden Sunrise Ascent of Kala Patthar (5,545 m)

Flawless Multi-Stage Acclimatization & Summit Reserve Day

Trekking EBC and Kala Patthar first builds superior red blood cell count before attempting 6,189 m, backed by an essential weather contingency reserve day.

19-Day Detailed Expedition Itinerary

Carefully paced for optimal hematocrit production, glacier safety, and 6,189m summit success.

Day 1: Arrival in Kathmandu & Transfer to Hotel

Tribhuvan International Airport (TIA) – 1,400 m / 4,593 ft – Private Transfer & Orientation

30–45 min transfer
6 km / 3.7 miles
Deluxe 3-Star Hotel in Thamel (Kathmandu)
Welcome Dinner with Traditional Cultural Show (D)

Arrive at Tribhuvan International Airport in Kathmandu. Our Igloo Himalaya Treks representative warmly welcomes you outside the arrivals terminal and transfers you by private vehicle to your hotel in Thamel. After settling in, meet your expedition climbing leader for an in-depth pre-trip briefing. We review the full 19-day schedule, verify your NMA climbing permits, and inspect personal mountaineering gear (boots, crampons, harness, jumar, ice axe). Enjoy an authentic Nepali welcome feast with live folk dance performances in the evening.

Day 2: Scenic Flight to Lukla & Trek to Phakding

Lukla (2,840 m) to Phakding (2,610 m) – 2,610 m / 8,563 ft – 3-4 hrs trek

35 min flight + 3-4 hrs trek
7.8 km / 4.8 miles
Authentic Teahouse / Mountain Lodge in Phakding
Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner Included (B, L, D)

Early morning transfer to the domestic airport for the thrilling 35-minute flight to Tenzing-Hillary Airport in Lukla (2,840 m). Enjoy breathtaking aerial views of the snow-capped Himalayan crest. Upon landing, meet our dedicated Sherpa porters who organize the expedition loads. Begin the trek descending gently along the Dudh Koshi (Milk River) Valley, passing traditional Sherpa farming hamlets, prayer wheels, and ancient mani stones to reach Phakding for our first overnight stay.

Day 3: Trek Phakding to Namche Bazaar via Hillary Suspension Bridge

Phakding to Namche Bazaar – 3,440 m / 11,286 ft – 5-6 hrs trek

5–6 hrs trek
10.4 km / 6.5 miles
Mountain Lodge in Namche Bazaar
Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner Included (B, L, D)

A stunning and energetic day of trekking. We crisscross the roaring Dudh Koshi River over soaring steel suspension bridges draped with colorful prayer flags through Benkar, Monjo, and Jorsalle. Enter Sagarmatha National Park where permits are verified. Cross the dramatic double-tiered Hillary Suspension Bridge suspended high above the river confluence, then tackle the steady zig-zag pine forest climb to Namche Bazaar (3,440 m), the bustling amphitheater capital of the Sherpa homeland.

Day 4: Acclimatization Hike to Hotel Everest View & Khumjung Village

Namche Acclimatization Day – 3,880 m viewpoint (Sleep: 3,440 m) – 4-5 hrs hike

4–5 hrs active hike
5.5 km / 3.4 miles
Mountain Lodge in Namche Bazaar
Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner Included (B, L, D)

Vital active acclimatization following the mountaineering golden rule of 'climb high, sleep low'. We ascend the steep ridge above Namche, passing the Syangboche airstrip to reach the legendary Hotel Everest View (3,880 m). From the sun terrace, savor hot ginger tea while absorbing your first close panoramas of Mount Everest (8,848.86 m), Lhotse, Nuptse, and the iconic pyramid of Ama Dablam. Continue down into Khumjung village to visit the local monastery housing the historic Yeti scalp and Edmund Hillary's Khumjung School before returning to Namche.

Day 5: Trek Namche Bazaar to Tengboche Monastery

Namche Bazaar to Tengboche – 3,867 m / 12,687 ft – 5-6 hrs trek

5–6 hrs trek
9.2 km / 5.7 miles
Mountain Lodge in Tengboche
Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner Included (B, L, D)

Walk along wide, scenic cliffside balconies high above the Dudh Koshi valley with unhindered views of Everest, Nuptse, and Ama Dablam. Spot Himalayan thar grazing on vertical grassy cliffs. Descend steeply through rhododendron woods to Phunki Tenga (3,250 m) for lunch by the river. In the afternoon, embark on the steady 2-hour forest ascent to the sacred ridge of Tengboche (3,867 m). Visit Tengboche Monastery, the spiritual centerpiece of the Khumbu, and attend the monks' afternoon chanting puja surrounded by snow peaks.

Day 6: Trek Tengboche to Dingboche via Pangboche

Tengboche to Dingboche – 4,410 m / 14,468 ft – 5-6 hrs trek

5–6 hrs trek
10.8 km / 6.7 miles
Mountain Lodge in Dingboche
Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner Included (B, L, D)

Descend through birch and rhododendron groves to Deboche, crossing the Imja Khola river on a suspended metal bridge. Follow the trail gently uphill past thousands of carved mani stones to Pangboche (3,985 m), standing directly beneath the imposing north face of Ama Dablam. Above Pangboche, trees vanish as we enter high alpine scrub and tundra. Follow the widening Imja Valley past Shomare into Dingboche (4,410 m), a picturesque village protected by stone walls built to shelter barley fields and grazing yaks.

Day 7: Second Acclimatization Hike to Nangkartshang Peak (5,083 m)

Dingboche Acclimatization – 5,083 m summit ridge (Sleep: 4,410 m) – 4-5 hrs hike

4–5 hrs active hike
4.8 km / 3.0 miles
Mountain Lodge in Dingboche
Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner Included (B, L, D)

A pivotal acclimatization milestone designed to supercharge your blood oxygen capacity before higher altitudes. Climb the rugged ridgeline rising directly north of Dingboche to Nangkartshang Peak (5,083 m). As you gain elevation, an extraordinary Himalayan panorama unfolds: Makalu (8,485 m, the world's 5th highest mountain), Lhotse, Island Peak, Cho Oyu, Baruntse, Taboche, and the sharp fluted ice face of Ama Dablam. Return to Dingboche for an afternoon of rest, hydration, and teahouse bakery relaxation.

Day 8: Trek Dingboche to Lobuche via Thokla Pass Memorials

Dingboche to Lobuche – 4,940 m / 16,207 ft – 5-6 hrs trek

5–6 hrs trek
7.9 km / 4.9 miles
Mountain Lodge in Lobuche
Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner Included (B, L, D)

Trek across the high alpine moorland to Dughla (4,620 m) at the snout of the massive Khumbu Glacier. From here, tackle the steep, boulder-strewn ascent to the crest of Thokla Pass (4,830 m). Here lies the poignant memorial plateau dotted with stone chortens commemorating mountaineers and Sherpas who lost their lives on Everest expeditions, including Scott Fischer and Babu Chiri Sherpa. Continue along the lateral moraine of the Khumbu Glacier to the windswept outpost of Lobuche (4,940 m).

Day 9: Trek Lobuche to Gorak Shep & Hike to Everest Base Camp (5,364 m)

Lobuche to Gorak Shep & EBC – 5,364 m / 17,598 ft – 7-8 hrs trek

7–8 hrs trek
12.8 km / 8.0 miles round-trip
Mountain Lodge in Gorak Shep (5,164 m)
Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner Included (B, L, D)

An unforgettable, bucket-list day. Follow the undulating, rocky crest of the Khumbu Glacier moraine to Gorak Shep (5,164 m), the highest permanent settlement on the route. Drop heavy daypacks and enjoy a warm soup lunch before continuing onto the active glacial bed toward Everest Base Camp (5,364 m). Hike alongside turquoise meltwater pools, towering seracs, and crumbling moraines to touch the iconic painted rock of Everest Base Camp. Stand directly beneath the thundering Khumbu Icefall and gaze up at Nuptse's 7,861m vertical rock wall before returning to Gorak Shep.

Day 10: Sunrise Ascent of Kala Patthar (5,545 m) & Trek Down to Dingboche

Kala Patthar (5,545 m) to Dingboche – 5,545 m summit – 6-7 hrs trek

6–7 hrs trek
14.5 km / 9.0 miles
Mountain Lodge in Dingboche
Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner Included (B, L, D)

Pre-dawn 4:30 AM alpine departure to climb Kala Patthar (5,545 m / 18,192 ft). In the sub-zero chill, slowly ascend the steep switchbacks to the summit ridge adorned with prayer flags. As dawn breaks, witness the sunrise illuminating the peak of Mount Everest (8,848.86 m), South Col, Nuptse, Pumori, and Changtse in fiery alpine glow. Descend to Gorak Shep for breakfast, then trek back down the Khumbu valley past Lobuche and Dughla into the warmer, oxygen-rich pastures of Dingboche.

Day 11: Trek Dingboche to Chhukung & Pre-Climbing Glacier Clinic

Dingboche to Chhukung – 4,730 m / 15,518 ft – 3 hrs trek + 2 hrs clinic

3 hrs trek + 2 hrs clinic
4.8 km / 3.0 miles
Mountain Lodge in Chhukung
Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner Included (B, L, D)

A relaxed morning trek traversing eastward into the Imja Valley beneath the colossal south face of Lhotse and Nuptse. Arrive in Chhukung (4,730 m) by midday. In the afternoon, our certified Sherpa climbing guides conduct an essential, mandatory mountaineering training clinic. You will practice wearing climbing harnesses, tying figure-8 knots, ascending fixed lines using jumars (mechanical ascenders), transitioning safety carabiners across anchors, and abseiling (rappelling) down steep rock and moraine slopes using figure-8 descenders.

Day 12: Trek Chhukung to Island Peak Base Camp & Camp Setup

Chhukung to Island Peak Base Camp – 5,200 m / 17,060 ft – 3-4 hrs trek

3–4 hrs trek
5.6 km / 3.5 miles
Expedition Tented Camp at Island Peak Base Camp
Freshly Cooked Expedition Camp Meals (B, L, D)

Trek along the lateral moraine of the Lhotse Glacier, then cross the dry moraine basin of the Imja and Lhotse Shar glaciers to Island Peak Base Camp (Pareshaya Gyab, 5,200 m). Settle into sturdy four-season high-altitude expedition tents pitched beneath the towering peak. Enjoy a freshly cooked, high-calorie lunch prepared by our camp chef. Rest, re-hydrate, perform a final gear check (fitting crampons to mountaineering boots, headlamps with fresh batteries, thermal summit clothing), and go to sleep early ahead of the midnight summit push.

Day 13: SUMMIT DAY: Island Peak (6,189 m / 20,305 ft) & Return to Chhukung

Island Peak Summit (6,189 m) – 6,189 m / 20,305 ft – 10-12 hrs alpine climb

10–12 hrs climbing & descent
10.2 km / 6.3 miles round-trip
Mountain Lodge in Chhukung
Summit Snacks, Camp Lunch & Celebration Dinner (B, L, D)

Summit day begins at 12:30 AM with hot tea and porridge. We set off at 1:30 AM by headlamp, scrambling up the steep gully and rock ridge past High Camp to Crampon Point (5,600 m). Put on crampons, tie into the rope team, and cross the crevassed glacier under our Sherpa guides' leadership. Ascend the thrilling 45-degree snow and ice headwall using fixed dynamic ropes and jumars, topping out onto the exhilarating knife-edge summit ridge. Step onto the summit of Island Peak (Imja Tse, 6,189 m / 20,305 ft) as sunrise washes the Himalayas in crimson light! Marvel at the sheer south wall of Lhotse (8,516 m) soaring just 3 km away, Makalu (8,485 m), Baruntse, and Ama Dablam. Descend carefully on fixed ropes, pack up Base Camp, and walk back to celebratory hot showers and beds in Chhukung.

Day 14: Contingency / Weather Reserve Day

Summit Weather Reserve Day – 4,730 m / 15,518 ft – Flexible Reserve Day

Flexible / Rest day
0 km (Reserve)
Mountain Lodge in Chhukung
Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner Included (B, L, D)

A built-in reserve day ensuring maximum summit success. If severe high-altitude weather, high summit winds, or personal acclimatization delayed our summit bid on Day 13, today is utilized for the summit attempt. If the summit was achieved successfully on schedule, this day serves as a deeply relaxing recovery day in Chhukung, celebrating with the guide team, resting weary legs, or exploring the dramatic views toward Chhukung Ri.

Day 15: Trek Chhukung to Pangboche via Dingboche

Chhukung to Pangboche – 3,930 m / 12,894 ft – 4-5 hrs trek

4–5 hrs trek
11.2 km / 7.0 miles
Mountain Lodge in Pangboche
Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner Included (B, L, D)

With the summit triumph behind us and oxygen levels increasing with every step downward, we descend along the Imja Valley. Stop in Dingboche for hot espresso and fresh apple pie. Continue downhill following the Imja Khola river into the ancient Sherpa village of Pangboche (3,930 m). In the afternoon, visit Pangboche Monastery, reputed to be the oldest Buddhist monastery in the entire Khumbu valley (dating back over 350 years), surrounded by ancient juniper groves and prayer flags.

Day 16: Trek Pangboche to Namche Bazaar via Tengboche

Pangboche to Namche Bazaar – 3,440 m / 11,286 ft – 5-6 hrs trek

5–6 hrs trek
13.0 km / 8.1 miles
Mountain Lodge in Namche Bazaar
Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner Included (B, L, D)

Descend through birch forests to Deboche, then climb back up to Tengboche Monastery for a final breathtaking gaze back at Everest and Ama Dablam. Descend steeply to Phunki Tenga and cross the suspension bridge over the Dudh Koshi. A steady ascent leads past Sanasa, where the trail merges onto the wide balcony path winding into Namche Bazaar. Enjoy long hot showers, browse mountaineering shops, and celebrate in Namche's cozy pubs and bakeries.

Day 17: Trek Namche Bazaar to Lukla via Phakding

Namche Bazaar to Lukla – 2,840 m / 9,317 ft – 6-7 hrs trek

6–7 hrs trek
18.2 km / 11.3 miles
Mountain Lodge in Lukla
Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner Included (B, L, D)

Our final day on the trekking trails! Descend steeply through pine forests to the Hillary Suspension Bridge and exit Sagarmatha National Park at Monjo. Continue down the valley through Phakding, crossing several suspension bridges along the rushing river. A final gentle 45-minute uphill trek brings us through the Lukla ceremonial archway. In the evening, host a festive farewell dinner and tip presentation for our hardworking Sherpa guides and porters who made your summit victory possible.

Day 18: Morning Flight from Lukla to Kathmandu & Hotel Transfer

Lukla to Kathmandu Flight – 1,400 m / 4,593 ft – Flight & Celebration

35 min flight + transfer
135 km flight
Deluxe 3-Star Hotel in Thamel (Kathmandu)
Breakfast & Farewell Dinner in Kathmandu (B, D)

Board the early morning flight from Lukla back to Kathmandu (or Manthali airport with private road transfer). Transfer to your hotel in Thamel. Enjoy a hot bath, comfortable city amenities, and a free afternoon for souvenir shopping, exploring historic Durbar Square, or relaxing. In the evening, gather for an official celebration banquet where you receive your official Nepal Mountaineering Association (NMA) Island Peak Summit Climbing Certificate.

Day 19: Final Departure from Kathmandu

Kathmandu – 1,400 m / 4,593 ft – Airport Farewell Transfer

30–45 min private transfer
6 km / 3.7 miles
None (Departure)
Breakfast Included (B)

Enjoy breakfast at the hotel. Depending on your flight schedule, take some last-minute photos or enjoy a quiet coffee in the gardens of Thamel. Three hours prior to your scheduled international flight departure, our representative transfers you by private car to Tribhuvan International Airport (TIA) for your journey home, forever carrying the triumph of standing on a 6,189-meter Himalayan summit.

1. Alpine Grade PD+ & Technical Climbing Demands

Understand the fixed rope techniques, 45° snow headwall, and summit ridge requirements.

Island Peak (Imja Tse, 6,189 m / 20,305 ft) is graded Alpine PD+ (Peu Difficile / A Little Difficult) under the international French alpine grading scale. While it is classified as a 'trekking peak' by the Nepal Mountaineering Association, it is a genuine mountaineering endeavor requiring basic technical climbing skills.

The technical challenges break down into three primary sections:

  • Crampon Point to Glacier (5,600 m): Scrambling over steep, loose rock gullies and scree requiring sturdy footwork in cold darkness.
  • Glacier Crossing & Crevasses (5,800 m): Navigating undulating snow slopes roped together with safety teammates, occasionally crossing snow bridges or fixed ladder ladders spanning glacial crevasses.
  • The 45-Degree Headwall & Summit Ridge (6,000 m to 6,189 m): Ascending a 100-meter wall of compacted snow and ice using fixed safety lines and a mechanical jumar (ascender). Topping out onto an airy, exposed knife-edge snow ridge where disciplined balance and clipping between safety anchors are mandatory.

Prior mountaineering experience is beneficial but not strictly mandatory. Trekkers in outstanding cardiovascular condition with strong mental resilience can successfully summit under the direct supervision of our certified Sherpa climbing leaders.

2. Why Trekking to EBC First Guarantees Summit Success

How reaching Everest Base Camp and Kala Patthar creates the ultimate physiological advantage.

Many discount operators attempt Island Peak in just 14 or 15 days by trekking directly to Chhukung. This shortcut leads to high failure rates because the human body requires time to produce adequate red blood cells and adapt to less than 50% sea-level oxygen.

Our 19-day itinerary combines the full Everest Base Camp circuit first for three strategic reasons:

  1. Staged Acclimatization Peaks: You spend progressive nights at Namche (3,440 m), Tengboche (3,867 m), Dingboche (4,410 m), and Lobuche (4,940 m), reaching Kala Patthar (5,545 m) and EBC (5,364 m) on Days 9 and 10.
  2. Elevated Baseline Hematocrit: By the time you drop down into Chhukung (4,730 m) on Day 11, your body feels energetic and strong because you have already acclimatized above 5,500 meters.
  3. Superior Summit Success: Our client summit success rate on this 19-day combined route exceeds 88%, compared to under 60% on shortened itineraries.

3. Mandatory Hands-on Glacier Training Clinic in Chhukung

What to expect during our practical afternoon mountaineering workshop on Day 11.

On Day 11 upon arriving in Chhukung, our certified IFMGA/NNMGA Sherpa climbing guides conduct a mandatory 2- to 3-hour practical training clinic on natural moraine and rock slopes. Every climber must demonstrate proficiency before advancing to Base Camp.

The clinic covers:

  • Harness Rigging & Cowstails: Correct fitting of alpine harnesses and attachment of twin dynamic safety cowstails.
  • Jumar Operation: Ascending fixed dynamic ropes smoothly using mechanical ascenders, maintaining proper thumb release technique, and coordinating arm thrust with foot stepping.
  • Safety Anchor Transitions: The critical skill of clipping past intermediate snow bar anchors using two locking carabiners without ever unclipping from the safety system.
  • Controlled Abseiling (Rappelling): Descending steep angles using a figure-8 descender or ATC device, managing brake-hand control, and safe stance positioning.

4. Island Peak Base Camp Tented Camp Living Conditions

What living and sleeping at 5,200 meters in expedition mountain tents is really like.

While the rest of the trek utilizes cozy wooden teahouses, Island Peak Base Camp (Pareshaya Gyab, 5,200 m) is a genuine wilderness mountaineering camp situated on the rocky moraine basin below the glacier.

Our expedition setup includes:

  • Sleeping Quarters: High-grade four-season North Face / Mountain Hardwear expedition dome tents shared between two climbers, equipped with insulated foam sleeping pads.
  • Camp Dining & Kitchen: A dedicated dining tent with camp stools and tables, accompanied by our professional expedition cook team who prepares hot soups, pasta, garlic potatoes, and tea.
  • Sanitation: A private, clean expedition toilet tent dug over deep pit facilities adhering to strict Leave No Trace environmental standards.
  • Cold Management: Temperatures at Base Camp frequently plunge to -10°C to -18°C at night. A four-season down sleeping bag rated to -20°C is strictly mandatory.

5. Dedicated Summit Reserve Day & Weather Window Strategy

How our built-in Day 14 contingency protects your summit bid against mountain weather.

High-altitude mountaineering is strictly governed by jet stream winds and weather systems. Gale-force summit ridge winds above 50 km/h or sudden snowfall can make the knife-edge ridge traverse dangerous.

Our itinerary includes a dedicated Weather Reserve Day (Day 14):

  • If Day 13 experiences high winds or heavy cloud, the team remains safely at Base Camp or Chhukung, resting and waiting for the front to pass.
  • We execute the summit push in the early hours of Day 14 under stabilized barometric conditions.
  • If the summit is successfully achieved on Day 13 as planned, Day 14 provides an unhurried, relaxing rest day to celebrate with guides and take in the Chhukung valley views.

6. Mandatory Mountaineering Insurance & Evacuation Protocols

Essential policy clauses required for climbing up to 6,200 meters in Nepal.

Standard travel insurance policies specifically exclude mountaineering and any activity above 4,000 meters. For this expedition, you must carry a specialized high-altitude mountaineering policy that explicitly covers:

  • Maximum Altitude: Coverage valid up to at least 6,200 meters (20,341 ft).
  • Activity Classification: High-altitude trekking and peak climbing using ropes, crampons, and ice axes.
  • Emergency Helicopter Evacuation: Uncapped emergency medical evacuation by helicopter directly from Island Peak Base Camp or Khumbu mountain lodges to Kathmandu hospitals.

Recommended insurers include Global Rescue, Ripcord, World Nomads (Explorer plan with mountaineering rider), and the British Mountaineering Council (BMC). A copy of your insurance certificate and 24/7 emergency dispatch phone number must be submitted during pre-trek registration.

7. Renting Technical Mountaineering Gear in Chhukung vs Kathmandu

What to buy, what to bring from home, and what can be easily rented locally.

You do not need to invest thousands of dollars purchasing technical climbing hardware that you may only use once. High-quality mountaineering equipment is widely available for rent:

  • Chhukung Gear Rental Hub: The village of Chhukung features well-stocked climbing rental stores operated by veteran Sherpas. You can rent a complete climbing package (double plastic/hybrid boots, crampons, harness, jumar, ice axe, helmet, carabiners, figure-8) for approximately $100 to $130 USD total for the climbing days.
  • Advantage of Renting in Chhukung: You don't have to carry heavy plastic mountaineering boots for 10 days over the trekking route from Lukla; you pick them up directly before Base Camp.
  • What You Must Bring Yourself: Broken-in trekking boots, thermal base layers, heavy down jacket, Gore-Tex hardshell outerwear, high-altitude mittens, category 4 glacier glasses, and a -20°C sleeping bag.

8. Lukla Flight Operations & Ramechhap Airport Logistics

Seasonal aviation shifts between Kathmandu (TIA) and Ramechhap (Manthali).

Flights into Lukla's Tenzing-Hillary Airport (2,840 m) operate strictly under Visual Flight Rules (VFR) and are subject to weather conditions. During peak trekking seasons (Spring: March–May; Autumn: October–November), the Civil Aviation Authority of Nepal (CAAN) diverts all Lukla flights to Ramechhap / Manthali Airport to minimize airspace congestion in Kathmandu.

When operating via Ramechhap:

  • We arrange a private vehicle transfer departing Kathmandu around 1:30 AM to 2:00 AM for the 4-hour paved scenic drive to Manthali.
  • You board a short 18-minute flight from Manthali into Lukla, landing early in the morning before mountain wind turbulence develops.
  • On the return journey, you fly from Lukla to Manthali and transfer back to Kathmandu by private car.

9. Cold-Weather Electronics & Battery Longevity at -20°C

How sub-zero cold drains batteries and essential techniques to preserve camera power.

Lithium-ion batteries lose voltage rapidly when temperatures drop below freezing. On summit night, temperatures near 6,000 meters frequently drop to -15°C to -20°C, which can deplete a fully charged smartphone or camera battery in under 15 minutes if exposed.

Essential cold-weather battery management rules:

  • Keep Batteries Inside Your Layers: Store your smartphone, spare camera batteries, and headlamp inside an interior zippered pocket close to your chest body heat until the moment of use.
  • Sleep with Your Devices: Place cameras, power banks, and smartphones inside the foot of your down sleeping bag overnight.
  • Bring Spare Lithium Batteries: Standard alkaline batteries fail in extreme cold; lithium AAA/AA batteries perform significantly better in headlamps.
  • Power Bank Capacity: Carry a 20,000 mAh power bank so you can recharge devices independently without paying lodge solar charging fees ($3–$5 per charge).

10. Porter Welfare, Ethical Weight Limits & Tipping Standards

Our commitment to ethical Sherpa porter treatment and fair tipping guidelines.

Igloo Himalaya Treks is a proud signatory of the International Porter Protection Group (IPPG) guidelines. We believe that ethical treatment of our crew is fundamental to our company values:

  • Strict Weight Limits: One porter carries gear for two trekkers, capped at a maximum of 20 kg total (10 kg per trekker). We weigh every duffle bag before leaving Lukla.
  • Proper Clothing & Gear: Every porter is provided with warm clothing, gloves, boots, sunglasses, and comprehensive high-altitude medical insurance.
  • Fair Tipping Guidelines: Tipping is customary in Himalayan trekking and mountaineering culture. We recommend a collective group tip of $150 to $200 USD per trekker for the trekking crew (guides and porters), plus an additional summit bonus of $100 to $150 USD per climber for the dedicated climbing Sherpas who fixed ropes and guided you to the summit.

Frequently Asked Questions for Everest Base Camp with Island Peak (19 Days)

Mountaineering & Technical

How technical is Island Peak climbing compared to trekking? ∨

Island Peak (6,189 m / 20,305 ft) is significantly more demanding than standard trekking. While the first 10 days to EBC and Kala Patthar are on established hiking trails, the climb from Island Peak Base Camp requires scrambling on loose rock, wearing 12-point steel crampons on crevassed glaciers, using a mechanical jumar (ascender) on a 45-degree snow/ice headwall, and walking along an exposed knife-edge summit ridge. Our Day 11 training clinic in Chhukung ensures you master all required rope skills.

Do I need previous mountaineering experience to climb Island Peak? ∨

Previous technical mountaineering experience is not strictly mandatory, but previous high-altitude trekking experience (such as Kilimanjaro, Annapurna Circuit, or Everest Base Camp) is strongly recommended. Climbers must possess outstanding cardiovascular endurance, strong mental grit, and comfort with heights. All required technical systems (jumaring, clipping safety anchors, and figure-8 rappelling) are thoroughly taught during our clinic in Chhukung.

What is the client-to-guide ratio on Island Peak summit day? ∨

We maintain a maximum ratio of 1 certified climbing Sherpa guide for every 2 climbers (1:2 ratio) on summit day. For solo climbers, you have a 1:1 dedicated personal climbing Sherpa. This ensures individualized safety monitoring, continuous rope management, and immediate assistance across technical headwalls and crevasses.

Can I rent all technical climbing gear locally instead of buying? ∨

Yes! High-quality, modern mountaineering equipment is readily available for rent in Chhukung. A complete package including plastic mountaineering boots, crampons, harness, ice axe, jumar, carabiners, helmet, and figure-8 descender costs approximately $100 to $130 USD for the climbing period. Renting in Chhukung saves you from carrying heavy boots and hardware over the first 10 days of the trek.

How does visiting Everest Base Camp first improve my summit chances? ∨

Acclimatization is the single biggest factor in summit success. By spending 10 days ascending progressively to Gorak Shep (5,164 m), Everest Base Camp (5,364 m), and Kala Patthar (5,545 m), your body undergoes deep physiological adaptation—generating new red blood cells and increasing capillary density. When you descend to Chhukung (4,730 m) for the climb, the altitude feels comfortable, leading to our exceptional 88%+ summit success rate.

What happens if someone suffers from altitude sickness (AMS)? ∨

Our expedition leaders carry pulse oximeters and check blood oxygen saturation (SpO2) and resting heart rates twice daily (morning and evening). If mild symptoms of Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) appear, we provide rest, hydration, and Diamox. If symptoms advance to HAPE or HACE, the only effective cure is immediate descent. Our guides are trained in wilderness first aid and carry emergency oxygen; if necessary, we coordinate immediate helicopter rescue.

What kind of travel and medical insurance is required? ∨

You must have a specialized mountaineering insurance policy that specifically covers trekking and climbing up to 6,200 meters (20,341 ft) with emergency medical evacuation by helicopter. Standard policies frequently exclude mountaineering with ropes and anything over 4,000 meters. Trusted providers include Global Rescue, Ripcord Rescue Travel Insurance, and World Nomads (with the Explorer mountaineering rider).

What if bad weather strikes on summit day? ∨

Our 19-day itinerary features a dedicated Weather Contingency Reserve Day (Day 14). If heavy snow, high summit winds, or dense cloud prevent a safe summit push on Day 13, the team remains rested at Base Camp or Chhukung and executes the summit push on Day 14. This buffer day dramatically increases overall expedition success.

Do flights fly from Kathmandu or Ramechhap (Manthali)? ∨

During peak trekking seasons (Spring: March–May; Autumn: October–November), the Civil Aviation Authority of Nepal diverts all Lukla flights to Ramechhap/Manthali Airport to avoid air traffic delays in Kathmandu. We provide private shared vehicle transport from Kathmandu to Manthali (approx. 4 hours) so you can catch your 18-minute flight to Lukla without delay. During off-peak windows, flights operate directly from Kathmandu.

What is the domestic flight luggage allowance to Lukla? ∨

The standard baggage limit for Lukla flights is 15 kg (33 lbs) per passenger, which includes 10 kg of checked luggage (your main duffle bag) and 5 kg of hand luggage (your daypack). Excess weight can occasionally be purchased at the airport ($2–$3 USD per extra kg), but we strongly advise adhering to the 15 kg limit. You can leave non-trekking city clothing securely stored at your hotel in Kathmandu for free.

How many hours do we hike per day on average? ∨

On standard trekking days, you walk between 4 and 6 hours over 8 to 14 kilometers at a steady, conversational pace with frequent tea and photo stops. Summit day on Island Peak is the longest and most demanding day, requiring 10 to 12 hours of combined climbing and descent starting at 1:30 AM.

What are teahouse accommodations and meals like along the trail? ∨

Teahouses are traditional mountain lodges offering clean, unheated twin-share rooms with foam mattresses, pillows, and blankets. The central dining hall is heated each evening by an iron wood/yak-dung stove. Menus offer hearty, freshly prepared meals including traditional Dal Bhat (lentil soup, rice, and vegetable curry with free refills), Sherpa stew, fried rice, noodle soups, porridge, pancakes, and eggs.

Is drinking water safe and available throughout the route? ∨

Untreated tap or stream water in Nepal is not safe to drink. At teahouses, you can purchase boiled drinking water ($2–$4 USD per liter) or fill your bottles from taps and purify it using water purification tablets (Aquatabs / Chlorine Dioxide) or a SteriPEN UV device. We discourage purchasing single-use plastic mineral water bottles to protect the fragile Himalayan environment.

Are hot showers and electricity available to charge devices? ∨

Gas-heated or solar hot showers are available at most teahouses up to Dingboche for an additional fee of $4 to $5 USD per shower. Electricity for charging camera batteries and phones is available in dining rooms via solar or micro-hydro power for $2 to $4 USD per full charge. We strongly recommend bringing a 20,000 mAh power bank so you can charge your devices independently.

How does mobile phone reception and Wi-Fi internet work in the Khumbu? ∨

Local 4G cellular coverage (NTC and Ncell) works surprisingly well up to Dingboche and in parts of Gorak Shep, though connection speeds vary with weather. Teahouses also sell prepaid 'Everest Link' Wi-Fi cards ($20 USD for 10 GB valid for 30 days), which provide reliable wireless internet throughout the entire Khumbu trek. At Island Peak Base Camp, cellular reception is weak to non-existent.
