Annapurna Base Camp Trek Heli Return — 11 Days Overview

Our 11-day Annapurna Base Camp Trek Heli Return combines the approach through Ghorepani with a helicopter flight from the Annapurna Sanctuary. You hike to Poon Hill for sunrise, continue through Chuile and Lower Sinuwa, and reach base camp at 4,130 m. The return flight replaces the several-day descent on foot before you travel to Kathmandu.

After the road journey to Pokhara, you drive to Birethanti and begin walking towards Ulleri. Stone steps lead through terraced hillsides, followed by oak and rhododendron forests on the way to Ghorepani. The Poon Hill hike gives you an early view across the Annapurna and Dhaulagiri ranges before returning for breakfast. You then continue through Tadapani towards Chuile, joining the village trails leading into the Modi Khola valley.

Beyond Chuile, the route crosses towards Chhomrong and Lower Sinuwa before continuing through Bamboo and Dovan. Forest gradually gives way to open mountain slopes above Deurali. You pass Machhapuchhre Base Camp on the approach to Annapurna Base Camp, where Annapurna I, Annapurna South and Hiunchuli rise around the valley. This is your highest overnight stop.

The planned helicopter departs from base camp on Day 9 and lands in Pokhara. You then fly to Kathmandu the following day. This saves the return walking stages, but it does not reduce effort or altitude exposure on the ascent. Flights depend on weather and operating conditions, so allow flexibility rather than relying on a guaranteed departure from the mountains.

You stay in mountain teahouses during the trek and hotels in Kathmandu and Pokhara. The programme covers arrival, the approach, helicopter return, onward flight and departure from Nepal.

1. Trek Difficulty and Fitness

Moderate-to-demanding trekking with stone stair climbs and helicopter flight return from ABC.

The route does not require technical climbing skills in suitable conditions, but it involves consecutive walking days and demanding stair sections. You climb from Birethanti towards Ulleri, then continue through hills and valleys to base camp. The helicopter replaces the return descent, not the uphill effort.

Previous hiking experience is helpful. First-time trekkers should build endurance before departure and become comfortable walking uphill and downhill regularly with a daypack.

Begin preparing around two to three months beforehand, allowing longer if you are starting from a low fitness level. Gradually combine regular walks, hill hikes and strength exercises. Practise in the boots you will wear on the trek.

2. Altitude and Acclimatisation

Steady ascent to 4,130m requiring careful monitoring of altitude symptoms.

This itinerary has no dedicated acclimatisation day. The ascent from Deurali to Annapurna Base Camp brings a substantial gain in sleeping altitude, so your health must guide your progress.

Tell your guide promptly about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not continue to a higher sleeping altitude while experiencing symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite resting at the same elevation, descent is necessary. The planned helicopter return must not delay urgent decisions.

3. Medical Conditions and Travel Insurance

Mandatory travel insurance covering trekking up to 4,130m and emergency helicopter evacuation.

If you have an existing medical condition, seek advice from a healthcare professional familiar with high-altitude travel before booking. Discuss your planned route and any medicines you take.

Arrange insurance covering trekking to at least 4,130 metres, planned helicopter travel, emergency evacuation and medical treatment in Nepal. Check exclusions, excess charges and the insurer’s assistance procedure. Carry your policy details and emergency contact number throughout the trek and the flight stages.

4. Accommodation and Room Facilities

3-star hotels in Kathmandu and Pokhara; comfortable local teahouse lodges on trail.

You stay in three-star hotels in Kathmandu and Pokhara and mountain teahouses along the trail. Rooms are on a twin-share basis unless you request single arrangements, where available.

Expect simple rooms and shared bathroom facilities at Deurali and Annapurna Base Camp, where space is limited. Bring earplugs and a sleeping-bag liner for personal comfort.

5. Food and Dietary Requirements

Three nutritious daily meals (breakfast, lunch, dinner) provided throughout the trekking route.

Three daily meals are included during the walking stages. Menus feature dal bhat, noodles, rice dishes, soups and porridge, offering carbohydrates suited to active days.

Tell us about allergies or dietary requirements before booking so we can discuss what teahouses can reliably provide. You are welcome to bring favourite trail snacks from home.

6. Drinking Water

Boiled and purified water using reusable bottles; single-use plastic bottles strongly discouraged.

Carry reusable bottles and drink boiled or appropriately treated water. Avoid buying single-use bottled water where possible, as disposal is challenging in the mountains.

Boiling is the most reliable way to kill germs. If boiling is unavailable, filtration followed by disinfection offers broader protection than many portable filters alone. Follow the instructions for your water-treatment equipment or tablets.

7. Guide and Porter Support

Government-licensed Sherpa trekking guide and 1 porter for every 2 trekkers.

A trekking guide and one porter for every two trekkers are included. Your porter carries the luggage during the approach. You carry a daypack with daily essentials; confirm how bags return to Pokhara.

8. Showers, Charging and Communication

Teahouse facilities vary with elevation; carry a power bank and plan for remote connectivity.

Hot showers, Wi-Fi and device charging may incur personal charges, with availability varying between lodges. Mobile reception and internet can be unreliable higher in the valley. Carry a power bank and let family know that you may be out of contact during parts of the trek.

9. Permits and Documents

Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP) and TIMS registration fully managed by our team.

The ACAP entry permit is included. TIMS registration is also required under Nepal Tourism Board rules; confirm its fee coverage with us. Carry your passport and insurance details. Your Nepal visa is separate from the package.

10. Flight Delays and Extra Time

Helicopter flight operations depend on mountain weather; buffer days strongly advised.

The helicopter return and Pokhara–Kathmandu flight depend on weather and operating conditions. Allow extra time before international departure rather than relying on fixed schedules.

Ask about the helicopter’s passenger and luggage allowances before booking. If flights are delayed, our team discusses available options and additional accommodation or transport.

Annapurna Base Camp Trek Heli Return FAQs

General & Route

Can I do this trek without previous trekking experience? ∨
Previous hiking experience helps because the approach includes consecutive walking days and steep stone steps. First-time trekkers should prepare with hill walks and discuss fitness and altitude concerns before booking. Technical climbing skills are unnecessary in suitable conditions.
What is the highest altitude on this itinerary? ∨
Annapurna Base Camp at approximately 4,130 metres is the highest planned point and overnight stop. We reach it on Day 8 and stay there before the planned helicopter return. Poon Hill is a viewpoint visited earlier.
How does this differ from the 13-day ABC trek? ∨
Our 13-day Annapurna Base Camp Trek via Poon Hill returns on foot through Bamboo and Jhinu Danda before travelling to Pokhara. This 11-day package ends the walking at base camp, returns by helicopter and includes a flight back to Kathmandu.
Does this itinerary include an acclimatisation day? ∨
No dedicated acclimatisation day is included. The approach reaches Deurali before the final climb to base camp. Report symptoms promptly, follow your guide’s advice and discuss extra time beforehand if you are concerned about altitude.
Does being fit protect me from altitude sickness? ∨
No. Good fitness helps with the walking demands, but it does not prevent altitude illness. Fit and experienced trekkers can also become unwell. Follow your guide’s advice and report symptoms promptly instead of assuming fitness will protect you.
What should I do if I feel unwell at altitude? ∨
Tell your guide promptly about headaches, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Do not ascend to a higher sleeping altitude while experiencing symptoms of altitude illness. If symptoms worsen despite rest at the same elevation, descent is necessary.
Do I need to carry my main trekking bag? ∨
A porter carries your main trekking luggage, with one porter shared between two trekkers. You carry your daypack containing water, weather protection, medicines and other daily essentials. Keep your passport, money and valuables with you.
Do I need to bring a sleeping bag and down jacket? ∨
We provide a sleeping bag, down jacket and duffel bag for use during the trek. Return them afterwards. Bring your own boots, clothing and personal equipment, and ask about the supplied gear if you have particular requirements.
Is insurance covering trekking to 4,000 metres sufficient? ∨
No. Annapurna Base Camp is approximately 4,130 metres, above a 4,000-metre policy limit. Confirm cover for the full route, planned helicopter travel, emergency evacuation and medical treatment, including exclusions and the insurer’s assistance procedure, before booking your trip.
Is the helicopter return guaranteed on Day 9? ∨
No. Day 9 is the planned helicopter return, but weather and operating conditions can delay departure. The pilot determines whether the flight can proceed. Allow extra time and discuss alternative arrangements before committing to onward travel.
Do we sleep at Annapurna Base Camp? ∨
Yes. We stay in a teahouse at Annapurna Base Camp on Day 8, after walking up through Machhapuchhre Base Camp. The next morning includes the planned helicopter return to Pokhara. The overnight stay remains part of the trekking experience.
Can I book as a solo traveller? ∨
Yes. Send us your dates so we can discuss available arrangements and quote for your trek and helicopter return. The final price depends on the confirmed flight arrangement; a shared seat is not automatically guaranteed.
