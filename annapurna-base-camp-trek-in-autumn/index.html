Annapurna Base Camp Trek in Autumn: Weather, Temperatures, Festivals & Route Guide

The Annapurna Base Camp trek in autumn (late September to late November) is widely celebrated as the ultimate Himalayan trekking experience. After the summer monsoon washes away airborne dust and humidity, the Himalayas settle into a glorious pattern of dry continental high-pressure systems. What awaits you is flawless cobalt skies, crisp invigorating mountain air, dry and safe trails, and breathtaking sunrises that turn the 4,000-meter vertical south face of Annapurna I into a blazing wall of gold and crimson. Whether you are walking through golden terraced rice fields during Dashain or stargazing under the Milky Way at 4,130 meters, autumn delivers Nepal at its absolute finest. Here is your definitive guide to autumn weather, temperatures, teahouse logistics, and packing.

Quick Answer: What You Need to Know About Annapurna Base Camp Trek in Autumn

Trailhead & Elevation: The classic trek departs from Nayapul or Ghandruk near Pokhara, climbing through the Modi Khola canyon to Machhapuchhre Base Camp (3,700m) and the Annapurna Sanctuary bowl at 4,130m.

Permits Required: Annapurna Conservation Area Project (ACAP) permit (NPR 3,000 / ~$23 USD) plus Trekkers' Information Management System (TIMS) card (NPR 2,000 / ~$15 USD).

Trail Atmosphere & Physical Demand: Famous for thousands of hand-cut stone steps between Tikhedhungga, Ulleri, and Chhomrong. Rated moderate-to-strenuous; requires solid knee stability and cardiovascular endurance.

Safety Precaution: The narrow gorge between Deurali (3,200m) and MBC (3,700m) passes several known avalanche chutes. Always listen to guide instructions and check daily snow conditions before crossing.

1. The Autumn Phenomenon: Why September to November is the King of Seasons for ABC

Across the global trekking community, autumn is regarded as the gold standard season for trekking in Nepal. For the Annapurna Sanctuary, this seasonal window offers an almost magical combination of optimal weather, minimal trail hazards, and rich cultural energy.

Between late September and late November, the atmospheric circulation over South Asia undergoes a complete reversal. The wet monsoon winds from the Indian Ocean are replaced by cool, dry winds originating from the Tibetan Plateau. This stable high-pressure system produces:

  • Over 85% to 95% Sunny Days: October and November average between 26 and 28 completely clear, sunny days per month.
  • Unrivaled Optical Clarity: The air is washed clean of dust, pollution, and haze, allowing visibility that extends for over 100 miles across the Dhaulagiri and Annapurna massifs.
  • Stable Mountain Slopes: Summer rains have consolidated loose scree, and heavy winter blizzards have not yet arrived, making avalanche corridors between Deurali and MBC exceptionally safe.
  • Vibrant Cultural Atmosphere: Mountain villages are alive with Nepal’s greatest Hindu and Buddhist festivals (Dashain and Tihar), with bamboo swings and flower garlands decorating every hamlet.

2. Month-by-Month Autumn Evolution: Late September vs October vs November

While the entire autumn window is excellent, each of the three months offers a distinct character and appeal:

Month Peak Visibility Day / Night Temp at ABC Trail Crowds Distinctive Seasonal Highlight
Late September 75% – 85% 14°C / -2°C Moderate Lush emerald hillsides, roaring waterfalls, quiet trails
October 95% – 100% 11°C / -5°C Very High The perfect month: flawless blue skies, Dashain/Tihar festivals
November 95% – 100% 7°C / -10°C Moderate to Low Crisp sub-zero clarity, dramatic alpenglow, empty late-month lodges

Late September: Ideal for trekkers who want lush green landscapes and fewer crowds, though occasional afternoon showers can still occur. October: The undisputed champion for weather perfection, though lodges must be booked in advance. November: Colder nights, but crystal skies continue with significantly thinner crowds after the first two weeks.

4. Atmospheric Clarity & Peak Visibility: The Science Behind Post-Monsoon Optical Transparency

Why do Himalayan peaks look sharper in autumn than any other time of year? The secret lies in the atmospheric physics of the retreating summer monsoon.

Between June and August, over 4,000 millimeters of rainfall scrub the atmosphere of all suspended particulate matter—eliminating agricultural smoke, airborne dust, and industrial aerosols from northern India. When cool, dry continental winds arrive from Central Asia in October, the air density drops and humidity evaporates.

The result is near-zero light scattering. Mountain features 30 kilometers away appear as if they are right in front of you. Every ice serac on Annapurna South, every hanging glacier on Hiunchuli, and the jagged rock bands of Annapurna I stand out in razor-sharp relief.

5. Trail Conditions & Avalanche Stability: Dry Granite Steps & Stable Mountain Slopes

Autumn offers the safest, most stable underfoot trail conditions of the entire trekking calendar:

  • Dry Granite Steps: The thousands of stone steps in Ulleri, Chhomrong, and Sinuwa are dry and provide excellent traction, reducing slips and ankle rolls.
  • Minimal Avalanche Hazard: The infamous avalanche couloirs between Deurali and MBC are dry and stable. The old winter snowpacks have long melted away, and new winter snowfalls have not yet accumulated.
  • Zero Leeches: By mid-October, the cool, dry air forces terrestrial leeches into dormant hibernation below the soil, allowing you to hike through bamboo and rhododendron forests without worry.
💡 Optimal Descent Footing

Because trail stones are dry during autumn, descending 1,800 vertical meters from ABC down to Bamboo can be done at a steady, confident pace. Using dual trekking poles ensures your knees absorb minimal shock on the dry granite pavers.

6. The Ideal Autumn 9-Day ABC Itinerary: Pacing, Rest Stops & Alpenglow Mornings

Here is our field-proven 9-day autumn itinerary, designed to maximize golden morning light and prevent altitude distress:

  • Day 01: Drive Pokhara to Siwai (2.5 hrs); Trek through terraced fields to Chhomrong (2,170 m). (4.5 hrs walking)
  • Day 02: Conquer the 2,140 Chhomrong steps; ascend through Sinuwa bamboo forests to Dovan (2,600 m). (5.5 hrs walking)
  • Day 03: Ascend past Himalayan Hotel and Hinku Cave to the sub-alpine gorge at Deurali (3,200 m). (4.5 hrs walking)
  • Day 04: Hike through the Gates of the Sanctuary to MBC (3,700 m); push gently across moraines to Annapurna Base Camp (4,130 m). Watch sunset over Annapurna I. (5.5 hrs walking)
  • Day 05: Witness the legendary 5:30 AM sunrise alpenglow; celebrate and descend to Bamboo (2,310 m). (6.5 hrs walking)
  • Day 06: Trek from Bamboo to Jhinu Danda (1,780 m); spend the afternoon soaking in natural geothermal hot springs. (5.0 hrs walking)
  • Day 07: Walk down to Siwai roadhead; private 4WD transfer back to Pokhara. (2.5 hrs walking + 2.5 hrs drive)
  • Day 08: Full leisure day in Pokhara; boating on Phewa Lake and celebrating with a lakeside dinner.
  • Day 09: Fly or drive back from Pokhara to Kathmandu.

7. Teahouse Dynamics During Peak Autumn: Navigating Full Lodges at Deurali & ABC

The only real downside of autumn is popularity. Because conditions are so magnificent, thousands of global hikers visit the Annapurna Sanctuary in October.

Because ACAP environmental regulations strictly limit construction to only 5 lodges at ABC and 4 at Deurali, beds fill to 100% capacity every afternoon. Independent hikers without reservations frequently find themselves sleeping on dining room benches or sharing dorms with 6 to 8 people.

8. Golden Terraces & Himalayan Wildlife: Harvest Season in Ghandruk & Chhomrong

Autumn transforms the lower valleys of the Modi Khola into a vibrant agricultural tapestry. The terraced mountainsides turn rich golden-yellow as Gurung farmers harvest millet and rice.

Himalayan wildlife is active before the winter freeze. Trekkers in the bamboo and oak forests frequently spot:

  • Himalayan Langur Monkeys: Trooping through the rhododendron canopy near Bamboo and Dovan.
  • Himalayan Monal (Danphe): Nepal’s national bird, displaying nine iridescent colors along the moraines near MBC.
  • Himalayan Tahr & Blue Sheep (Bharal): Grazing on steep crags high above the sanctuary floor.

9. Experiencing Living Festivals: Celebrating Dashain & Tihar in Gurung Villages

One of the greatest privileges of an autumn trek in Nepal is sharing in the nation’s two grandest spiritual celebrations:

  • Dashain (September / October): Celebrating the victory of the goddess Durga over evil. Across villages like Ghandruk, giant 20-foot bamboo swings (Ping) are built. Locals dress in vibrant traditional attire, and teahouse families invite trekkers to participate in blessings and feasts.
  • Tihar / Deepawali (October / November): The five-day Festival of Lights. Teahouse doors are framed with marigold garlands, rangoli colored powder patterns, and butter lamps. In the evenings, youth groups visit teahouses to sing joyful Deusi-Bhailo carols, accompanied by drums and dancing.

11. Autumn 4-Layer Clothing Checklist: Handling Warm Daytime Trails and Freezing Nights

The secret to comfortable autumn trekking is a versatile, moisture-wicking four-layer system:

  1. Base Layer: 2 lightweight synthetic/Merino short-sleeve tees for warm daytime climbs; 1 mid-weight Merino long-sleeve thermal top and bottom for sleeping.
  2. Mid Layer: 1 breathable grid-fleece pullover or technical active insulation jacket (e.g., Polartec or Coreloft).
  3. Insulation Layer: 1 high-loft hooded down jacket (700 to 800 fill power) rated for -10°C evenings at ABC.
  4. Outer Shell: 1 windproof and waterproof Gore-Tex jacket and pants to block cold afternoon canyon drafts.
  5. Sleeping Bag: A 3-season down sleeping bag rated down to -10°C (14°F). While teahouses provide heavy quilts, lodge rooms have zero heating.

13. High-Altitude Safety: Hydration in Dry Air, Sun Protection & Diamox Protocols

While autumn weather is benign, high-altitude physiology must never be ignored:

  • Dry Air Dehydration: Autumn high-pressure air has very low relative humidity. You lose massive amounts of moisture simply by breathing. Drink at least 4 liters of treated water daily.
  • Intense UV Radiation: With clear skies and high elevation, UV exposure is 35% stronger than at sea level. Apply SPF 50+ sunscreen every 3 hours and wear Category 3 or 4 UV sunglasses.
  • Gradual Pacing: Keep your walking pace slow and measured ("Bistari, bistari" in Nepali). Do not race up the stone staircases; match your stepping cadence to your breath.
