Manaslu Circuit Trek Guide: Complete 2026–2027 Route, Permits & Larkya La Guide

Circling Mount Manaslu (8,163m)—the eighth-highest mountain on Earth—the Manaslu Circuit Trek is widely celebrated as Nepal's premier teahouse wilderness adventure. Combining dramatic subtropical river gorges, preserved Tibetan Buddhist gompas of the Nubri Valley, and the triumphant crossing of the glaciated Larkya La Pass (5,160m), it offers the pristine, unpaved Himalayan magic that modern commercial trails have lost. Curated by our certified Sherpa leaders at Igloo Himalaya Treks, here is the complete, field-tested guide for 2026–2027.

Quick Answer: Key Facts About the Manaslu Circuit Trek

Official Grade: Strenuous (177 km total distance, 14 days, max elevation 5,160m at Larkya La Pass).

Restricted Area Mandates: Solo trekking without a guide is strictly illegal. The Nepal government legally requires a minimum of two foreign trekkers accompanied by a government-licensed guide hired through a registered agency.

Mandatory Permits (3 Total): Manaslu Restricted Area Permit (MRAP: $100/week in Autumn, $75/week in Spring) + Manaslu Conservation Area Permit (MCAP: NPR 3,000) + Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP: NPR 3,000).

Crux Crossing: Larkya La Pass (5,160m / 16,929ft)—a pre-dawn 8-hour alpine traverse between Dharamsala and Bimthang across lateral moraines and snow fields.

Best Time to Trek: Autumn (October to November) for crystal visibility and stable skies; Spring (March to May) for blooming rhododendrons and snow-framed peaks.

1. The Spirit of Manaslu: Why It Is the Ultimate Teahouse Circuit in Nepal

Rising majestically in west-central Nepal, Mount Manaslu (8,163m / 26,781ft) is known locally as Kutang—derived from the Sanskrit word Manasa, meaning "Mountain of the Spirit."

While commercial trekking highways like the Annapurna Circuit have seen dirt roads encroach on classic footpaths, the Manaslu Circuit has preserved its authentic, wild soul:

  • Zero Roads on the High Trail: Once you leave Machha Khola, the entire upper valley through Jagat, Namrung, Samagaon, and over Larkya La to Bimthang is 100% road-free footpath, traversed only by hikers, local villagers, and yak caravans.
  • Controlled Low Crowds: Because the Nepal Department of Immigration classifies Manaslu as a restricted zone requiring special permits, only 9,000 to 11,000 trekkers visit each year—compared to over 40,000 on the Annapurna trail and 35,000 at Everest Base Camp.
  • Untouched Tibetan Cultural Sanctuary: The upper valley (Nubri) was closed to foreign travelers until 1992. The local inhabitants, of direct Tibetan lineage, speak the Nubri dialect, wear traditional woolen chubas, and preserve centuries-old Buddhist monastic traditions virtually untouched by the modern world.

3. Mandatory Permits & Strict Restricted Area Regulations in 2026

Because the northern Manaslu region borders the sensitive Tibet Autonomous Region of China, the Nepal Department of Immigration enforces strict legal regulations:

The Three Statutory Regulations

  1. Minimum Group Size: You must have a minimum of two foreign passport holders. Individual solo permits cannot be legally issued by immigration.
  2. Mandatory Government-Licensed Guide: You must be accompanied throughout the entire trek by a certified guide licensed by the Nepal Academy of Tourism and Hotel Management (NATHM).
  3. Registered Agency Processing: Permits cannot be purchased independently by travelers. They must be submitted and processed by a government-registered trekking agency (such as Igloo Himalaya Treks).

Itemized Permit Fees (2026–2027)

  • Manaslu Restricted Area Permit (MRAP):
    • Autumn (Sep – Nov): $100 USD per person for the first 7 days, plus $15 USD/day for additional days.
    • Spring, Winter & Summer (Dec – Aug): $75 USD per person for the first 7 days, plus $10 USD/day for additional days.
  • Manaslu Conservation Area Permit (MCAP): NPR 3,000 (~$23 USD) per person.
  • Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP): NPR 3,000 (~$23 USD) per person (required because the trek exits through Dharapani in the Annapurna district).

4. Larkya La Pass Crossing (5,160m): Tactical Crux & Pass Mechanics

Crossing the Larkya La Pass (5,160m / 16,929ft) on Day 10 is the physical, mental, and emotional climax of the Manaslu Circuit:

  • Pre-Dawn 3:30 AM Departure: The day begins in the freezing dark at Dharamsala (4,460m). You eat a warm porridge breakfast by headlamp and step into the biting sub-zero alpine air (-10°C to -18°C). Early departure is mandatory to crest the pass before afternoon winds and whiteouts arrive.
  • The Ascent Mechanics: A steady 3.5 to 4.5 hour climb (+700m vertical gain) over frozen glacial moraines, past blue alpine tarns, and across rolling snow ridges. Under ~52% oxygen, the key is the rhythmic Sherpa "rest step"—locking the rear knee momentarily on each stride to rest the quadriceps.
  • The Summit Panorama: The pass crest is marked by massive cairns draped in thousands of colorful Buddhist prayer flags. Looking west and north reveals an awe-inspiring 360-degree amphitheater: Himlung Himal (7,126m), Cheo Himal (6,820m), Gyaji Kang, Kang Guru (6,981m), and the icy massif of Annapurna II (7,937m).
  • The Knee-Jarring Descent (-1,440m): Descending from the pass down to Bimthang (3,720m) takes 4 to 5 hours across steep loose scree, snow banks, and lateral moraines. Dual trekking poles and lightweight microspikes are indispensable to prevent slipping on hard-packed snow.

5. Acclimatization Strategy: Samagaon, Birendra Lake & Manaslu Base Camp

Acclimatization on the Manaslu Circuit is naturally assisted by the long, gradual walk up the Budhi Gandaki river canyon over 6 days. However, Day 7 in Samagaon (3,530m) is the non-negotiable medical pivot point of the trek:

Hike A: Birendra Lake (3,691m)

A gentle 3-hour round-trip walk to an awe-inspiring turquoise glacial lake sitting at the snout of the Manaslu glacier. Huge chunks of ice calve into the lake beneath the soaring north face of Manaslu. Ideal for relaxing active acclimatization.

Hike B: Pungyen Gompa (4,050m)

A 5- to 6-hour round-trip hike ascending a hidden alpine valley to a 400-year-old cliffside monastery. Delivers the absolute best direct panoramic views of Manaslu's east face and provides a perfect "climb high, sleep low" altitude stimulus.

Hike C: Manaslu Base Camp (4,800m)

For exceptionally fit trekkers, this rigorous 7- to 8-hour round-trip climb ascends directly to the crampon point and mountaineers' tent city at 4,800 meters. Offers breathtaking close-up views of the Manaslu icefall.

6. Nubri & Tsum Cultural Heritage: Ancient Monasteries & Mani Walls

Trekking through the Nubri Valley is like stepping back into medieval Tibet:

  • The Longest Mani Walls in the Himalayas: As you enter Lho and Samagaon, you will pass monumental stone Mani walls stretching over 200 meters, meticulously carved with the sacred mantra "Om Mani Padme Hum" by generations of Buddhist monks.
  • Ribung Gompa (Lho Village): Perched on a forested ridge directly framing the twin peaks of Mount Manaslu, Ribung Gompa houses over 100 young novice monks studying ancient Nyingma Buddhist texts.
  • Sacred Trail Etiquette: Always circumambulate all chortens, stupas, and Mani walls clockwise (keeping the monument on your right shoulder). Never step over prayer flags, remove shoes before entering temple prayer halls, and ask permission before photographing resident lamas.

7. Tsum Valley Extension: The Hidden Valley of Happiness (Beyul Kyimolung)

For trekkers with extra time, adding the Tsum Valley extension (adding 5 to 7 days, creating an 18- to 21-day expedition) is an extraordinary experience:

  • The Sacred Beyul: Tsum was consecrated by the 8th-century Buddhist master Padmasambhava as a Beyul—a sacred hidden sanctuary where Buddhism and peace would remain preserved during times of global strife.
  • A Culture of Pure Non-Violence (Ahimsa): Since 1920, the local community has enforced a legal decree prohibiting all animal slaughter, hunting, and trapping. Meat is never produced in the valley.
  • Ancient Monasteries & Milarepa's Cave: Highlights include Mu Gompa (3,700m), the remote nunnery of Rachen Gompa, and the meditation cave of the 11th-century Tibetan poet-saint Milarepa at Piren Phu.

8. Trail Terrain & Geological Transitions: Subtropical Canyons to Glacial Moraines

Few treks on Earth traverse such a magnificent sequence of bioclimatic life zones:

Lower River Gorges (900m – 2,000m)

Warm, humid subtropical terrain hugging the roaring Budhi Gandaki river. Narrow rocky paths carved into sheer vertical cliffs, lush bamboo groves, cascading waterfalls, and spectacular steel cantilever suspension bridges bolted directly into sheer granite rockfaces.

Alpine Basin & Moraines (3,000m – 5,160m)

Dense fir and birch forests transition into broad alpine pastures where yaks graze. Beyond Samdo, vegetation gives way to barren glacial moraines, jagged scree slopes, lateral moraines, and the high-altitude permafrost of Larkya La Pass.

9. Teahouse Realities & Accommodation Standards Along the Circuit

Over the past five years, accommodation along the Manaslu Circuit has evolved from rudimentary homestays into comfortable, well-managed teahouse lodges:

  • Lower Valley Lodges (Machha Khola to Namrung): Stone-built lodges with clean twin rooms, western-style flush toilets, hot solar/gas showers, and mobile charging in dining halls.
  • High Valley Outposts (Samagaon & Samdo): Comfortable, atmospheric timber lodges with warm communal dining halls heated by central wood or dried yak-dung stoves. Bedrooms are unheated, requiring a four-season -15°C down sleeping bag.
  • Dharamsala / Larkya Phedi (4,460m): The highest and most rustic staging post. Accommodations consist of basic stone rooms and prefabricated dormitories. Beds are limited during peak October and April months, making advance booking through an authorized agency essential.

10. Food, Nutrition & Safe Hydration on Remote Trails

Fueling your body with wholesome mountain nutrition is critical for sustained endurance:

  • Dal Bhat Power: The legendary staple of lentils, steamed rice, vegetable curry, and spicy pickles remains the best meal on the trail. It is freshly cooked, easily digestible, and comes with free unlimited refills.
  • Local Tibetan Specialties: Enjoy Tibetan flatbread with yak cheese, buckwheat pancakes, tsampa porridge (roasted barley flour), vegetable momos, and hot noodle soups (Thukpa and Sherpa stew).
  • Meat Advisory: Beyond Namrung, religious Buddhist traditions prohibit animal slaughter. Meat carried up from lower valleys is unrefrigerated. We strongly advise adhering to a 100% vegetarian diet above Namrung to prevent digestive illnesses.
  • Hydration: Drink 4 to 5 liters of fluid daily. Use chlorine dioxide purification tablets or a SteriPEN UV device rather than buying single-use plastic bottles.

11. Seasonal Weather Windows: Autumn vs. Spring vs. Winter vs. Monsoon

Season Months Weather & Trail Conditions Larkya La Pass Accessibility
Autumn (Peak) Oct – Nov Crystal-clear blue skies, minimal precipitation, crisp mountain views, stable high pressure. Open & Prime: Dry trails, manageable snow on saddle.
Spring (Prime) Mar – May Blooming rhododendrons in lower valleys; warming days; snow melting; active mountaineering. Open: Snowpack on pass; microspikes recommended.
Winter Dec – Feb Severe freezing cold (-20°C at Dharamsala); heavy snow storms; lodge closures. Frequently Closed: Deep snow blocks Larkya La pass.
Monsoon Jun – Aug Heavy rainfall; active landslides in Budhi Gandaki gorge; swollen rivers; leeches. High Hazard: Landslide road blockages; not recommended.

12. Physical Demands & Fitness Preparation for the Manaslu Circuit

The Manaslu Circuit is officially rated as STRENUOUS. You will hike an average of 15 to 22 kilometers daily (5 to 7 hours) carrying a 5kg to 7kg daypack:

  • Aerobic Base (Zone 2 Cardio): Perform 45 to 60 minutes of incline treadmill walking, cycling, or trail running 3 times per week to build mitochondrial density.
  • Stair Climbing: Train on the gym StairMaster for 45 minutes wearing a 6kg backpack to prepare for thousands of stone switchbacks.
  • Eccentric Leg Conditioning: Bulgarian split squats, weighted box step-downs, and goblet squats condition quadriceps to absorb the brutal 1,440-meter descent from Larkya La down to Bimthang.

13. Comprehensive Packing List & Remote Expedition Gear

Because Manaslu is remote with limited gear shops above Namrung, your packing list must be complete:

  1. Clothing Layers: 2x merino wool thermal base layers, 1x mid-layer fleece, 1x 800-fill down jacket with hood, 1x Gore-Tex waterproof/windproof shell jacket, 2x quick-dry trekking pants.
  2. Footwear: Broken-in waterproof trekking boots with ankle support, 4x pairs of merino wool hiking socks, 1x pair of lightweight camp shoes/crocs.
  3. Pass Crux Equipment: Lightweight walking microspikes / trail crampons (vital for the icy descent from Larkya La), dual adjustable trekking poles, UV Category 4 sunglasses.
  4. Sleeping & Warmth: -15°C to -20°C four-season down sleeping bag (complimentary on Igloo Himalaya Treks tours), sleeping bag silk liner.
  5. Electronics & Power: 20,000 mAh power bank (cold drains batteries fast!), USB cables, headlamp with extra lithium batteries.

14. Medical Safety, Altitude Sickness & Emergency Evacuation

Managing high-altitude health in a remote restricted valley requires professional protocols:

  • Daily Oximeter Monitoring: Certified guides from Igloo Himalaya Treks log blood oxygen saturation (SpO2) and pulse every morning and evening.
  • Lake Louise AMS Scoring: Any headache, nausea, or dizziness is immediately assessed. Ascending with symptoms of Acute Mountain Sickness is strictly prohibited.
  • Helicopter Evacuation Protocol: While mobile signals are dead on high passes, guides carry satellite communicators (Garmin inReach). In the event of acute altitude sickness (HAPE/HACE) or serious trauma, emergency rescue helicopters from Kathmandu can land at Samagaon, Samdo, or Bimthang within 60 to 90 minutes. Ensure your travel insurance covers helicopter rescue up to 6,000 meters.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) About the Manaslu Circuit Trek

General & Planning

Is the Manaslu Circuit better than the Annapurna Circuit? ∨
Manaslu offers authentic road-free wilderness, preserved Tibetan Buddhist culture, and far fewer crowds than the commercialized Annapurna Circuit.
How difficult is crossing the Larkya La Pass (5,160m)? ∨
Larkya La is a non-technical but strenuous 8–9 hour alpine push across moraines in freezing pre-dawn air, requiring solid fitness and microspikes.
Are microspikes required for the Manaslu Circuit? ∨
Yes. Lightweight trail microspikes are essential for traction during the steep, icy descent from Larkya La Pass down to Bimthang.
How do you travel from Kathmandu to the trailhead at Machha Khola? ∨
Travel is via a 7- to 8-hour private 4WD jeep drive from Kathmandu via Arughat, which is much faster and safer than crowded local buses.
Can a solo traveler trek the Manaslu Circuit? ∨
Government regulations mandate a minimum of two foreign trekkers and a licensed guide. Agencies can pair solo hikers with group departures or coordinate permits.
How many permits are required for the Manaslu Circuit in 2026? ∨
Three permits: Manaslu Restricted Area Permit (MRAP), Manaslu Conservation Area Permit (MCAP), and Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP).
What is the accommodation like at Dharamsala (Larkya Phedi)? ∨
Dharamsala (4,460m) is rustic, featuring stone rooms and dormitories. Pre-booking through an authorized agency guide is essential to secure beds.
Can I charge my phone and use Wi-Fi in Manaslu? ∨
Yes, charging and satellite Wi-Fi are available in teahouses up to Samdo for small fees, but electricity is unavailable at high-altitude Dharamsala.
