01 • OVERVIEW

One Day Kathmandu City Tour Overview

Our One Day Kathmandu City Tour brings together five monument zones within the Kathmandu Valley UNESCO World Heritage property. The route connects the former royal squares of Kathmandu and Patan with Swayambhunath, Boudhanath and Pashupatinath. An experienced local guide accompanies the visits, and a private vehicle links the sites during this single-day cultural sightseeing programme.

Begin with Kathmandu Durbar Square, where palace courtyards, temple roofs and carved wood introduce the city’s former royal centre. Continue to Swayambhunath, a hilltop stupa with shrines and views over Kathmandu when the weather allows. Boudhanath provides a different setting: a large white stupa surrounded by a prayer circuit, shops and monasteries. Walk respectfully alongside worshippers, following the clockwise direction and your guide’s advice about visiting religious places.

Pashupatinath stands beside the Bagmati River and remains an active Hindu pilgrimage place. Non-Hindu visitors use permitted surrounding areas, as the main temple is restricted to Hindus. At Patan Durbar Square in Lalitpur, the focus returns to former palace buildings, stone temples and examples of Newar craftsmanship, with your guide explaining the places you see.

The five stops make this a full sightseeing day. Private transport connects them, but you still walk on steps, uneven paving and temple approaches. Traffic, opening arrangements and religious events can affect the time available at each place. Agree your meeting point, start time and finish before departure, and discuss mobility needs so the guide can assess the planned visits.

The tour has no overnight stay. Meals, drinks and personal snacks, including seasonal fruits, are separate expenses; bring suitable food or buy refreshments during the breaks that day.

Walking Demands and Accessibility

Expect walks at several sites, including steps, courtyards and uneven stone paving. The private vehicle connects destinations but does not remove these walking demands. Swayambhunath has a steep stair approach; discuss road access and the remaining walk if your group needs an alternative to those steps.

Trekking experience is unnecessary. Comfortable walking shoes and the stamina for several visits help you manage the day without becoming tired between stops.

Tell us before booking if you use a wheelchair, walking aid or pushchair. Historic thresholds and stairs can limit access. Your guide needs to assess the route and assistance, rather than assume every courtyard or temple is accessible.

Elevation and Conditions

Kathmandu’s elevation is around 1,400 m, or 4,593 ft. The sightseeing route stays within Kathmandu Valley, with a hilltop visit at Swayambhunath rather than a high mountain trekking stage.

The main physical demands are walking, stairs and time outdoors. Sunshine, rain, cooler winter mornings and road traffic can affect your comfort. Carry water and suitable clothing, take breaks as needed and tell your guide if you feel unwell. Share health or mobility concerns before travel.

Medical Conditions and Travel Insurance

Carry your medicines and any information needed for your care. Discuss relevant health concerns with a healthcare professional before travel, considering the walking, stairs and time outdoors involved.

Arrange travel insurance covering sightseeing, medical treatment and emergency transport in Nepal. Check policy exclusions and excess charges, and keep the assistance number accessible. The guide and vehicle arrangement does not establish medical cover or an included allowance for treatment during the tour.

Meeting Point and Transport

Agree your Kathmandu meeting point and finishing arrangements before departure. Share your contact number and accommodation location. Hotel collection, airport connections and any different drop-off require specific confirmation within your quotation.

Accommodation is excluded, so arrange your stay before and after the tour. Keep your day bag with you during visits. Confirm the vehicle arrangements if carrying extra luggage or travelling with a wheelchair, pushchair or child seat.

Food and Dietary Requirements

Meals, beverages and personal refreshments are excluded. Plan for self-purchased lunch, drinks and snacks, including seasonal fruits, during the day’s sightseeing breaks.

Tell your guide about allergies or dietary needs before choosing food. Carry suitable snacks if required. Wash or peel seasonal fruits using safe water, and pay separately for food and drinks.

Drinking Water

Carry safe drinking water during the visits. Bottled water or other beverages are separate expenses, so bring your bottle and budget for purchases.

Use sealed bottled, boiled or appropriately treated water. Untreated tap water may contain germs even when it looks clear. Check the source before refilling bottles and wash your hands before handling food or fruit.

Local Guide and Language

An experienced local guide accompanies the visits and explains the places you see. Follow their advice about access, religious customs and meeting points. Tell us your required language so we can confirm availability.

Photography, Worship and Communication

These are active places of worship as well as visitor sites. Ask before photographing people, ceremonies or temple interiors, and follow posted restrictions. At Pashupatinath, respect funeral privacy and stay clear of grieving families. Keep your guide’s contact details and the meeting point available while exploring.

Admissions and Identification

Entry fees for the five heritage sites are included. Carry identification if requested, as visitor categories may affect arrangements. Included entry does not override religious restrictions or establish access to every museum, courtyard or temple interior.

Traffic, Opening Hours and Flexibility

Five visits require a day. Traffic, festivals and temporary restrictions can change the pace, so avoid arranging an onward connection immediately after the programme.

Your guide may adjust timing or order to conditions. Discuss alternatives before changing the programme, including entry coverage and any extra costs for arrangements.
