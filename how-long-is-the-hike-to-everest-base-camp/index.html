How Long Is the Hike to Everest Base Camp: Exact Days, Distance & Daily Walking Hours

One of the most common questions from aspiring Himalayan trekkers is: exactly how long does it take to hike to Everest Base Camp? While the physical distance from Lukla to Base Camp is only about 65 kilometers (40 miles) one-way, high-altitude physiology dictates that you cannot walk it like a regular trail. Here is the definitive breakdown of total days, daily trail distances, walking hours, acclimatization schedules, and return dynamics.

Ang Tshering Sherpa Founder & Managing Director · 5x Everest Summitter
Published Oct 4, 2026
Updated Oct 5, 2026
26 min read
Key Takeaway

Quick Summary: How Long Does the Everest Base Camp Trek Take?

Standard Duration: The classic guided trek requires 12 to 14 days round-trip from Kathmandu (including 11 to 12 active trekking days and 2 built-in acclimatization rest days).

Total Distance: 130 kilometers (80.7 miles) round-trip starting and ending at Tenzing-Hillary Airport in Lukla (65 km up, 65 km down).

Daily Walking Hours: Expect to hike between 5 and 7 hours per day at a steady, deliberate pace. The longest day (Gorak Shep to Everest Base Camp or Kala Patthar) lasts 7 to 8.5 hours.

Ascent vs. Descent Split: The ascent requires 8 to 9 days to climb safely from Lukla (2,846m) to Base Camp (5,364m), while the descent takes only 3 to 4 days because your body benefits from rapidly increasing oxygen pressure.

1. Total Distance & Time: The Core Numbers at a Glance

At sea level, an average fit person can easily walk 15 to 20 kilometers in four hours on flat asphalt. In the high Himalayas, that equation is completely turned on its head. On the trail to Everest Base Camp (EBC), you will cover a total distance of 130 kilometers (80.7 miles) round-trip, but you will do so over 12 to 14 days.

The primary constraint on speed is not your leg strength—it is barometric pressure and oxygen availability. When ascending above 3,000 meters, your body must manufacture millions of new red blood cells to transport scarce oxygen molecules to working muscles. Attempting to rush the trail to finish in 7 or 8 days dramatically increases your risk of life-threatening Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS), High Altitude Pulmonary Edema (HAPE), or emergency helicopter evacuation.

Sherpa Golden Rule: "Bistari, Bistari" (Slowly, Slowly)

"The fastest trekkers on Day 1 are almost always the ones riding helicopters home on Day 4. The secret to completing the 130km journey is adopting a slow, rhythmic, meditative pace from the very first hour out of Lukla. In the Sherpa language, we say 'Bistari, Bistari'—walk at a pace where you can easily hold a full conversation without gasping for breath." — Ang Tshering Sherpa, 5x Everest Summitter

3. The 9-Day Ascent vs. 3-Day Descent: Why the Discrepancy?

First-time trekkers are frequently surprised to learn that it takes nearly three times as long to walk up as it does to walk down:

  • Going Up: 8 to 9 days to travel 65 kilometers (an average of just 7 to 8 km per day).
  • Coming Down: 3 to 4 days to travel that exact same 65 kilometers (an average of 16 to 19 km per day).

This drastic difference is rooted in high-altitude medicine. When climbing, you must artificially throttle your speed to match your body's oxygen adaptation rate. Once you summit Kala Patthar (5,545m) and start descending, your body experiences the reverse phenomenon: air becomes noticeably thicker, oxygen saturation in your bloodstream leaps from 75% back up toward 95%, your lungs stop burning, and your leg muscles recover rapidly.

For example, on Day 11 you will walk a massive 19 kilometers (11.8 miles) from Namche Bazaar all the way to Lukla in 7 hours—a distance that took two full, exhausting days on the way up!

5. Why Rest Days Are Essential (And Why They Aren't Spent Resting)

On a standard 14-day itinerary, you will notice two designated "Acclimatization Days": Day 3 in Namche Bazaar (3,440m) and Day 6 in Dingboche (4,410m). Novice hikers often assume these are idle rest days spent lounging in bed. In reality, they are active acclimatization hiking days.

Sitting motionless in a cold lodge slows your circulation and does very little to stimulate physiological adaptation. Instead, your guide will lead you on a moderate 3-to-4-hour round-trip hike to a higher vantage point:

  • In Namche Bazaar: You hike 440 vertical meters up to the panoramic terrace of the Hotel Everest View (3,880m) and Khumjung village, admire views of Everest and Ama Dablam, spend an hour letting your body register the thinner air, and descend back to Namche to sleep.
  • In Dingboche: You hike up the steep rocky ridge of Nangkartshang Peak (5,080m), crossing the critical 5,000-meter threshold for the first time, before descending back to Dingboche (4,410m).

These two strategic hikes are the single greatest reason our guided expeditions achieve a 98% summit success rate at Base Camp and Kala Patthar.

6. Walking Speed & Terrain: Why High-Altitude Kilometers Take Longer

Hikers frequently ask why an 11-kilometer day like Phakding to Namche Bazaar takes 6 hours to complete. The answer lies in the combination of trail terrain and gradient:

Lower Trail Terrain (Lukla to Tengboche)

  • Trail Surface: Hand-cut stone staircases, packed dirt, and pine needle switchbacks.
  • Obstacles: Continuous undulating elevation ("Nepali flat"), suspension bridge bottlenecks, and pack animal caravans.
  • Average Speed: 2.5 to 3.5 km/h (1.5 to 2.2 mph).

Upper Glacial Terrain (Lobuche to EBC)

  • Trail Surface: Loose scree, jagged granite boulders, shifting glacial moraine, and packed ice.
  • Obstacles: Slippery footing, thin air requiring rest-stepping, sub-zero wind chills, and frozen stream crossings.
  • Average Speed: 1.5 to 2.0 km/h (0.9 to 1.2 mph).

7. Express Itineraries: Can You Trek EBC in 10 or 11 Days?

Many online travel agencies market "10-Day" or "11-Day" Everest Base Camp packages. While physically possible for elite athletes or pre-acclimatized mountaineers, shortened itineraries carry a dangerous 40% to 50% failure rate among recreational hikers due to altitude sickness.

In a 10-day itinerary, agencies achieve the shorter timeframe by cutting out the acclimatization days in Namche or Dingboche, or combining long high-altitude days. This forces your body to ascend directly into the hypoxic zone without sufficient physiological preparation.

The Smart Express Alternative: Helicopter Return

"If you only have 9 to 10 days of vacation, never cut out your ascent acclimatization days. Instead, choose our EBC Trek with Helicopter Return: follow the standard, safe 8-day ascent to Base Camp and Kala Patthar, and then fly directly out of Gorak Shep back to Kathmandu by helicopter in 45 minutes! You save 4 days of grueling downhill walking while keeping 100% of your safety margin intact."

8. The Classic Overland Routes: Jiri (21 Days) & Paphlu (16 Days)

Before the airstrip at Lukla was constructed by Sir Edmund Hillary in 1964, every legendary mountaineering expedition—including the historic 1953 British first-ascent team—walked into the Khumbu from the Kathmandu valley roadhead at Jiri.

  • The Classic Jiri to EBC Route (20 to 22 Days): Adds 6 to 7 days of strenuous trekking across the lower Solu hills before reaching Lukla. You cross multiple 3,000-meter passes (Deurali, Lamjura La at 3,530m) and descend deep river gorges. It provides extraordinary physical conditioning and nearly bulletproof acclimatization.
  • The Paphlu / Salleri Route (16 to 17 Days): Take a shared jeep from Kathmandu to Salleri or Paphlu (a 9-hour drive) and walk 3 days through quiet Sherpa and Rai farming hamlets to connect with the main trail at Surke, just below Lukla.

9. Why You Must Plan Buffer Days in Kathmandu (Aviation Delays)

When calculating how much total time you need for your Nepal vacation, do not look only at the 12-day trekking window. Tenzing-Hillary Airport in Lukla operates strictly under Visual Flight Rules (VFR). If afternoon cloud cover, mountain fog, or high crosswinds occur, all flight operations halt immediately.

To prevent missing expensive international return flights home, you should always build at least two contingency buffer days into your overall travel itinerary. If your trek ends on Day 12, do not book your flight back to your home country before Day 15 or 16.

11. Physical Training: How to Prepare Your Legs for 6 Hours Daily

Walking 5 to 7 hours per day for 11 consecutive days with a daypack on your back requires sustained muscular endurance and cardiovascular stamina. Begin training 8 to 12 weeks before departure using this targeted schedule:

Cardiovascular & Stair Training (3x / week)

  • Stairmaster / Stadium Bleachers: 45 to 60 minutes of steady climbing with a 5kg to 8kg weighted backpack. This closely mimics the 3,000 vertical meters of stone stairs on trail.
  • Incline Treadmill: Set treadmill to 12%–15% incline at 4.0 km/h for 60 minutes.
  • Zone 2 Aerobic Base: Cycling, rowing, or swimming for 45 minutes to expand mitochondrial density.

Weekend Long Hikes (1x / weekend)

  • Trail Hiking: 4 to 6 hours on natural uneven dirt trails with a 7kg daypack.
  • Boot Break-In: Wear your actual high-ankle mountain boots on every single training hike to prevent trail blisters.
  • Trekking Pole Practice: Master the double-pole rhythm to take 25% of load off your knees.

12. The Two Hardest Days on Trail: Gorak Shep to EBC & Kala Patthar

When budgeting your daily energy, be prepared for Day 8 and Day 9, which represent the physical and mental crux of the expedition:

  • Day 8 (Lobuche to Gorak Shep to EBC and back): You depart Lobuche at 7:00 AM, hike 3 hours over rocky moraine to reach Gorak Shep, take a short lunch, and immediately set out on a 4.5-hour round-trip traverse over shifting glacial rubble to touch the iconic Everest Base Camp marker stone. Total day duration: 7.5 to 8.5 hours.
  • Day 9 (Kala Patthar Sunrise and descent to Pheriche): You wake up at 4:30 AM in sub-zero darkness (-15°C to -20°C) with your headlamp to tackle the punishing 2-hour, 381-meter vertical grind up Kala Patthar (5,545m). After soaking in the sunrise over Everest, you descend to Gorak Shep for breakfast, pack your gear, and hike 4 more hours down to Pheriche. Total day duration: 7 to 8 hours.

13. The Descent: Fast Miles & The Surge of Oxygen

The final three days of the trek (Pheriche to Namche, and Namche to Lukla) feel completely different from the grueling ascent. As you plunge below 3,800 meters back into pine and juniper forests, your body experiences an exhilarating physical resurgence.

Your appetite returns in full force, headaches vanish, and you will find yourself cheerfully chatting with your guides and fellow trekkers while effortlessly clocking 15 to 19 kilometers in a single day. Arriving in Lukla on the afternoon of Day 11 is a joyous milestone marked by hot showers, cold celebratory beers with your Sherpa crew, and tipping your porters for their heroic efforts.

14. Frequently Asked Questions About Trek Duration

Can I do the Everest Base Camp trek in 7 or 8 days?

Not on foot. Ascending from Lukla (2,846m) to Base Camp (5,364m) in fewer than 8 days carries an unacceptably high risk of severe Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS), cerebral edema (HACE), or pulmonary edema (HAPE). However, you can complete the journey in 8 to 9 days if you trek up safely over 8 days and fly back to Kathmandu by helicopter from Gorak Shep.

What is the average walking pace on the trail?

On lower trails (Lukla to Namche), average speed is roughly 2.5 to 3.5 km/h (1.5–2.2 mph). Above Dingboche (4,410m), your speed drops naturally to 1.5 to 2.0 km/h (0.9–1.2 mph) due to 50% thinner oxygen. Guides encourage small, rhythmic steps without stopping frequently.

How many kilometers do you walk each day?

On the ascent, you walk between 8 and 13 kilometers per day (with shorter 4–5km acclimatization walks on rest days). On the descent, daily distances increase to 14 to 19 kilometers per day as descending is much faster and physically easier on the lungs.

What time do you start hiking each morning?

Trekkers typically start walking between 7:30 AM and 8:00 AM after breakfast. Early departures ensure you take advantage of clear blue morning skies and calm winds, arriving at your afternoon teahouse by 2:00 PM to 3:30 PM before temperatures drop and valley clouds roll in.

Do I need to carry all my luggage during those daily hours?

No. When trekking with Igloo Himalaya Treks, sturdy Sherpa porters or pack animals carry your heavy main duffel bag (up to 10–12 kg per person). You only carry a lightweight 25L to 30L daypack containing your water bottle, rain shell, down layer, sun protection, camera, and basic personal snacks.

How many total days should I book off work for this trip?

For a standard 12-day Lukla-to-Lukla trek, we recommend booking a total of 16 to 17 vacation days. This accounts for international travel flights to Nepal (2 days), pre-trek gear checking in Kathmandu (1 day), the 12-day trek, and 2 contingency buffer days in Kathmandu in case Lukla flights are delayed by mountain weather.

Is the trek suitable for older hikers or seniors?

Yes. Many hikers in their 50s, 60s, and even 70s successfully reach Everest Base Camp. Senior hikers often fare better than young hikers because they naturally maintain a slow, steady walking pace without rushing, which gives their bodies superior acclimatization adaptation.

Can I take a rest day if I feel fatigued on trail?

Yes. While scheduled group departures have set itineraries, private guided departures offer complete flexibility. If you or a family member feels unusually tired, you can spend an extra night in Namche Bazaar, Tengboche, or Dingboche and adjust downstream walking schedules accordingly.
